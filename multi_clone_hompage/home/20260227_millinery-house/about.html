The Maison

A Legacy
Crowned in Time

Since 1987, Chapeau Noir has defined the upper echelon of bespoke millinery — guided by a singular vision: that a hat is the most intimate form of couture.

The Founder

Madeleine Noir

"I have always believed that the hat is where a woman's architecture meets her spirit."

Born in Lyon in 1960, Madeleine Noir arrived in Paris at twenty-two with a portfolio of sketches, a letter of introduction to a retired chapelier on Rue Saint-Honoré, and an absolute conviction that the art of millinery deserved a new voice for a new era.

After seven years as a senior milliner at two of Paris's most storied couture houses — names she declines to invoke, preferring the hat to speak for itself — she founded Chapeau Noir in 1987. The name was deliberate: a provocation. Black, she has always said, is not the absence of colour. It is the presence of everything at once.

Today, Madeleine serves as Creative Director across all three maisons, approving every commission that bears the house name. She designs in collaboration with a team of fifteen master milliners but insists on blocking every wedding hat personally.

Recognition

Crowned with Honours

2023

Prix de la Chapellerie d'Excellence

Awarded by the Fédération Française de la Mode for sustained contribution to the art of haute millinery over four decades.

2019

British Hat Award — Grand Prix

Recognition for the Mayfair atelier's role in reinvigorating British occasion dressing following its opening in 2003.

2015

CFDA Honorary Award

The Council of Fashion Designers of America honoured Madeleine Noir for her influence on American luxury fashion and bespoke culture.

2011

Vogue Talent Award

Vogue France recognised Chapeau Noir as the foremost living example of Parisian millinery tradition in a rapidly changing fashion landscape.

2005

Worshipful Company of Feltmakers

Honorary membership conferred in London for exceptional mastery of felt working and contribution to the preservation of traditional millinery techniques.

1998

Chevalier des Arts et des Lettres

The French Ministry of Culture invested Madeleine Noir as Chevalier, recognising millinery as a legitimate fine art and her role in its elevation.
