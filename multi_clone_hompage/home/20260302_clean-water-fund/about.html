About AquaVerde

Our Mission

AquaVerde Foundation exists to ensure that every human being has access to safe, clean water — not as a development aspiration, but as a present-day reality requiring urgent infrastructure and long-term stewardship.

We design and build water purification systems, boreholes, rainwater harvesting infrastructure, and watershed restoration programs in communities where contaminated or absent water supply is causing preventable illness, economic paralysis, and daily suffering.

Our distinguishing commitment is longevity. We stay in every community for at least eighteen months, train local technicians to a recognized qualification standard, and provide five years of monitoring and support after project handover. Our 97% five-year operational rate reflects this commitment.

Founding Story

Born from a Broken Well

In 2009, hydrologist Dr. Ines Carvalho was conducting field research in the Horn of Africa when she encountered a village whose internationally-funded borehole had been non-functional for three years. The NGO that installed it had long since departed. There was no spare parts supply, no trained maintenance person, and no funding mechanism for repairs.

She spent two weeks fixing the well with what she could source locally. She then spent two years designing a better model — one that would never leave a community without the knowledge and resources to maintain what it had been given.

She co-founded AquaVerde with water policy specialist Tariq Osei in 2011. Their first project served a community of 800 people in eastern Ethiopia. That borehole is still operational today.

Our Values

What We Stand For

01

Community Ownership

The community must own its water system — legally and technically. Projects without genuine ownership fail. Systems with genuine ownership last.

02

Ecological Integrity

Clean water infrastructure is only durable if the watershed feeding it is protected. We invest equally in ecological stewardship and engineered systems.

03

Radical Transparency

We publish our operational data, failure rates, and cost per beneficiary annually. We believe accountability is not optional for organizations working with donated funds and vulnerable communities.
