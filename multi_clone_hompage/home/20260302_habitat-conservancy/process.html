Conservation Science

How We Work

Our Method

Evidence-Rooted Restoration

Habitat restoration is not a simple act of planting trees and walking away. It demands a deep understanding of ecological processes, hydrological systems, soil biology, and species dynamics — combined with the patience to let nature respond on its own timeline. Our method is built on 38 years of learning.

Three Pillars

Our Restoration Framework

I
Assess & Diagnose
Every reserve begins with a 3-year baseline survey: soil profiles, hydrology mapping, botanical quadrats, bird and invertebrate transects. We do not act without understanding.
II
Restore & Rewild
Interventions are targeted and minimal: blocking drainage ditches, removing invasive species, reinstating grazing regimes with native breeds. We unlock natural processes rather than direct them.
III
Monitor & Adapt
Annual monitoring using standardised protocols tracks biodiversity recovery, carbon sequestration, and hydrological function. Management is continuously adjusted in response to ecological data.
Restoration Process

From Acquisition to Recovered Habitat

01
Year 1–3
Baseline Survey & Land Assessment
Ecological teams survey all habitat types, map drainage systems, document species assemblages, and identify the historical land use patterns that degraded the site. A restoration target map is produced defining the desired future state of each parcel.
02
Year 2–5
Hydrological Restoration
Drainage channels are blocked, bunds constructed, and watercourses re-meandered where straightened. On peatland sites, surface rewetting begins immediately, raising the water table to prevent further carbon loss.
03
Year 3–10
Vegetation Restoration
Native provenance seed mixes, natural regeneration from seed banks, and targeted planting of keystone species re-establish the plant communities that drive ecosystem function. Grazing is reintroduced with Hereford cattle and Exmoor ponies on appropriate sites.
04
Year 5–20
Faunal Recovery
As vegetation matures, invertebrate communities establish, which in turn supports recovering bird and mammal populations. Some species recolonise naturally; others — including water vole, white-tailed eagle, and beaver — are reintroduced with statutory permissions.
05
Year 20+
Long-Term Stewardship
Established habitats require ongoing but light-touch management: scrub control, invasive species surveillance, and hydrological maintenance. Annual monitoring continues indefinitely, feeding into our open-access ecological data archive.
Research Partnerships

Science at Our Core

We maintain active research partnerships with eight universities, contributing our long-term monitoring datasets to national and international biodiversity assessments. All data is published under open access.

Current research programmes include peatland carbon flux measurement, native woodland bird community response to rewilding, and the role of large herbivores in structuring temperate woodland.
