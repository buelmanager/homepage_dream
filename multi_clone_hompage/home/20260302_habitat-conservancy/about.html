About Sylvan Conservancy

Who We Are

Guardians of Ancient Woodland

Founded in 1988 by a coalition of ecologists and landowners, Sylvan Conservancy has grown from a single 12-hectare oak woodland into one of Britain's most respected habitat restoration organisations. Today we steward over 290,000 hectares of ancient woodland, upland bog, lowland fen, and native grassland.

Our work is rooted in evidence-based conservation — combining long-term ecological monitoring, landscape-scale rewilding, and deep community engagement. We do not simply preserve; we actively restore the biological complexity that centuries of mismanagement have stripped away.

Our Principles

What Guides Us

01
Evidence-Led Action
Every management decision is informed by 30+ years of in-house ecological survey data and peer-reviewed research partnerships with leading universities.
02
Landscape Scale
Isolated nature reserves cannot sustain biodiversity alone. We work at catchment and landscape scale, creating ecological corridors between fragmented habitats.
03
Community Roots
Conservation is most durable when it is embedded in local communities. We partner with farmers, schools, local authorities, and businesses in every landscape we work in.
04
Long Horizons
Woodland restoration is measured in decades, not quarters. Our endowment model ensures continuity of land management regardless of short-term funding cycles.
05
Radical Transparency
We publish all monitoring data, land management plans, and financial accounts in full. Our donors and the public deserve to see exactly what their support achieves.
06
Species First
Every decision is evaluated first by its impact on native species and ecological processes — not aesthetics, visitor numbers, or commercial viability.
History

38 Years of Restoration

1988
Foundation
Sylvan Conservancy established by Dr Margaret Holt and seven co-founders, acquiring the first 12-hectare Ashgrove Oak Wood in Herefordshire.
1995
First Rewilding Project
Launched our first large-scale passive rewilding programme on 800 hectares of former sheep pasture in the Welsh Borders, reintroducing native scrub and wetland habitats.
2000
100,000 Hectares
Reached our first major milestone — 100,000 hectares under stewardship — and launched the National Ancient Woodland Inventory in partnership with Natural England.
2012
Wetland Rewetting Programme
Began the largest fen and blanket bog rewetting initiative in England, restoring 40,000 hectares of drained peatland to active carbon capture and species-rich habitat.
2024
290,000 Hectares
Our current estate reaches 290,000 hectares across 14 distinct reserve complexes in England and Wales, supporting over 6,200 recorded native species.
