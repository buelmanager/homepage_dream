The Founder

Éléonore Voss — A Life in Thread

"I began embroidering at seven years old, guided by my grandmother's hands. Forty years later, I understand that she was not teaching me a skill. She was teaching me how to be still."

Éléonore Voss was born in Lyon, France, into a family with deep roots in the textile arts. Her great-grandmother embroidered for a Parisian couture house; her grandmother kept the tradition alive in the domestic sphere. By adolescence, Éléonore had mastered the foundational European stitches and was studying under a master embroiderer in Angers.

After formal training at the École Supérieure des Arts et Techniques de la Mode and a decade of work with luxury fashion houses in Paris and Milan, Éléonore felt a growing need to create work that existed outside the tyranny of seasonal trend. In 1995, she established BRODERIE in a small atelier in southwest London, determined to make pieces built for permanence.

Today, three decades later, BRODERIE has completed over twelve hundred commissions for private clients, couture houses, royal residences, and cultural institutions across eighteen countries. Éléonore remains in the studio five days a week, needle in hand, teaching and making in equal measure.

The Artisans

Four Masters, One Vision

Each member of the BRODERIE studio brings a distinct classical training, ensuring that every commission can draw from the full breadth of the world's embroidery traditions.

Éléonore Voss
Founder & Lead Artisan

Thirty years of practice in French silk shading, goldwork, and couture embroidery. Trained in Lyon and Paris. Taught at the Royal School of Needlework.

Speciality: Silk Shading, Goldwork, Couture
Hiromi Tanaka
Master — Japanese Traditions

Trained in Kyoto under a National Living Treasure in Sashiko and Kogin-zashi. Hiromi joined BRODERIE in 2004, bringing an architectural precision to geometric needlework.

Speciality: Sashiko, Kogin-zashi, Geometric Design
Orsolya Fehér
Master — Eastern European Traditions

Born in Kalocsa, Hungary, the embroidery capital of the country. Orsolya brings explosive colour theory and folk narrative motifs honed across twenty-two years of practice.

Speciality: Kalocsa Folk, Botanical Motif, Colour Theory
Sigrid Bakke
Master — Nordic Traditions

From Bergen, Norway, Sigrid studied Hardanger whitework under her great-aunt and later trained in needle lace in Brussels. Her whitework pieces are breathtaking in their architectural restraint.

Speciality: Hardanger, Whitework, Needle Lace
Our History

Three Decades, One Thread

1995
The Studio Opens

Éléonore Voss establishes BRODERIE in a small atelier in Kensington, London. The first collection of twelve commissioned wall pieces sells entirely within six weeks.

Royal Warrant Inquiry

BRODERIE receives its first commission from a British royal residence — a set of embroidered seat covers for a private drawing room. The relationship endures to this day.

2001
2004
First International Artisan

Hiromi Tanaka joins the studio from Kyoto, marking the beginning of BRODERIE's commitment to multi-tradition mastery. The studio's geographic commissions expand to Japan and Southeast Asia.

Couture Collaboration

BRODERIE begins a lasting collaboration with Maison Aurel in Paris, creating hand-embroidered bodices and cuffs for three consecutive haute couture seasons. The partnership earns international press coverage.

2010
2015
Victoria & Albert Exhibition

Three BRODERIE pieces are selected for the V&A Museum's "Slow Stitch" exhibition, introducing the studio's work to an audience of over 280,000 visitors over fourteen weeks.

Studio Expansion

BRODERIE relocates to a larger converted Victorian workshop in Marylebone. Orsolya Fehér and Sigrid Bakke join, completing the current team of four master artisans.

2019
2025
Thirty Years

BRODERIE celebrates its thirtieth year with a retrospective exhibition and the launch of a limited Heritage Collection — thirty pieces, each embodying a different technique mastered over three decades.

What We Stand For

The Principles of Slow Making

01

Patience Over Speed

We will not rush a piece. If the design requires eight months, we take eight months. We believe the world needs more things made slowly, and that client trust is the only capital worth building.

02

Material Honesty

Only 100% natural materials enter our studio: silk thread, linen ground, pure beeswax for thread conditioning, and acid-free backing boards. We do not use synthetic fibres, adhesives, or digital transfer methods.

03

Teaching as Legacy

Each senior artisan teaches an apprentice. Knowledge held only in one generation dies with it. We mentor two apprentices per year through a twelve-month residency programme.

04

Fair Livelihood

The studio pays its artisans a living wage that respects the extraordinary skill they bring. We do not compete on price; we compete on quality, integrity, and the depth of our attention.

05

Client as Collaborator

We do not take briefs; we have conversations. Every client is a collaborator who shapes the narrative of the work. We share design drafts, material samples, and progress photographs throughout the process.

06

Work Built to Last

Every piece is made to be used, loved, and passed on. We use only archival-grade materials rated for three hundred years. We include a full care guide and will accept any piece back for restoration.
