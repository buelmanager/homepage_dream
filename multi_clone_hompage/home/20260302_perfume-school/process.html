Notre Méthode

The Five Stages
of the Nose

Our curriculum moves from raw olfactory perception through structured vocabulary, accord knowledge, composition and professional practice — building each stage on the irreducible foundation of honest smelling.

"The perfumer's greatest enemy is not a lack of talent but a lack of honesty. Before you can compose, you must learn to smell what is actually there — not what you expect, not what you hope, but what is." — Isabelle Vernet, Founder
01
Weeks 1–4

Olfactory Vocabulary

Students work through 200 core raw materials, building the vocabulary and neurological infrastructure needed to identify, name, retain and recall individual aromatic signatures. This stage is entirely perceptual — no chemistry, no history, only the nose and what it discovers.

02
Weeks 5–8

The Structure of Fragrance

Top notes, heart notes, base notes — the temporal architecture of a complete fragrance. Students learn how volatility, substantivity and fixative power determine the arc of a perfume from first impression to dry-down. Classic structures are examined through iconic twentieth-century examples.

03
Weeks 9–14

Accord Study

Classic accord families — chypre, fougère, oriental, floral aldehyde, aquatic, woody — are dissected and reconstructed from component parts. Students learn to read formula logic, understand the function of each material within an accord and develop a working typology of fragrance families.

04
Weeks 15–22

Original Composition

Students develop three original fragrances in response to written creative briefs. Each fragrance goes through multiple iterations — modified in response to rigorous evaluation sessions with the maître parfumeur and peer cohort. Formula notation is completed in IFRA-compliant format throughout.

05
Weeks 23–26

Professional Practice

The business and ethics of independent perfumery: IFRA compliance, safety regulation, fragrance brief interpretation, client communication, formula protection, pricing and the commercial realities of bringing an original fragrance to market. Graduates leave with both the creative and the professional tools required to work.
