About
L'École du Nez

A school founded on a single question: what does it mean to truly educate the nose? Twenty-eight years in Grasse — the world's fragrance capital — have shaped our answer.

An Education Born of Conviction

L'École du Nez was not born from commerce. It was born from a question that Isabelle Vernet could not stop asking during her years as an industrial parfumeur: why is the knowledge of fragrance so jealously guarded within corporate laboratories, when it belongs — in its deepest historical sense — to the culture of France?

The school she founded in 1998 answered that question by opening its doors to all sincere students, regardless of professional background. A chemist sits beside a novelist; a couture buyer learns alongside a botanical farmer. What they share is curiosity about smell — and the patience to educate it.

Today, L'École du Nez is one of only three independently accredited perfumery schools in France and holds one of the most extensive raw material teaching libraries available to students anywhere in the world.

Ce que nous croyons

Our Beliefs

The Nose is Educable

Contrary to popular belief, the capacity to smell with precision and discrimination is not a fixed gift but a trained skill. We have watched students with no prior olfactory training develop the nose of a professional parfumeur through disciplined study.

Tradition Informs Invention

Original fragrance composition requires a thorough knowledge of what has already been composed. We teach the history and structure of great twentieth-century perfumery before inviting students to add their own voice to the tradition.

Nature and the Synthetic

We refuse the false binary between natural and synthetic. Both have their place in the complete perfumer's palette. Our curriculum embraces the full material world of fragrance without ideology or commercial interest.

Teaching Through Questions

Our method is Socratic. We do not tell students what a fragrance is — we ask them what they perceive, and build understanding from their honest answers. The nose leads; the teacher follows.
