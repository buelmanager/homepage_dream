The Beginning

A Room, a Form, and a Conviction

In the spring of 1994, Isabelle Morel returned to Paris after six years at Givenchy Haute Couture and rented a single north-facing room on Rue de Passy. She had a dress form, three bolts of Duchess satin, and a conviction that the most important dress a woman would ever wear deserved to be made with the same gravity as a couture collection piece.

Her first commission was a wedding gown for the daughter of a former Givenchy client. The appointment lasted four hours. By its end, Isabelle had produced twenty-three sketches and the client had wept twice. The gown took seven months and required sixteen fittings. It was perfection. Word spread immediately.

Within two years, the single room had expanded into the entire floor. By 1998, Isabelle had hired three additional petites mains — specialist hand-sewers — each trained at a major Paris house. Today, ALBA occupies a full hôtel particulier in the 16th arrondissement, with seventeen artisans, a private fitting suite, and a climate-controlled archive of every gown ever made.

"I could have made beautiful gowns faster. But fast and beautiful are not the same thing as true."

The People

The Hands Behind the Gown

Every gown passes through the hands of our principal artisans — each a practitioner of a single, highly specialised discipline.

Graduate of the École de la Chambre Syndicale de la Couture Parisienne. Six years at Givenchy Haute Couture under Claude Bertrand. Oversees all creative direction and the petites mains at ALBA.

Trained at the Lesage embroidery school. Twenty-two years at Elie Saab Paris. Responsible for all structural construction, embroidery placement, and hand-needle floral work.

Education & Training

Paris-Trained, Globally Sought

Every principal designer and senior petites mains at ALBA holds credentials from one of the major Paris couture schools or houses. This is not incidental — it is foundational to how we approach a garment and a bride.

Paris couture training instils an understanding of the body that no pattern book can teach. We learn to drape on living forms, to understand how silk behaves differently in movement than in repose, and to see structure not as constraint but as the invisible architecture of beauty.

Milestones

Thirty Years in the Making

1994

The Atelier Opens

Isabelle Morel establishes ALBA in a single rented room on Rue de Passy, 16th arrondissement. First commission: a cathedral-train duchess satin gown for a June wedding in the Loire Valley.

1998

The First Team

Three additional petites mains join from Valentino and Christian Lacroix. The atelier expands to occupy the full floor of the Rue de Passy building. A private fitting room is installed with custom three-way mirrors.

2005

Hôtel Particulier

ALBA acquires and renovates its current home — a 19th-century hôtel particulier with north-facing studios, a bridal salon, and a climate-controlled gown archive. Seventeen artisans are now at work.

2013

Camille Joins

Camille Morel returns from three seasons at Chanel Couture to lead design. Her first collection introduces the Modern Romance and Minimalist gown lines, expanding the atelier's aesthetic range.

2024

Five Hundred Brides

ALBA marks its 500th bridal commission. A private evening at the atelier brings together past brides, some with their own daughters now beginning their bridal journeys. The archive is opened to all.
