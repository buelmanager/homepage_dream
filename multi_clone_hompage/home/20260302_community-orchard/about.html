About Commons Orchard

Who We Are

Commons Orchard is a community-governed permaculture collective dedicated to cultivating shared food forests, orchards, and growing spaces that produce food and belonging in equal measure.

We are not a charity distributing services, nor a business selling experiences. We are a commons — governed by our members, maintained by collective labor, and open to all who wish to grow and share.

Founded in 2014 by a group of urban dwellers who wanted to turn disused land into productive community space, we now steward eighteen growing sites across the city, tended by over three thousand members from all walks of life.

Founding Story

A Neglected Allotment, a Big Idea

In the spring of 2014, Nadia Ferreira and a dozen neighbors knocked on the door of a housing association to ask permission to cultivate a derelict allotment behind their block. They expected bureaucratic delay. Instead, they got a ten-year lease and a key.

The first planting day drew forty people. By the end of that summer, they had harvested runner beans, courgettes, and enough apples to fill a community kitchen pantry for six weeks. More than the food, they had built something rarer: a shared purpose.

That allotment is still growing. And the network it seeded — now eighteen sites, three thousand members, twelve tonnes of food per year — carries the same spirit of that first morning in the mud.

Core Values

What We Stand For

01

Land as Commons

No one owns the orchard. We steward it collectively for current and future community members, always resisting the impulse to privatize what belongs to all.

02

Ecological Reciprocity

We take only what we need and always give back more than we take — in compost, in planting, in restoration. The land's health comes before our harvest.

03

Radical Openness

Membership is free. We do not gatekeep by skill, by schedule, or by social background. The orchard is for everyone who shows up — beginners most of all.
