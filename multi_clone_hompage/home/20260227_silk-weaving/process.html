The Craft

From Cocoon to Cloth

Five stages of patient transformation — each demanding its own form of mastery — through which raw silk filament becomes a textile of enduring distinction.

Our Methodology

Every Stage, Intentional

At FILAMENT, we do not shortcut the process. The time we invest in each stage — preparation, dyeing, warping, weaving, finishing — is precisely what distinguishes our cloth from that of lesser origin.

01

Reeling

The First Unraveling

Silk reeling is the process by which continuous filament is drawn from the silkworm cocoon and combined with filaments from three to eight additional cocoons to form the raw silk yarn. This process requires water at a precise temperature — typically between 65° and 90°C — to soften the sericin protein that binds the cocoon together.

FILAMENT sources its cocoons exclusively from Bombyx mori silkworms raised on white mulberry leaf in Italy and Japan. Our reeling is carried out by specialist suppliers with whom we maintain long-term relationships, ensuring consistency of filament fineness and uniformity of twist.

Filament fineness: 10–13 denier per thread
Cocoons per yarn: 4–8, depending on weave specification
Sourcing: Bombyx mori, Italy and Japan
02

Dyeing

The Language of Color

FILAMENT maintains an in-house botanical dye studio under the direction of Nadia Ferrante, a trained chemist and natural dyer with nineteen years of practice. All yarns are dyed before weaving — a process known as yarn dyeing — which ensures color penetration to the very core of each thread, resulting in hues of exceptional permanence.

Our colorants are derived exclusively from botanical and mineral sources: indigo from Isatis tinctoria for blues; weld (Reseda luteola) for warm yellows; madder (Rubia tinctorum) for reds and earths; oak gall and iron mordants for deep greys and blacks. No synthetic dyes are introduced.

Dye method: Yarn (hank) dyeing, pre-weave
Mordanting: Alum, iron, tannin — natural mineral salts only
Colorfastness: ISO 105-B06 Grade 4–5 minimum
03

Warping

Preparing the Foundation

The warp is the longitudinal skeleton of the cloth — the threads that run the length of the fabric and through which the weft is interlaced. Warping is the meticulous process of measuring and tensioning the precise number of warp threads required to produce a given cloth width and thread count.

FILAMENT's warp preparation is carried out entirely by hand on horizontal warping mills. Each warp is measured to a stated tension and verified with a spring-loaded tension meter before being beamed (wound onto the loom's warp beam). This phase is responsible for the dimensional stability and structural integrity of the final cloth.

Ends per centimeter: 30–60 EPC depending on silk type
Tension tolerance: ±2% variation across full warp width
Threading: All heddles threaded by hand
04

Weaving

Thread by Thread

The act of weaving is the atelier's heartbeat — the rhythmic passage of the shuttle through the shed, the beat of the beater consolidating each pick, the gradual materialization of cloth on the cloth beam. At FILAMENT, this process unfolds across twelve heritage looms operated exclusively by hand.

Our weavers work at a rate that prioritizes quality over output. A complex Jacquard commission may advance by no more than fifteen centimeters per hour. The weaver's continuous attentiveness to tension, pattern alignment, and beat density is what distinguishes hand-woven from mechanically produced cloth at the most fundamental level.

Picks per centimeter: 20–45 PPC by weave type
Shuttle passage: 100% manual fly-shuttle method
Pattern monitoring: Visual inspection every 10 picks
05

Finishing

The Last Refinement

Freshly woven silk — known as "grey cloth" — must be transformed through a sequence of finishing processes before it is ready for delivery. At FILAMENT, finishing is overseen by Théodore Blanc, whose protocols have been developed over twenty-two years of practice and refined to exacting standards.

The grey cloth is first scoured in a mild soap bath to remove residual sericin and weaving lubricants. It is then tensioned on a stenter frame to establish the correct width and weave structure, pressed with a calender iron at a temperature specific to the weave type, and finally submitted to a full hand inspection under raking light before being measured, labeled, and prepared for delivery.

Scouring: pH-neutral soap, 40°C maximum
Pressing: Calender iron, 120–160°C by weave type
Inspection: Full visual inspection, both faces, under raking light
Materials Philosophy

What We Work With

Bombyx Mori Silk

We use only mulberry silk from Bombyx mori silkworms — the species that produces the longest, finest, and most uniform filament in the natural world. Sourced from certified sericulture farms in Italy and Japan.

Botanical Dyes

Every colorant used in our dye studio is derived from botanical or mineral sources: indigo, weld, madder, oak gall, iron mordants, and others. No synthetic dyes are introduced at any stage of the process.

Heritage Loom Infrastructure

Our twelve looms range from 1940s oak-framed floor looms to precision Jacquard looms. Each is maintained to manufacturer specification and recalibrated by Sebastian Lohr before every new commission.
