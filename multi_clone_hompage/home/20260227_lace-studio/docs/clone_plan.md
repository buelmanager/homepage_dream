# Clone Plan — DENTELLE Lace Studio

## Concept Origin

This template is an original fictional brand design. It is not a clone of any specific website. The design draws inspiration from the general category of European artisan luxury brands — studios such as Mokuba, Osterfelder, and the Kantcentrum Bruges — but does not reproduce any specific layout, content, or visual identity.

## Target Industry

Traditional handmade textile craft studios, specifically:
- Bobbin lace ateliers
- Needle lace studios
- Heritage textile preservation organisations
- Couture lace suppliers

## Design Rationale

### Hero TYPE G (Text-Driven Scroll Transform)
Selected because it communicates the central brand concept — patience and transformation — through the motion of large typographic elements. The words DEN / TELLE split across two lines reference the French etymology of "dentelle" (lace, from "dent" / tooth — the toothed edge of the lace). The TYPE G hero creates drama without relying on imagery quality, allowing the design to work even when hero images are placeholder.

### Arctic Slate Palette (P5)
The blue-grey cool palette references:
- The quality of northern Belgian light (diffuse, soft)
- The grey-white of aged Belgian linen
- The cold clarity of the lace itself — structural precision without warmth

### Spectral + Mulish Typography
- Spectral provides the historic gravitas appropriate to a four-century tradition, with excellent italic weight for emotional emphasis
- Mulish provides a clean, slightly humanist sans that bridges the historical tone and contemporary luxury positioning

## Page Architecture

```
  ├── about.html    Studio story, lineage, museum credentials
  ├── collection.html  Five lace traditions, full product range
  ├── process.html  Complete making process, tools, thread
  └── contact.html  Commission form, location, care guide
```

## Content Strategy

All copy is original. It presents a fictional but historically grounded studio. Key content decisions:
- Flemish heritage rooted in documented Bruges lace history
- Specific technical accuracy (bobbin counts, thread weights, crossing techniques)
- Museum partnerships given specific names and piece types for credibility
- Commission process given realistic timelines based on actual bobbin lace production rates
- Care guide based on genuine archival textile preservation practice

## Adaptation Notes

To adapt this template for a real studio:
1. Replace all brand names, names, and contact details
2. Replace all images with authentic studio photography
3. Adjust timelines, piece names, and prices to match actual offerings
4. Keep the technical process content accurate — it reflects genuine practice
5. Verify museum and heritage claims against actual studio credentials
