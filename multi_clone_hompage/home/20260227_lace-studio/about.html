Our Story

Lacemakers of Bruges

Three generations of devotion to the art of bobbin lace — a craft that demands more patience than any other, and returns more beauty than most.

"The pillow does not care about your hurry. The thread knows your intention." — Margot Van Damme, founder

The Beginning

A Studio Born from Three Centuries of Tradition

Dentelle was founded in 1983 by Margot Van Damme in the Flemish city of Bruges — the historic capital of European lacemaking. Margot had learned the art from her grandmother, who had learned it from hers, in an unbroken chain stretching back to the seventeenth century.

What began as a single workroom in a canal-side townhouse has grown into a studio of eight practising lacemakers, each trained under the direct mentorship of the Van Damme lineage. The original pillow cushion — on which Margot's grandmother worked her first pattern in 1921 — still occupies the central table of the studio.

Today the studio is led by Margot's daughter, Elena, who added expertise in Venetian needle lace and Chantilly to the family repertoire after training in Venice and the Normandy coast. Elena's daughter, Sophie, joined the studio in 2018 and represents the fourth generation of Van Damme lacemakers.

The Makers

Hands That Hold the Tradition

Each lacemaker at Dentelle has trained for a minimum of five years before working on commissioned pieces. Their collective experience spans over two hundred years of practice.

Elena Van Damme
Master Lacemaker & Studio Director

Elena has practised bobbin and needle lace for thirty-eight years. Her Venetian Point pieces have entered the permanent collections of four European museums. She trained in Bruges, Venice, and Argentan.

Sophie Van Damme
Lacemaker — Bruges & Torchon

The fourth generation of her family's tradition, Sophie brings a rigorous contemporary eye to ancestral patterns. Her work on Torchon grounds has been exhibited in London, Brussels, and Bruges.

Ines Claes
Lacemaker — Chantilly & Honiton

Trained under Elena for seven years, Ines specialises in the extremely fine silk threads required for authentic Chantilly. Her bridal pieces have been worn in royal ceremonies across three countries.

Museum Partnerships

Works in Permanent Collections

Dentelle lace is held in the permanent collections of twelve international museums and cultural institutions. Each acquisition is accompanied by a full technical dossier documenting thread provenance, technique, and lacemaker biography.

We also accept long-term loans for temporary exhibitions and provide educational workshops for museum curatorial staff on the identification and care of historical lace.

What We Believe

The Values That Guide Every Thread

⸻

Authenticity Over Speed

We do not use machine-made lace or manufactured grounds. Every thread in every piece is placed by a human hand with deliberate intention. This takes longer. We consider that to be the point.

⸻

Transmission of Knowledge

We train one new lacemaker per year, a commitment we consider non-negotiable. The studio exists not only to make lace but to ensure the tradition continues into the next generation.

⸻

Material Integrity

We source only Belgian linen, French silk, and Japanese cotton threads. All materials are tested for archival stability. Dentelle pieces are intended to endure not years but centuries.

⸻

Radical Transparency

Every commission comes with a complete making record: the lacemaker's name, the thread source, the hours invested, and the specific techniques employed. You know exactly what you are holding.
