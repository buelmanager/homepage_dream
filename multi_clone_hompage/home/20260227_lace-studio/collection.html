The Collection

Five Traditions, One Studio

Every piece in the Dentelle collection is made entirely by hand using centuries-old techniques. Each tradition demands its own tools, thread weight, and body of pattern knowledge accumulated across generations.

01
Bruges Bobbin Lace

The City's Own Language

Bruges bobbin lace — also called Bruges Flower or Bruges Guipure — is the foundational tradition of our studio. Characterised by a continuous thread path, flowing floral motifs, and a distinctive raised braid ground, it is considered among the most demanding of the European bobbin traditions.

A standard collar requires between four and six weeks of daily work by a single lacemaker. Collars, cuffs, ecclesiastical trimmings, and bridal accessories are our primary Bruges offerings. Custom dimensions accepted.

02
Venetian Point

Needle Lace of Venice

Venetian Point — Punto in Aria — is needle lace, worked entirely with a single needle and thread over a drawn pattern rather than with bobbins. It is the oldest form of true lace and the most structurally complex. Each stitch is a separate buttonhole knot; there is no shortcut, no approximate.

Elena Van Damme trained in Burano, Venice, and is one of fewer than thirty practitioners in the world capable of working full Venetian Point at the historic thread count. Our Venetian pieces are intended for museum collections and the most significant private commissions.

03
Torchon

Geometric Precision

Torchon is the most geometric of the European lace traditions, built on a diagonal grid of crossings that produces bold, angular patterns quite unlike the flowing curves of Bruges or the organic forms of Honiton. Its name derives from the French for "duster cloth" — a reflection of its historically utilitarian origins.

What Dentelle brings to Torchon is elevation: we work with fine Belgian linen at thread counts reserved for decorative or wearable pieces, producing Torchon panels, table borders, and fashion accessories of extraordinary refinement. Torchon is also our primary tradition for teaching new lacemakers.

04
Chantilly

The Language of Silk

Chantilly lace is worked entirely in black or white silk, on a hexagonal mesh ground, with motifs outlined in a thicker silk cordonnet thread. It is the preeminent lace for bridal, couture, and high-fashion application — demanding a lightness of touch that only comes from years of working exclusively with silk.

Ines Claes leads our Chantilly programme. Her bridal veils are made to measure, with the bride's initials or family crest worked into the border pattern. Chantilly pieces for couture houses are made to specification, with samples provided for fabric coordination.

05
Honiton

The English Flower Garden

Honiton lace originated in Devon, England, and is distinguished by its appliquéd construction: individual motifs — flowers, leaves, butterflies — are worked separately and then assembled onto a net ground. The effect is three-dimensional and pictorial, closer to botanical illustration than to textile.

Dentelle is one of a small number of studios outside England that practises authentic Honiton. Our pieces are made in fine linen thread, with the naturalistic motifs that characterised the tradition at its zenith in the nineteenth century. Royal collections have historically favoured Honiton for state occasions.
