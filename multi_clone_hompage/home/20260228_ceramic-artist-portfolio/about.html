About Terra Forma

Terra Forma Studio, established 2018

Artist Biography

Where Earth and Hand Meet Fire

My work begins in silence — with a ball of grey clay and the slow, meditative rhythm of the wheel. I am a studio ceramicist and sculptor working from a wood-fired kiln in the Welsh borders, where the landscape itself becomes a collaborator.

Trained at the Royal College of Art and the Jingdezhen Ceramic Institute, my practice draws on both the European tradition of studio pottery and the ancient Asian ceramic philosophies of wabi-sabi and anagama firing — a belief that imperfection carries truth, and that the most beautiful objects bear the visible evidence of their making.

I work primarily with stoneware and porcelain, often mixing my own clay bodies from locally sourced materials. Each firing is an exercise in surrender — to the unpredictability of the kiln atmosphere, the movement of ash glazes, the way iron transforms in reduction. Every piece that emerges is a collaboration between intention and chance.

Terra Forma commissions work for private collectors, galleries, and architectural spaces. I take a limited number of commissions each year to ensure every piece receives my full attention.

Eleanor Voss

Ceramicist & Sculptor — Founder, Terra Forma

Studio Story

A Studio Built on Obsession

Terra Forma began in a converted stone barn in 2018, with a second-hand wheel, a small electric kiln, and an obsessive curiosity about the chemistry of heat and mineral. The name — Latin for "earth form" — was chosen deliberately: it describes both the material and the intention.

Within two years, we had built our first wood-firing kiln — a noborigama, a stepped climbing kiln based on ancient Japanese designs. The first firing lasted 36 hours. We lost half the pieces. The other half were extraordinary.

The studio now operates two kilns: the wood-firing noborigama for ash-glazed stoneware and the electric kiln for porcelain work requiring precise temperature control. We fire approximately four times per year — each firing an event, a ritual, a two-day burning.

We source clay from quarries in Cornwall, Wales, and Derbyshire, blending our own bodies. Our glazes are made from scratch — feldspar, quartz, dolomite, iron oxide, copper carbonate — minerals that tell the story of geological time compressed into the surface of a bowl.

Materials & Methods

The Craft of Deep Materials

Every material decision is intentional. From the clay beneath our feet to the ash that settles on a shoulder in the kiln — we work with the earth's own chemistry.

Clay Bodies

Locally Sourced Stoneware

Our primary stoneware is a blend of Cornish stone, red earthenware, and ball clay — a body that fires warm and responsive, showing the marks of the hand clearly. We blend our own bodies for each collection series.

Porcelain

White & Translucent Forms

For our sculptural and tableware series, we use a high-fired porcelain that achieves near-translucency at 1280°C. Porcelain demands precision — it warps, cracks, and surprises in ways that stoneware does not.

Glazes

Mineral-Based, Hand-Mixed

We make every glaze from raw minerals: feldspar, silica, whiting, iron oxide, copper carbonate, cobalt. Our ash glazes are derived from wood ash collected after each kiln firing — the material continues to participate.

Kiln Firing

Wood-Fire & Reduction

Our noborigama wood-fire kiln is the heart of the studio. Each firing burns for 30-36 hours at temperatures reaching 1300°C. In reduction atmosphere, oxides transform — copper becomes red, iron becomes green or amber.
