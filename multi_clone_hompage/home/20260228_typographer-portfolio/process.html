How We Work

Five Phases of Type Design

Every typeface commission follows a rigorous five-phase process — one that honours centuries of typographic craft while delivering production-ready digital font files.

01
Phase One

Research & Discovery

Every typeface begins with deep contextual research. We study the brand's history, voice, and competitive landscape; examine relevant historical type specimens; and interview key stakeholders to understand what personality the type must carry. This phase often involves visits to archives, libraries, and printing collections.

  • Brand voice & typographic audit
  • Historical reference presentation
  • Competitive landscape analysis
  • Design direction moodboard
  • Project brief sign-off
"Begin always with looking."
02
Phase Two

Concept & Sketching

Before opening Glyphs or Illustrator, we sketch. Hundreds of pencil drawings explore the basic proportions, stroke contrast, terminal styles, and serif treatments of the new typeface. A small control set — typically H, O, n, o, p, and a — establishes the system that all remaining characters will follow.

  • Pencil sketch exploration (50+ drawings)
  • Key character set definition
  • Proportion & contrast study
  • Direction presentation to client
  • Approved design direction
"The pencil knows things the mouse cannot."
03
Phase Three

Drawing & Refinement

Approved sketches are digitised in Glyphs App, with each letterform carefully constructed using Bézier curves. This phase is the most time-intensive: a full typeface family of 500+ glyphs per weight takes 300–600 hours of drawing. Letterpress proofing at our studio informs digital decisions throughout.

  • Full character set drawing (A–Z, a–z, 0–9, punctuation)
  • Ligatures & alternates
  • Diacritics & multilingual support
  • Letterpress proof review
  • Client specimen sign-off
"Every curve is a decision."
04
Phase Four

Spacing & Kerning

Spacing is the most invisible and most critical aspect of type design. We use a systematic rhythm-based approach — first setting sidebearings for all characters, then manually kerning thousands of critical pairs. The final typeface is tested at 6pt, 12pt, 36pt, and 200pt across both screen and print environments.

  • Full sidebearing metrics
  • Manual kerning (3,000–8,000 pairs)
  • Multi-environment testing
  • Optical size calibration
  • Print proof at final sizes
"Space between letters is as important as the letters themselves."
05
Phase Five

Release & Support

The typeface is delivered as production-ready OTF, TTF, WOFF, and WOFF2 files with comprehensive licensing documentation. We provide a full specimen PDF, implementation guidance, and a 12-month support period for any technical issues. All custom typefaces remain exclusive to the commissioning client.

  • OTF / TTF / WOFF / WOFF2 files
  • OpenType feature documentation
  • Specimen PDF (40+ pages)
  • Implementation guide
  • 12-month technical support
"A typeface is never finished — only released."
Tools of the Trade

Digital & Analogue

Gg
Glyphs App

Primary typeface design software — used for all character drawing, spacing, kerning, OpenType feature coding, and font export.

Ai
Adobe Illustrator

Vector refinement and complex custom glyph construction — particularly for logotype letterforms and ornamental elements.

2B
Pencil & Paper

All ideation begins with pencil. We use 2B graphite on layout paper for initial sketching, and ink brush for calligraphic reference.

LP
Letterpress

Our Heidelberg Platen press and Vandercook proofing press provide analogue testing at every stage — the ink and paper tell truths the screen cannot.

Letterpress Revival

Why We Still Use the Press

Letterpress proofing is not nostalgia — it is a discipline. When ink meets paper under pressure, every imperfection in the letterform is revealed. Our press workflow informs and improves every digital typeface we design.

  • 01
    Set the type

    Hand-composed from our collection of Monotype and Linotype cast metal type — over 40 cases spanning 18 faces.

  • 02
    Lock the forme

    Type is locked in a galley and imposed into the press bed with furniture and quoins — tested for correct alignment.

  • 03
    Ink and proof

    We pull proofs on Zerkall and Fabriano papers — noting how each letterform behaves under varying ink pressure and impression depth.

  • 04
    Translate to digital

    Observations from the proof — ink traps, stroke weights, optical corrections — are applied back to the digital masters in Glyphs.
