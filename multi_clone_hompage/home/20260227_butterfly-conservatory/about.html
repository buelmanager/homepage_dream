Our Story

Two scientists, one shared obsession, and a conviction that beauty is the most powerful argument for conservation.

The Founding

Two Minds, One
Living Dream

In 1987, Dr. Elena Maris, a Costa Rican lepidopterist returning from a decade of fieldwork in the Amazon, met Alejandro Vega, a botanist specializing in tropical plant ecology, at a conservation symposium in San José.

Their conversation lasted three days. By the end, they had sketched the floor plan of what would become LEPIDOPTERA on the back of a field notebook — a conservatory where butterflies would live freely among the plants that their species depended on, and where visitors would experience that relationship from the inside.

They broke ground on a two-hectare plot in the Zona Verde hills in 1988. The first butterflies — sixty Blue Morphos and forty Monarchs — arrived on March 12, 1989. Today, that same land holds five climate-controlled biomes, a research library of 4,000 volumes, and the most diverse captive butterfly collection in the Western Hemisphere.

Conservation Mission

Three Pillars of
Purpose

Active Breeding

We operate twelve breeding chambers, each tuned to the host plants and microclimate of a specific species. Every quarter, we release captive-bred butterflies into protected forest reserves where wild populations have declined.

Scientific Research

Our on-site laboratory has contributed 212 peer-reviewed papers on butterfly behavior, wing pigmentation, migration patterns, and host plant ecology. We maintain open-data partnerships with eight universities across four continents.

Community Education

We host more than 8,000 students annually through our school programs, providing curriculum-aligned workshops on metamorphosis, biodiversity, and tropical ecosystems. Admission is subsidized for Title I schools.

Milestones

Thirty-Seven Years
of Growth

1987
The Sketch and the Promise

Dr. Elena Maris and Alejandro Vega meet at a conservation symposium in San José, Costa Rica. Over three days of conversation, the founding concept of LEPIDOPTERA takes shape.

1989
First Butterflies Arrive

The original greenhouse opens with 100 butterflies across three species. Within the first month, the first successful breeding cycle is documented and the scientific mission begins.

1994
Borneo Partnership Established

LEPIDOPTERA enters a formal conservation partnership with the Sabah Forestry Department, enabling the acquisition of rare Birdwing butterfly species and expanding research into Southeast Asian ecology.

2002
Five Zones Complete

The fifth and final biome — the Afrotropical Zone — opens, completing the conservatory's global representation. Total collection surpasses 1,000 butterflies across 120 species.

2011
100,000th Release

LEPIDOPTERA achieves a landmark milestone: the 100,000th captive-bred butterfly is released into protected forest corridors. A sunrise ceremony marks the occasion with 300 attending guests.

2019
International Heritage Designation

LEPIDOPTERA is awarded designation as a Site of Biological and Cultural Heritage by the International Union for Conservation of Nature, recognizing three decades of sustained conservation impact.

2026
300 Species in Collection

The collection reaches 300 documented species — the most diverse captive butterfly collection in the Western Hemisphere. LEPIDOPTERA now welcomes over 140,000 visitors annually from 68 countries.
