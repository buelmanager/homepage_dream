01 Amazon Basin Zone
Morpho Genus

Blue Morpho

Morpho peleides

The Blue Morpho is not technically blue — it is an optical phenomenon. Microscopic scales on its wings refract light in a way that produces the most vivid blue in the natural world, without any blue pigment at all. In direct sunlight, a Morpho in flight looks like a fragment of sky that has learned to navigate.

We maintain one of the largest Morpho collections outside of Brazil, with breeding programs ensuring we can support wild population recovery in deforested Amazon regions.

Wingspan 12–20 cm
Lifespan 115 days
Status Stable
02 Costa Rica Cloud Zone
Nymphalidae Family

Monarch

Danaus plexippus

No butterfly has captured human imagination quite like the Monarch, whose annual migration spans 4,500 kilometers from Canada to the mountains of Michoacán, Mexico. A creature weighing less than a paperclip navigates this journey using the sun as a compass and a circadian clock of extraordinary precision.

Our Monarch colony represents several migratory generations. Their orange-and-black coloring is a warning signal: they sequester toxins from milkweed during the caterpillar stage, making them unpalatable to most predators.

Wingspan 8.9–10.2 cm
Migration 4,500 km
Status Endangered
03 Southeast Asian Zone
Saturniidae Family

Atlas Moth

Attacus atlas

The Atlas Moth is the world's largest moth by wing surface area — a wingspan reaching up to 30 centimeters, with wing tips that have evolved to mimic the head of a snake as a predator deterrent. An Atlas adult has no functional mouth and lives entirely off fat reserves built during the caterpillar stage.

Their cocoon silk, stronger than commercial silk, has been used for centuries in Taiwan to make small purses. Our breeding program works with artisan communities to create ethical silk textiles while protecting wild populations.

Wingspan 25–30 cm
Adult Lifespan 1–2 weeks
Status Vulnerable
04 Borneo Lowland Zone
Papilionidae Family

Ulysses Swallowtail

Papilio ulysses

The electric blue of the Ulysses Swallowtail is so vivid that Australian tourism once placed its image on every airline seat pocket magazine. Found in northeast Australia and surrounding islands, the male's iridescent wings are used in courtship displays that can be seen from 50 meters away.

Ulysses butterflies are attracted to the color blue with extraordinary intensity — our keepers must avoid wearing blue clothing inside the Borneo zone, as the butterflies will persistently land on them, mistaking them for flowers.

Wingspan 10.5–14 cm
Flight Speed 30 km/h
Status Stable
05 Borneo Lowland Zone
Papilionidae Family

Queen Alexandra's Birdwing

Ornithoptera alexandrae

The largest butterfly on Earth, with a wingspan exceeding 30 centimeters in females. Named after Queen Alexandra of Denmark in 1906, this species is found only in a small area of Oro Province, Papua New Guinea. Trade in wild specimens has been illegal since 1966.

LEPIDOPTERA holds one of only fourteen institutional permits worldwide to maintain captive Birdwing specimens. Our three breeding pairs are part of an international conservation program coordinated with the Papua New Guinea Department of Environment and Conservation.

Wingspan Up to 31 cm
Rarity CITES I
Status Endangered
