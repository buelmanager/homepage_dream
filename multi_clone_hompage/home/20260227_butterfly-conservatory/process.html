Lifecycle & Experiences

From Egg to Wing

We have designed the world's most intimate encounter with metamorphosis. Follow a butterfly from its first breath as an egg to the moment its wings dry in the morning light.

The Four Stages

Metamorphosis Up Close

I
Stage One

The Egg

A butterfly egg is a marvel of miniature engineering. Smaller than a pinhead, each one is architecturally distinct — ridged, ribbed, spherical, or flattened, depending on the species. The surface microstructure regulates moisture and gas exchange with extraordinary precision.

Our egg chamber houses over 40 host plant species. Female butterflies select their plant with extraordinary chemical sensitivity — detecting the correct plant compounds through receptors on their feet before laying. An egg placed on the wrong plant will not survive.

Duration: 3–14 days
Temperature: 26–32°C
Host plants: 40+ species
II
Stage Two

The Larva

The caterpillar exists for one purpose: to eat. In as few as ten days, a Monarch caterpillar increases its mass by 2,000 times. During this phase, the larva molts its skin (exoskeleton) four times, each instar — as each stage is called — revealing a slightly different form.

Our Larval Gallery is arranged by feeding strategy: some species are generalists, others specialized to single plant families. Many have evolved extraordinary defenses — toxic spines, cryptic markings, false eye spots, even the ability to sequester plant toxins into their own bodies.

Duration: 10–30 days
Instars: 4–5 molts
Mass gain: 2000×
III
Stage Three

The Chrysalis

The chrysalis is not a resting state — it is the most radical biological restructuring in the animal kingdom. Within the sealed case, most of the caterpillar's body dissolves into an undifferentiated soup of cells called imaginal discs, which then reorganize entirely into the butterfly's new form.

Our Chrysalis Chamber hosts hanging pupae in various stages of development. Many are visible in UV light — their cases becoming increasingly transparent in the final hours before emergence, allowing visitors to observe the folded wings of the butterfly taking shape inside.

Duration: 7–21 days
Humidity: 70–85%
Visible under: UV light
IV
Stage Four

Emergence & Release

Emergence — called eclosion — is the defining event of LEPIDOPTERA. Each morning at 9:00 AM, our head naturalist leads guests into the Emergence Chamber as the overnight chrysalides begin to split open. The butterfly that steps out pumps fluid into its wings over 1–2 hours, and then, briefly, holds perfectly still.

Butterflies destined for breeding programs remain in the conservatory. Overstock and healthy wild-type individuals are released at our partner forest corridor. Guests who book the Emergence Ceremony experience are invited to be present for the release — watching dozens of wings catch wind for the first time.

Eclosion time: 1–2 hours
Daily releases: 9:00 AM
Wing dry time: 45–90 min

Breeding Program

Conservation Through Cultivation

Our twelve breeding chambers operate year-round, each dedicated to a specific species and its host plant ecosystem. Dr. Elena Maris and her team of four resident lepidopterists manage every generation, tracking individual lineages to maintain genetic diversity and prevent inbreeding depression.

The program operates on a simple principle: butterflies thrive when they have the right plant, the right temperature, and the right light cycle. When those conditions are met precisely, breeding success rates exceed 87% — far above the industry average of 60–65%.

Quarterly releases to our partner forest corridors ensure that captive genetics return to wild populations. We work with GPS-tagged individuals in some species to track post-release survival and integration — data we share with the IUCN for global conservation planning.

The Ceremony

Daily Emergence & Release

Every morning, the Emergence Chamber becomes a theater of biology. Overnight, chrysalides darken as the butterfly inside completes its final reorganization. At first light, they begin to split. A butterfly that emerged hours ago is still pumping hemolymph into its wings — a process that takes 60–90 minutes — when guests arrive.

We offer three daily emergence viewing windows, with a limited number of guests per session to ensure the butterflies are undisturbed. Fridays include an extended afternoon release ceremony at our adjacent forest corridor, where guests may watch individuals fly into the tree canopy for the first time.
