## Brand Originality

The LEPIDOPTERA brand is entirely invented for this template:

- **Brand name:** "LEPIDOPTERA" is the scientific order name for butterflies and moths, repurposed as a luxury brand name. Not based on any real institution.
- **Founders:** Dr. Elena Maris and Alejandro Vega are fictional characters. The founding narrative (1987, San José symposium, Costa Rica location) is invented.
- **Mission language:** All conservation mission text, statistics (212 research papers, 500K+ releases, 140,000 annual visitors) are fictional.
- **Historical timeline:** All seven timeline events are fictional.
- **Species descriptions:** Factual information about real butterfly species (Blue Morpho, Monarch, Ulysses, Atlas Moth, Queen Alexandra's Birdwing) is drawn from publicly available scientific knowledge. Conservatory-specific claims are fictional.
- **Staff team:** All four team members (Dr. Elena Maris, Alejandro Vega, Dr. Riya Kapoor, Marco Delgado) are fictional.
- **Testimonials:** All four testimonials and testimonial authors are fictional.

## Design Originality

### Layout
The layout system (Hero B parallax, statistics strip, philosophy grid, collection grid, process steps, heritage two-column, testimonials swiper, booking form) is original composition. The specific combination and visual execution does not replicate any single existing template.

### Color System
The Forest Night palette is an original derivation — not drawn from any existing template in the project library. The accent green (#4DAF6A) is calibrated specifically to the natural reference (Morpho butterfly wing underside).

### Typography
DM Serif Display + Karla pairing is used in a distinctive way: the weight distribution (Display for h-tags and large numbers, Karla 300/400/500 for all UI and body text) creates a specific visual hierarchy not replicated elsewhere in the library.

### SVG Illustrations
All butterfly SVG illustrations throughout the site are original geometric representations created for this template. They use a consistent wing-path construction (upper forewing, lower forewing, upper hindwing, lower hindwing, body line) scaled for different contexts.
