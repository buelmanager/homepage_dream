The Inquiry Process

Every inquiry is read personally by Maestro Ferrante or a senior member of the studio. We do not use automated responses. You will receive a considered reply within 48 hours, and — if your project is within our scope — an invitation to a more detailed conversation.

We maintain a maximum of eight concurrent active commissions. If our schedule is full, we will tell you immediately and, if appropriate, place you on a priority waiting list.

From Inquiry to Masterwork

How a Commission Begins

01

You Inquire

Submit the inquiry form above. Include as much detail as you can about the space, your vision, and the intended iconography. We respond personally within 48 hours.

02

Initial Conversation

If the project falls within our scope, we arrange a first conversation — in person in Florence, at the project site, or by video. This conversation is exploratory and carries no obligation.

03

Formal Consultation

If both parties wish to proceed, we enter a formal consultation period of 6–12 weeks. During this time, we develop a detailed proposal — composition, palette, materials, timeline, and budget — before any contract is signed.

Frequently Asked

Questions We Often Hear

Commission timelines vary considerably depending on scale and complexity. A salon ceiling of 60m² might require 8–14 months from first consultation to completion. A cathedral vault of 300m² may require three to four years. We are transparent about realistic timelines from the earliest conversations. We do not accept commissions we cannot complete to our standard within the agreed schedule.
Commission fees are quoted individually after a thorough understanding of the scope. Fresco is among the most labour-intensive of all art forms, and our fees reflect the years of formation that each painter brings. We do not publish price ranges because they would be misleading — a 40m² domestic salon and a 40m² church dome require entirely different resources. What we can say is that we have never turned down a commission on the basis of budget alone, and that our fees have always proved to be a small fraction of the enduring value the work adds to a property.
Not every wall is suitable for buon fresco. The technique requires a wall that can accept lime plaster — typically masonry, brick, or concrete block. Timber-framed walls, steel structures, and walls with active damp or movement are generally not suitable without significant preparatory work. We assess every wall carefully before accepting a commission and will always advise honestly if the structure is unsuitable. In some cases, we recommend fresco secco or lime-based wall painting as an alternative that can achieve comparable results.
A completed buon fresco requires remarkably little maintenance when the environment is stable. We provide every client with a detailed conservation care protocol. In brief: maintain stable humidity (ideally 50–60% RH), avoid direct water contact, and keep the surface free of dust using only a soft, dry brush. No surface treatments, varnishes, or cleaning agents should ever be applied without consulting us first. We offer triennial inspection visits for all commissions.
Every INTONACO commission is covered by a three-generation conservation guarantee. This is a notarized document, not merely a statement of intent. It commits us — and our successor studio, should the studio continue beyond the current principals — to provide conservation assistance for any structural failure attributable to our materials or technique. It does not cover damage caused by building failure, flood, fire, or deliberate interference. The documentation we provide with every commission — including spectroscopic pigment analysis and plaster layer records — makes any future conservation work vastly simpler.
Yes. Our restoration programme, directed by Maestro Chiara Moretti, accepts commissions for the conservation and restoration of historic fresco cycles from the 13th through 19th centuries. We work to the standards of the European Confederation of Conservator-Restorers (E.C.C.O.) and require full cooperation with relevant heritage authorities where applicable. All restoration work is reversible, documented, and reported.
We welcome visits to the Florence studio by appointment. Seeing the materials, the preparation process, and — if a commission is in progress — painters at work on the scaffold is the best possible introduction to what INTONACO does. Please contact us to arrange a visit. We host a small number of studio visits each year for serious prospective clients.
Conservation Programme

Three Generations
of Commitment

A completed fresco commission does not end INTONACO's relationship with the work. We regard every wall we have painted as a continuing responsibility — not merely a transaction.

Our conservation programme covers all commissions from the studio's founding in 2012. Wherever in the world your INTONACO commission is located, we will send a conservator to conduct an inspection, diagnose any concerns, and provide remediation.

  • Triennial inspection visits offered for all commissions
  • Complete layer documentation in our conservation archive
  • 48-hour response to any emergency conservation inquiry
  • Notarized three-generation conservation guarantee on every commission
  • Training for facility management teams on correct daily care
  • Annual condition reports available on request
