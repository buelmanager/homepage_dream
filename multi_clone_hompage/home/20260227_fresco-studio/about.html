The INTONACO Story

A Florentine atelier built on seven centuries of fresco tradition — and one master painter's refusal to let it die.

2012 Florence, Italy
The Founding

A Master's
Obsession Made
Into a Studio

INTONACO was born from a single act of conviction. In 2012, Maestro Aldo Ferrante — then completing his final year of Vatican restoration work — turned down three international museum positions and rented a damp studio on Via delle Terme in Florence. He had one intention: to build a fresco workshop that could accept the most ambitious architectural commissions in the world and execute them with the full rigour of the Renaissance tradition.

The name INTONACO refers to the final layer of plaster onto which fresco pigment is applied — the irreversible, breathing surface where ancient art and living architecture become one. For Ferrante, it was the most honest name he could give a studio: everything depends on that layer. Get it wrong, and the work is lost. Get it right, and it endures centuries.

In twelve years, the studio has grown from one painter to forty-seven. The apprenticeship model remains unchanged. The ambition has only grown larger.

Studio History

Twelve Years,
Fourteen Countries

2012

Studio Founded in Florence

Maestro Aldo Ferrante establishes INTONACO in a 16th-century building on Via delle Terme. First commission: the private chapel of Palazzo Vettori, Siena.

2014

First International Commission

INTONACO is engaged by a collector in Geneva for the entrance hall of a private residence. The project introduces the studio to the international luxury residential market.

2016

Sacred Spaces Programme Launched

Following the restoration of a 17th-century fresco in Umbria, INTONACO establishes a dedicated sacred spaces programme for church and chapel commissions globally.

2018

Atelier Paris Opened

A satellite studio in the Marais district enables deeper engagement with French hôtels particuliers and hospitality clients across Europe.

2020

Pigment Research Published

INTONACO's laboratory research on the longevity of mineral pigments versus synthetic dyes in humid environments is published in the Journal of Cultural Heritage.

2022

Ten-Year Retrospective Exhibition

A retrospective of forty commissions is mounted at the Palazzo Strozzi foundation, Florence. The exhibition travels to Paris and New York.

2024

Forty-Seven Masters, 340 Commissions

INTONACO completes its 340th commission — a ceiling vault for a private residence in Kyoto — marking the studio's expansion into Asia and its largest roster of master painters.

Our Masters

Painters Formed
by Seven Years
of Discipline

Each painter who bears the INTONACO name has completed the full seven-year formation: three years in traditional fresco technique, two in restoration science, and two under a named master of our lineage. No exceptions have ever been made.

A
Aldo Ferrante
Founder & Maestro

Trained at the Accademia di Belle Arti di Firenze and the Vatican restoration workshop. Over thirty years of practice in buon fresco, sinopia drawing, and architectural restoration. Ferrante is regarded as the foremost practitioner of classical fresco in the modern era.

  • Accademia di Belle Arti, Florence — First Class
  • Vatican Museums Conservation Laboratory
  • Fellow, Istituto Nazionale di Archeologia e Storia dell'Arte
C
Chiara Moretti
Head of Restoration

Specialist in the consolidation and recovery of damaged fresco cycles. Moretti spent eight years working with the Opificio delle Pietre Dure in Florence before joining INTONACO. She has directed restoration on works from the 13th to 18th centuries.

  • Opificio delle Pietre Dure, Florence
  • Conservation training, Hermitage Museum, St Petersburg
  • PhD, Conservation Science, Università di Bologna
L
Luca Bernardi
Maestro — Sacred Spaces

Bernardi leads all sacred and ecclesiastical commissions. A former seminarian turned painter, his iconographic knowledge is unrivalled within the studio. He has completed fresco cycles in eleven countries across four continents.

  • Studio di Teologia Pontificia, Rome
  • Formation under Maestro Ferrante, 2014–2021
  • Visiting lecturer, Accademia di Brera, Milan
E
Elena Vasquez
Maestro — Pigment Laboratory

A trained chemist before becoming a painter, Vasquez directs the INTONACO pigment laboratory. Her research into the behaviour of natural mineral pigments in modern architectural environments has reshaped the studio's practice across all climates.

  • BSc Chemistry, Universidad Complutense de Madrid
  • Formation under Maestro Ferrante, 2015–2022
  • Published researcher, Journal of Cultural Heritage
T
Thomas Kuhn
Maestro — Germany & Northern Europe

INTONACO's first non-Italian master, Kuhn trained in Munich before undertaking his full formation in Florence. He leads all commissions in Germany, Austria, Switzerland, and Scandinavia, where the studio's work is increasingly sought for contemporary architecture.

  • Akademie der Bildenden Künste München
  • Formation under Chiara Moretti, 2016–2023
  • Commission: Bayerische Staatsbibliothek ceiling cycle
Y
Yuki Tanaka
Maestro — Asia Pacific

Tanaka bridges Italian fresco tradition and Japanese spatial philosophy. Her approach to scale, negative space, and the relationship between mural and architecture has earned critical attention across Asia. She leads INTONACO's newest and fastest-growing regional practice.

  • Tokyo University of the Arts — Painting
  • Formation under Maestro Ferrante, 2017–2024
  • INTONACO's 340th commission, Kyoto, 2024
What We Believe

The Principles of the Studio

Irreversibility as Discipline

Buon fresco cannot be corrected after the plaster dries. This constraint is not a limitation — it is the source of every painter's highest focus and most decisive hand.

Time is the Medium

We do not rush. A commission may require months of consultation before a single mark is made. The right beginning is worth any patience it requires.

Stewardship, Not Ownership

A completed fresco belongs to the wall, to the building, and to every generation that will live within it. We are only the first custodians of many.

Formation Above All

We will always refuse a commission rather than accept one that requires a painter not yet ready to execute it. The work has a right to the best hand available.
