The Scripts

Choose Your Hand

Copperplate

England, c. 17th Century

The most celebrated Western script — thin hairline upstrokes and dramatically swelling downstrokes, executed at a 52° slant with a flexible pointed nib. Copperplate demands patience and rewards practice with extraordinary elegance.

Duration
2 Days
Max Students
8
Level
All Levels
Investment
£380
Curriculum
  • Pen selection, nib preparation, and ink mixing
  • The oblique pen holder — angle, grip, and arm movement
  • Oval exercises and the basic letterform structure
  • Full lowercase alphabet, day one
  • Uppercase letterforms and variations, day two
  • Flourishing and layout for envelope addressing

Spencerian

United States, c. 1850

America's great contribution to formal penmanship — lighter, more oval than Copperplate, with a gentle 52° slant and an emphasis on effortless speed. Originally taught as a business hand, Spencerian is today prized for its lyrical grace.

Duration
2 Days
Max Students
8
Level
Beginner+
Investment

Curriculum
  • The Spencerian seven principles of motion
  • Pen hold, paper position, and arm movement
  • Primary shades — when and how to press
  • Lowercase letterforms and connections
  • Capitals with flourishes
  • Addressing practice and personal style development

Italic

Italy, c. 15th Century

The great Renaissance hand — structured, legible, and immensely versatile. Written with a broad-edged nib, Italic is one of the most accessible classical scripts and an ideal foundation for those new to formal calligraphy.

  • The basic stroke family — arch, oval, diagonal
  • Full lowercase and uppercase in one day
  • Spacing, layout, and simple flourishing
  • Personal project: a name or short quotation

Modern Calligraphy

Contemporary

Expressive, free-form, and joyful — Modern Calligraphy breaks the rules deliberately. Bouncy baselines, exaggerated ascenders, and fluid brush pen strokes define this highly fashionable style, ideal for wedding signage and social media.

  • Flourishing, banners, and decorative elements
  • Practical project: a welcome sign or gift tag set

In the Atelier

What to Expect

A Georgian Atelier in Marylebone

Workshops take place in our writing room — a quiet, naturally lit space with individual desks, adjustable lamp rings, and a materials library of over 200 nibs, 40 inks, and 30 paper stocks.

All Materials Provided

Every student receives a full starter kit: oblique or straight pen holder, four nibs, two ink bottles, practice sheets, and a quality cotton rag sheet for the final piece. The kit is yours to take home.

Lunch and Refreshments

A light lunch from a Marylebone delicatessen and unlimited tea and coffee are included for all full-day and two-day workshops. One-day workshops include morning pastries and afternoon biscuits.

Post-Workshop Support

All students receive a private ink house online resource with practice sheets, reference alphabets, and a personal feedback channel for questions in the 30 days following the workshop.

Practical Info

What We Provide & What to Bring

Included in Your Kit

  • Oblique or straight pen holder (yours to keep)
  • Selection of four pointed nibs
  • Two 30ml bottles of walnut and carbon ink
  • Guided practice booklet (A4, 40 pages)
  • One sheet of 100% cotton rag paper
  • Ink wipes and cleaning cloth

Please Bring

  • Comfortable, washable clothing (ink happens)
  • A notebook for personal observations
  • Reading glasses if needed for close work
  • An open and patient mind
  • Nothing else — we have everything ready
