Five-Phase Lifecycle

From Concept
to Legacy

Every mission we undertake follows a rigorous five-phase lifecycle, ensuring scientific integrity, engineering excellence, and responsible stewardship of public resources.

01

Concept & Proposal

Duration: 6–18 months
Scientific community proposals are evaluated by our Mission Review Board — a panel of independent scientists, engineers, and policy advisors. Each proposal must define clear scientific objectives, justify resource requirements, identify orbital windows, and demonstrate feasibility within existing or projected technical capabilities. Approved proposals advance to a feasibility study phase before receiving formal mission designation.
02

Design & Engineering

Duration: 18–48 months
Once approved, mission design begins in earnest. Payload engineers, systems architects, and software teams collaborate on spacecraft design, sensor integration, and communications architecture. All design decisions are documented and validated against international standards. Our international partner framework enables joint development, with partner agencies co-funding instruments or providing launch vehicles in exchange for shared data access.
03

Launch Preparation

Duration: 3–9 months
The final phase before launch encompasses fuelling, environmental testing, vibration and thermal cycling validation, payload installation, and countdown procedure rehearsals. Our Launch Complex supports both indigenous and partnered launch vehicles. Mission Control undergoes full simulation runs including anomaly response drills. Launch window is confirmed no less than 90 days in advance following final systems review.
04

Active Operations

Duration: Mission-specific
Our Mission Control operates around the clock throughout active mission phases. Flight controllers monitor telemetry, execute trajectory corrections, and oversee instrument operations. The global ground station network — including partner stations on four continents — ensures near-continuous contact with spacecraft. Scientific data is downlinked, processed, and distributed to principal investigators within 48 hours of receipt.
05

Data & Legacy

Duration: Ongoing
Mission completion is not an ending — it is the beginning of the scientific legacy phase. All mission data is permanently archived in the Stellaris Open Data Repository, freely accessible to researchers worldwide. Mission findings are published in peer-reviewed journals, and educational materials are developed for our national schools network. Hardware and lessons learned are formally documented to inform future mission generations.
Operating Standards

Our Commitment
to Excellence

◆

Safety First

All Stellaris missions adhere to ECSS and NASA safety standards. Independent safety panels review critical design reviews and launch readiness assessments.

◆

Open Science

We are committed to open data principles. All mission data enters the public archive within 18 months of collection, enabling global scientific discovery.

◆

Orbital Responsibility

Stellaris adheres to international debris mitigation guidelines. All spacecraft include end-of-life disposal plans to protect the orbital environment.
