Our History

Founded in a Reading
Room, 1891

The Grand Chess Guild emerged from a reading room in the autumn of 1891, when fourteen members of a private club decided that the casual chess played after dinner was insufficient for players of genuine seriousness. Their founding charter — handwritten in precise copperplate — declared that the Guild existed to play chess with "the gravity it deserves and the joy it inspires."

Through two world wars, the Guild continued to meet. Members have played correspondence chess from the trenches, hosted refugee players fleeing occupied Europe, and emerged from every period of history with their commitment to the game intact. That continuity gives us our character.

We have produced three national amateur champions. Our junior programme, launched in 1978, has guided hundreds of young players into serious competitive chess. Two of our past youth members hold international master titles today.
