How to Commission

The Commission Journey

A TAKE commission is not an order form — it is the beginning of a relationship between your vision, Master Yamamoto's craft, and the character of the bamboo itself. We ask that you come to this process with openness, knowing that the material may suggest possibilities you had not considered.

We accept a maximum of twelve commissions per year. This limitation is not scarcity — it is the honest capacity of a studio that will not compromise on attention. Each commission receives Master Yamamoto's personal involvement from initial consultation to final inspection.

  1. 01
    Initial Enquiry Complete the form below. We respond within five working days with a questionnaire and initial thoughts on feasibility.
  2. 02
    Consultation A video or in-person consultation with Master Yamamoto to discuss the piece — its purpose, scale, material, and character.
  3. 03
    Design & Proposal We prepare a written proposal with sketches, material specification, timeline, and price. No obligation at this stage.
  4. 04
    Confirmation & Deposit On approval, we issue a commission agreement. A 40% deposit confirms your place in the production schedule.
  5. 05
    Making & Delivery The piece is made over the agreed timeline. You receive progress photographs at key stages. Final balance on completion.

Your enquiry is treated with complete confidentiality. We do not share client information. We respond to all enquiries within five Japanese working days.

Common Questions

What Collectors Ask

How long does a commission take?

The minimum timeline is eight weeks for a simple tea utensil set. A complex woven basket requires twelve to sixteen weeks. Furniture commissions range from four to six months. Timeline includes harvesting if specific bamboo species must be sourced seasonally.

What is the minimum commission value?

Commission works begin at ¥120,000 (approximately EUR 760 / USD 800 at current rates). This reflects the minimum meaningful unit of Master Yamamoto's time and attention. Stock pieces available for immediate purchase begin at lower price points.

Can I visit the workshop during making?

Yes, by appointment. We welcome commission clients to the Higashiyama atelier at one or two points during the making process. These visits are not tours — they are working conversations, and many clients find them as meaningful as receiving the finished piece.

Do you ship internationally?

Yes. Every piece is packed in museum-grade acid-free tissue and a paulownia wood box, then crated for international freight. We work with specialist art shippers who have experience with organic material customs requirements. We have shipped to the UK, US, France, Australia, and Singapore.

How involved will I be in the design?

As involved as you wish to be. Some clients have very specific dimensions and pattern requirements; others say "I trust you completely." Both are valid starting points. What we ask in all cases is that you trust the bamboo to have input — the material sometimes insists on adjustments that make the final piece better than the original specification.

What if I am not satisfied with the result?

In twenty years and over five hundred commissions, this has not happened in a way that could not be resolved through conversation. If a finished piece genuinely diverges from the agreed specification without our prior communication, we make it right. The commission agreement details the terms of this guarantee.

Can bamboo furniture be used outdoors?

Outdoor-specific pieces, made from heat-treated Moso bamboo, are available for commission. Standard indoor pieces should not be exposed to direct weather. All bamboo benefits from occasional re-oiling with camellia oil — we include a small bottle and instructions with each piece.

Is there a waiting list?

We accept twelve commissions per year. At any given time there is typically a four to eight week queue before a new commission can enter the production schedule. We recommend enquiring as early as possible, particularly for pieces with fixed delivery requirements such as gifts or installation deadlines.

Visit the Atelier

Come to Higashiyama

The TAKE atelier occupies a restored Meiji-era machiya townhouse on a stone-paved lane in Higashiyama, Kyoto's most storied artisan district. The studio is open for visits on Saturdays, and at any time by appointment for commission clients.

From the atelier, you can walk to the Yasaka Shrine in twelve minutes, or north to the Chion-in temple in seven. The Nineizaka stone steps, with their traditional craft shops, are directly outside our gate. There is nowhere else in Japan quite like it.
