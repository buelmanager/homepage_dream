Our Method

The Art of
Cultivation

Every orchid in our collection is the product of decades of accumulated knowledge, precise environmental control, and unwavering patience.

Our five-stage cultivation protocol has been refined over 70 years and is now referenced by botanical institutions and horticultural societies worldwide.

I
Ethical Acquisition
Ongoing

Every specimen enters our collection through verified ethical channels. We work exclusively with certified nurseries, conservation programmes, and licensed importers who share our commitment to protecting wild orchid populations.

CITES documentation is verified for all applicable species, and we maintain relationships with conservation organisations to support sustainable sourcing.

All wild-collected specimens require provenance documentation and government permits.
II
Quarantine
60 Days

New arrivals are placed in our dedicated quarantine greenhouse for a minimum of 60 days. During this period, each specimen is monitored daily for signs of disease, pest activity, or environmental stress.

This protocol protects our existing collection and ensures that every new plant is in optimal health before integration.

Temperature, humidity, and light are adjusted to simulate each specimen's native environment.
III
Acclimatisation
3–6 Months

Plants transitioning from their native habitat or a nursery environment require a careful acclimatisation period. We gradually adjust conditions to match our greenhouse microclimates over three to six months.

Substrate, water chemistry, and fertilisation regimes are calibrated based on each species' natural growing conditions and seasonal rhythms.

Detailed care journals are maintained for each specimen throughout acclimatisation.
IV
Cultivation
Year-Round

Established specimens receive year-round professional care within one of our five microclimate zones, each calibrated to specific genus requirements. LED spectral lighting, automated misting systems, and precision temperature control ensure optimal growing conditions.

Members are involved in the cultivation process through weekly greenhouse access, hands-on workshops, and mentored cultivation of their own assigned specimens.

Environmental data is logged every 15 minutes across all greenhouse zones.
V
Exhibition
Annual

The culmination of our cultivation programme is participation in botanical exhibitions and competitions. Our specimens compete at the Royal Horticultural Society, the World Orchid Conference, and our own Annual Orchidea Exhibition.

Award-winning specimens are documented, propagated where possible, and their cultivation protocols shared with the broader botanical community.

28 international awards have been awarded to Orchidea specimens since 1967.
