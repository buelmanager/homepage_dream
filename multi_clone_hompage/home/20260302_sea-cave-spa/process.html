The Journey

What Happens
to the Body

I

Arrival & Transition

You arrive by boat or cliff path. Shoes remain above. The cave entrance is low — a deliberate architectural choice that requires a bow of the head, a physical act of surrender. Below, the temperature drops 8 degrees in twelve steps.

II

The Cold Confrontation

The 14°C tidal pool is not a punishment — it is a reset. The cold forces the sympathetic nervous system to engage fully, then stand down. Heart rate spikes, then normalises. The body rediscovers its own regulatory intelligence within three minutes.

III

Thermal Immersion

The deep pool at 38°C induces vasodilation — blood vessels open, muscle tension releases, the parasympathetic system takes over. The high magnesium content of the geothermal water is absorbed transdermally, reducing cortisol markers by up to 27% in 60-minute sessions.

IV

Resonance

The final chamber is the deepest point. The basalt walls amplify low-frequency sound. At 64Hz, the resonance corresponds to Delta brainwave states associated with deep rest and cellular repair. Guests spend between 20 minutes and two hours here. Time, in the usual sense, stops.

V

Return

Ascent back to the surface is slow by design. The steps widen. The light increases gradually. Guests emerge on the cliff terrace above the Caldera — the same world as before, but perceived differently. Most describe the sensation as arriving somewhere new while standing in a familiar place.
