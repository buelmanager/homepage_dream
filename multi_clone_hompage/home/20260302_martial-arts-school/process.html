The Path

THE
METHOD

A structured five-stage progression from physical fundamentals to philosophical mastery. No shortcuts. No exceptions.

Five Stages

THE TRAINING
PATH

01

FUNDAMENTALS

Before any technique can be taught, the body must be prepared. Stage one covers stance, balance, breakfalls, breathing patterns, and the etiquette of the dojo. The bow, the uniform, the silence — these are not formalities but foundations.

Duration: First 4-6 weeks. Assessment: Supervised by head instructor before progression.

02

KATA

Pre-arranged forms encode the technical wisdom of each discipline. Students practise kata with increasing precision, learning not just the movement but its purpose. A kata is not a performance — it is a compressed fighting manual.

Duration: Ongoing throughout all stages. New kata introduced at each grading level.

03

SPARRING

Controlled contact tests the kata under dynamic pressure. Randori in judo, jigeiko in kendo, and free-sparring in taekwondo are introduced progressively. Contact is calibrated to the student's level — never reckless, always instructive.

Duration: Introduced after fundamentals are confirmed. Intensity scales with grading.

04

PHILOSOPHY

Technical mastery without philosophical grounding is merely aggression with discipline. Weekly philosophy seminars introduce Bushido, Sun Tzu's Art of War, Musashi's Book of Five Rings, and the writings of Jigoro Kano. These texts are not historical curiosities — they are living instruction.

Duration: Saturday seminars, 2 hours. Open from intermediate level onwards.

05

GRADING

Examination at Bushido is not a performance for an audience. It is a test of character conducted before the sensei who has observed you for months. Technical execution accounts for sixty percent of the grade. Composure, attitude, and respect account for the rest.

Schedule: Grading held quarterly. Students are invited to grade by their instructor — not self-nominated.

Teaching Philosophy

THREE
PRINCIPLES

I

NO SHORTCUTS

There is no accelerated path. The curriculum takes the time it takes. Students who ask how quickly they can reach black belt are missing the point of the journey.

II

TEACH BY DOING

Instructors at Bushido do not just teach — they continue to train. A sensei who no longer practises cannot teach from lived experience, and lived experience is the only source of genuine instruction.

III

THE WHOLE STUDENT

Physical technique, philosophical depth, and ethical character are developed together. A practitioner who excels technically but fails ethically has not succeeded — they have failed in the most important dimension of all.
