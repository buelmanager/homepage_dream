Our Story

The Living
Bakery

Est. 1994

"A loaf of bread is a conversation between grain, water, time, and the hands that shape it — a dialogue that has been ongoing for ten thousand years."

— Marcel Dubois, Founder

A Jar of Living Culture

In 1994, Marcel Dubois left a three-star restaurant kitchen in Lyon with a small jar of sourdough starter — a culture that had been kept alive for decades by a retired boulanger named Henri Caron. Marcel was 26 years old and obsessed with the idea that bread could be more than a vehicle for butter.

1994

Paris, Rue du Faubourg

Marcel opened the first LEVAIN atelier in a converted workshop on Rue du Faubourg Saint-Antoine. The space held a single deck oven, a stone mill, and 12 seats. Word spread through the neighbourhood within weeks — the crust sang, the crumb wept, the sourness was polite but present.

1998

The School Is Born

Customers kept asking: "How do you make this?" LEVAIN launched its first bread-making masterclass with 8 students in a room that smelled of fermentation and sawdust. The waiting list never cleared. The school was born from necessity and became the heart of everything we do.

2007

The Grain Revolution

Élise Moreau joined as Heritage Grain Specialist and transformed our sourcing entirely. We partnered with six farms across France and Scotland to grow ancient varieties — einkorn, emmer, rouge de Bordeaux — and established a stone-mill on the premises. No more bags from the commodity market.

The Starter

Meet Henri —
Our Living Culture

We named our starter "Henri" after the boulanger who first entrusted it to Marcel. Every morning before the city wakes, a baker feeds Henri a precise measure of freshly milled flour and filtered spring water at exactly 26°C.

Henri has lived through three bakehouses, two floods, and a global pandemic. He is kept at constant temperature, never refrigerated, and visited more frequently than most family members.

⏱

48-Hour Cold Fermentation

Every shaped loaf rests in a cold retarder for a minimum of 48 hours. This slow fermentation develops complex acids, improves digestibility, and deepens flavour in ways no mechanical process can replicate.

🌾

Single-Origin Heritage Grain

We know the name of every farm and every farmer behind our grain. Heritage varieties contain more complex proteins, resist environmental stress, and produce bread with character that modern hybrid wheat cannot.

🔥

Stone-Deck Baking

Loaves are baked directly on ancient volcanic stone at 260°C with steam injection in the first minutes. The result is a crust that shatters like porcelain and a crumb that breathes.

The Grain

Ancient Varieties,
Modern Purpose

We source six heritage varieties across France and Scotland. Each grain tells a story of landscape, climate, and agricultural tradition that disappeared from commercial baking fifty years ago.

⬡

Einkorn

Burgundy, France

The oldest cultivated wheat, unchanged for 10,000 years. Small kernel, low gluten, high carotenoid content. Produces a golden crumb with a nutty, almost buttery flavour.

⬡

Rouge de Bordeaux

Aquitaine, France

A 19th-century French heritage variety prized by artisan bakers. Rich in protein, produces excellent gluten structure and a distinctive wheaty depth of flavour.

⬡

Emmer Farro

Normandy, France

An ancient wheat prized in Roman kitchens. Dense, mineral-rich, and slow to digest. Our emmer loaves have a dense, chewy texture and an earthy, complex taste.

⬡

Scottish Bere

Orkney, Scotland

A 6-row barley grown in Orkney for over 5,000 years. Imparts a deep, slightly bitter, almost malty quality. Used in our seasonal dark loaves and crackers.

⬡

Spelt

Alsace, France

An ancient wheat with a fragile husk that protects it from pests without pesticides. Higher in fibre and easier to digest than modern wheat. Light, nutty, and wholesome.

⬡

Khorasan (Kamut)

Provence, France

A large, golden-kernelled ancient wheat with a rich, buttery flavour. High in selenium and zinc. Our Khorasan loaves are a gentle luxury — golden inside and out.

The Team

The Hands
Behind the Bread

Our bakers are selected not only for skill but for devotion. To bake here is to accept that the bread comes first — before sleep, before convenience, before anything.

Learn with them →
Marcel Dubois
Founder & Head Baker
Marcel spent six years in the kitchens of three-star restaurants before deciding that bread was the only food worth dedicating a life to. His obsession with fermentation science has led him to correspond with microbiologists at INRAE, consult for artisan bakeries across Europe, and write a self-published manifesto on wild yeast entitled "The Living Dough." He bakes every Saturday and refuses to take holidays in August.
Élise Moreau
Heritage Grain Specialist
Élise studied agronomy at AgroParisTech before a chance encounter with a Périgord wheat farmer redirected her entirely. She spent two years working on farms before arriving at LEVAIN in 2015. She manages all grain relationships, develops seasonal recipes around harvest windows, and runs our most popular workshop: "Grain to Loaf." She grows einkorn in three terracotta pots on her kitchen windowsill.
Kenji Tanaka
Scoring & Technique Master
Kenji trained in Kyoto as a ceramicist before moving to Paris and discovering sourdough — an intersection of patience, heat, and the unexpected that felt entirely familiar. His scoring patterns are architectural in their precision; several have been photographed for food publications. He teaches the Art of Scoring masterclass and is working on a book about the geometry of crust.
