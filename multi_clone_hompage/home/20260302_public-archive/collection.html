Featured Collection

Medieval — 1086

The Domesday Survey

Commissioned by William I in 1085, the Domesday Book is one of the earliest comprehensive surveys of landholding in England. Its two volumes — Great Domesday and Little Domesday — document over thirteen thousand settlements and remain essential to understanding feudal society.

2 Volumes

By Era

1086 – 1485

Medieval Records

Royal charters, pipe rolls, plea rolls, and the earliest judicial records of the English Crown. Includes the Domesday Book and a thousand years of chancery proceedings.

1.4 million items catalogued

1485 – 1714

Early Modern State Papers

Foreign and domestic state papers of the Tudor and Stuart monarchs, including the letters of Henry VIII, Elizabeth I, and Cromwell's Commonwealth. Diplomatic dispatches and privy council minutes.

2.1 million items catalogued

1714 – 1945

Modern Government Records

Cabinet papers, census returns, military records from two world wars, Colonial Office files, and the comprehensive bureaucratic record of the British state during its most expansive era.

5.2 million items catalogued

1945 – Present

Contemporary Holdings

Post-war cabinet papers, declassified intelligence files, welfare state records, and materials tracing Britain's evolving relationship with Europe and the Commonwealth. Thirty-year rule releases.

1.9 million items catalogued

Digital Collection

Born-Digital Archive

Government emails, digital records, websites, and datasets transferred to the Archive since 2000. Represents the fastest-growing part of our holdings, requiring new preservation methodologies.

420,000 digital objects preserved

Special Collections

Maps & Visual Records

Over 100,000 maps, charts, plans, and architectural drawings spanning five centuries. Includes Ordnance Survey originals, county maps, and military cartography from Waterloo to the Falklands.

100,000+ cartographic items
