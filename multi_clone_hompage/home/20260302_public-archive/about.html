Founded 1838

The Nation's Memory

The National Archive was founded by Act of Parliament in 1838 — a decisive moment in the evolution of democratic government. For the first time, the nation committed itself to the systematic preservation of public records and their eventual disclosure to citizens. The founding Keeper, Sir Francis Palgrave, established the principles that still guide us: rigorous appraisal, careful preservation, and generous public access.

Over 186 years, we have grown from a single building housing a few hundred thousand documents to a institution holding 11 million items spanning 900 years of national history. The Domesday Book of 1086, the log of the Mayflower, Cabinet minutes from the Second World War, and last year's government email chains all sit in our care.

We have never wavered from the conviction that a free and functioning democracy requires transparent, permanent, public records. The archive is not a bureaucratic necessity — it is a cornerstone of civilised government.

Our Charter

What We Stand For

I

Permanence

Records in our care are preserved permanently. We hold them in trust for all future generations — not for the next five years, but for the next five centuries.

II

Integrity

We guarantee the authenticity and integrity of every document in our care. The record is the record — unaltered, uncensored, and preserved exactly as created.

III

Freedom of Access

Citizens have the right to consult public records. Access to our reading rooms and online catalogue is free to all. Knowledge is not a privilege.

IV

Independence

We are independent of government. No minister may withhold, alter, or suppress records in our custody. Our independence is the guarantee of the record's trustworthiness.
