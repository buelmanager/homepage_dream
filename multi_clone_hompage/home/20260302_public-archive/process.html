The Archival Pipeline

I

Appraisal

Records are assessed for long-term historical, evidential, or administrative value. Only approximately five per cent of government records meet selection criteria for permanent preservation.

II

Transfer

Selected records are formally transferred from government departments after twenty to thirty years, following the Public Records Act. Provenance and chain of custody are rigorously documented throughout.

III

Conservation

Physical records are assessed for damage, cleaned, deacidified, and stored in climate-controlled vaults. Fragile items receive specialist conservation treatment before any access is permitted.

IV

Digitisation

High-priority documents are digitised at archival resolution using calibrated scanners. Metadata is attached, images are quality-checked, and surrogates stored on redundant servers with offsite backups.

V

Access

Catalogued records become available to registered researchers in our reading rooms and, where digitised, via the online catalogue. Sensitive material may remain closed under exemptions in the Freedom of Information Act.

Conservation
Laboratory

The Archive's conservation laboratory employs fourteen specialist conservators working with parchment, paper, vellum, maps, photographs, and digital media. Using techniques refined over nearly two centuries, our scientists extend the life of fragile documents by decades or centuries.

Environmental monitoring maintains constant temperature (18°C) and relative humidity (45–55%) in all document stores. Light-sensitive materials are kept in total darkness and viewed only under conservation-safe lighting.

  • Paper deacidification and alkaline buffering
  • Parchment humidification and flattening
  • Iron gall ink stabilisation
  • Encapsulation and housing fabrication
  • Digital media migration and format normalisation
  • Disaster recovery and emergency treatment

Digital
Preservation

The Archive operates one of the largest archival digitisation programmes in Europe. Our in-house digitisation studio processes over 800,000 document images per year, with external partnerships extending this capacity further.

All digitised records are stored in the Archive's Trusted Digital Repository, which meets ISO 16363 standards for digital preservation systems. Files are held in multiple formats, with checksums verified quarterly.

45K daily online users
ISO 16363 certified repository

Research Access

Online

Online Catalogue

Free access to the complete online catalogue, which describes every item held by the Archive. Many documents are available as high-resolution digital images at no charge.

  • Full catalogue search and browse
  • Free digital images where available
  • Document ordering for reading room visits
  • Personal research account and saved searches
Reading Room

Reader's Ticket

A Reader's Ticket is required to access original documents in our reading rooms. Registration is free, requires identity verification, and may be completed online or in person.

  • Access to all reading rooms at Archive House
  • Same-day document delivery from the store
  • Use of specialist reference collections
  • Supervised handling of fragile originals
Scholarly

Research Fellowship

The Archive's annual fellowship programme supports independent scholars undertaking sustained research in our collections. Fellows receive dedicated workspace, extended access hours, and curatorial support.

  • Private study carrels for the fellowship year
  • Extended access to closed materials
  • Conservation laboratory observation visits
  • Publication support and academic network

Conservation Specialists

Dr. James Hargreaves

Head of Conservation

A Fellow of the Institute for Conservation, Dr. Hargreaves leads the Archive's conservation laboratory and chairs the national advisory group on paper preservation in public collections.

Dr. Mei Lin

Digital Preservation Lead

Dr. Lin oversees the Archive's Trusted Digital Repository and leads international research into the long-term preservation of born-digital government records and complex digital objects.

Thomas Ashby

Senior Parchment Conservator

Thomas specialises in the treatment of medieval parchment and vellum manuscripts. He developed the Archive's iron gall ink stabilisation protocol, now adopted by eight national archives internationally.
