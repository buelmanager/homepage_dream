The National Archive is open to all registered researchers and members of the public. Reading rooms operate on a booking system. We recommend registering for a Reader's Ticket before your first visit to ensure immediate access to original documents.

Getting a Reader's Ticket

1

Register Online

Complete the online registration form with your personal details and confirm your email address. Registration is free and takes approximately five minutes.

2

Verify Identity

Bring one form of photographic identification on your first visit, or upload a scanned copy as part of online registration. Passports, driving licences, and national identity cards are all accepted.

3

Book a Seat

Reserve a reading room seat in advance through the online booking system. Seats can be booked up to six weeks ahead. Same-day booking is sometimes available at quieter times.

4

Order Documents

Order specific documents from the online catalogue before your visit. Documents are retrieved from the store and delivered to your reading room desk within two hours of your arrival.

Frequently
Asked

  • Do I need to book in advance?

    Reading room seats must be booked in advance through the online booking system. If you wish to view original documents, you should also order them before arrival — documents are retrieved from secure storage and typically arrive at your desk within two hours.

  • What identification is accepted for a Reader's Ticket?

    We accept any government-issued photographic identification: passport, driving licence, or national identity card. For postal and online applications, a scanned copy is sufficient.

  • Can I photograph documents?

    Personal photography is permitted in most reading rooms using a handheld camera or smartphone, provided no flash is used and the document is not damaged by handling. Photography is not permitted for closed or conservation-sensitive records.

  • Are records available online?

    A growing proportion of our holdings is available as free digital images via the online catalogue. We currently provide access to over three million digitised document images. Documents not yet digitised can be ordered for reading room consultation.

  • What if the record I need is closed?

    Records that remain sensitive may be closed under exemptions in the Freedom of Information Act 2000. You may apply for the early opening of a closed record or make a Subject Access Request if you believe you have a legal right to see it. Our advisory team can guide you through the process.
