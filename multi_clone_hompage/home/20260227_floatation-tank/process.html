What to Expect

A Journey Into the Self

A float at ZERO is not simply a wellness treatment — it is a structured encounter with your own consciousness. We have designed every moment, from the second you step through our doors to the hour after you emerge from the pod, as part of a single continuous experience.

Most people arrive with some anticipation and a few questions. By the time they leave, they have found something they did not expect — a quality of quiet that is not empty but full, not absence but presence.

Here is what happens, step by step.

The Five Stages

Your Float, In Detail

I
Before You Enter

Arrival & Preparation

You arrive at ZERO. You are welcomed by a float guide — not a receptionist, but a trained practitioner who has floated hundreds of times. They know what questions you have before you ask them. You are offered herbal tea. There is no hurry. First-time floaters receive a 15-minute orientation: what to expect, how to lie in the pod, how to breathe, and crucially — how to resist nothing. Experienced floaters are shown directly to their private room.

Your suite contains everything you need: a private shower, shampoo and body wash, earplugs, a neck float cushion, and towels. You shower before entering the pod. This removes oils and products that could affect the water clarity and — importantly — begins the ritual of shedding the outside world.

II
The First Contact

Entering the Pod

You step into the pod. The water is 34.5°C — skin-receptor neutral temperature. Your body cannot distinguish it from air. You lie back. The salt solution takes your weight completely. You find you are floating without any effort at all. This moment — the first discovery of effortless buoyancy — is one that almost all floaters describe as memorable.

You reach up and close the lid. Or you leave it slightly ajar for the first few minutes. There is no wrong way. First-time floaters often use the optional interior light. After 10 minutes, most switch it off on their own. Darkness comes not as a threat but as a relief.

III
The Core Experience

The Float

This is the territory we cannot fully describe, because it is different for every person and every session. In the first 20 minutes, most floaters experience restlessness — the mind, suddenly deprived of input, generates its own. Thoughts race. To-do lists appear. The body wants to fidget. This is entirely normal. It is, in fact, the beginning of the process.

After roughly 30–40 minutes, something shifts. The mind, finding nothing to grasp, begins to release. Brainwave activity descends toward theta — 4 to 8 Hz. The boundary between body and water becomes unclear. Many floaters lose track of time entirely. Some sleep. Some encounter vivid hypnagogic imagery — the visual half-dreams that occur at the edge of sleep. Some simply experience a quality of silence they have never felt before.

IV
Coming Back

Emergence

Gentle music begins — a signal that your session is complete. The shift from silence to sound is profound. Floaters describe this transition as one of the most beautiful moments of the experience: the return of sensation after its absence makes everything newly vivid. You emerge slowly. There is no rush.

A warm shower awaits. Our amenities include carefully chosen products — unscented, mineral-balanced — that feel extraordinary on skin that has been nourished by an hour or more of magnesium absorption. You dress slowly. Your skin feels different. The air seems more textured. Everything is slightly more itself.

V
The After

Integration

Integration is the stage most studios forget. At ZERO, we consider it essential. You move to our integration lounge — a quiet, low-lit space with deep chairs, herbal teas, and notebooks. You are not hurried. Your float guide checks in briefly — not intrusively, but to offer a space for whatever you want to share or explore.

Many floaters write in our provided journals. Some sketch. Some simply sit. The post-float state — characterized by reduced cognitive load, heightened sensory awareness, and unusual emotional openness — can last for hours. We send you back into your life when you are ready, with a brief note about what you might notice in the coming days.

The Neuroscience

What Happens Inside Your Brain

β
Beta — Entry
13–30 Hz

Your normal waking state when you enter the pod. Active thinking, problem-solving, external focus. The mind is busy, alert, managing.

α
Alpha — Release
8–12 Hz

The transition state. Relaxation deepens. The internal monologue quiets. A feeling of calm without drowsiness. Often reached within 20 minutes of entering the pod.

θ
Theta — The Float State
4–8 Hz

The signature state of deep float. Associated with creativity, insight, hypnagogic imagery, and the dissolution of ego boundaries. This is where the breakthroughs happen.

δ
Delta — Deep Sleep
0.5–4 Hz

Some experienced floaters reach delta states while remaining conscious — an extraordinarily rare phenomenon normally associated only with advanced meditators. About 12% of our regular clients report this.

Before You Arrive

Preparation Guide

These simple steps will help you get the most from your float.

  • ◎Avoid caffeine for 2–3 hours before floating
  • ◎Eat a light meal — not too full, not empty
  • ◎Do not shave for at least 24 hours (salt will sting)
  • ◎Remove contact lenses before arriving
  • ◎Wash your hair beforehand if preferred (you will shower again after)
  • ◎Arrive 10–15 minutes early for your first session
  • ◎Wear something comfortable and loose for after
  • ◎Leave a clear 30–60 minutes after your session for integration
Health Considerations

When to Contact Us First

Floating is safe for most people, but we ask that you contact us before booking if:

  • —You have epilepsy or a history of seizures
  • —You have open wounds, active skin infections, or recent surgery
  • —You are pregnant (we may recommend a later date)
  • —You are taking medications that affect consciousness or balance
  • —You have a serious psychological condition (we are not a clinical treatment)
  • —You have dyed your hair in the past two weeks
  • —You have hypersensitivity to magnesium
