Training Pathway

The Artisan Bridge Program

Our program is built on a single conviction: that craft expertise deserves rigorous support, not charity. Every step is designed to move artisans toward genuine independence through skill, market access, and community.

Five Steps to Independence

01

Artisan Referral and Welcome

We receive referrals from settlement support organizations and community networks. Every new artisan receives an interpretation-supported welcome conversation to understand their background, craft heritage, and goals.

02

Skills Portfolio and Tradition Mapping

Working with a craft specialist and cultural mediator, we document each artisan's technique heritage — which traditions they carry, how deeply, and what training would best serve them and the craft.

03

Placement in a Craft Discipline Stream

Artisans join one of five streams: textile, embroidery, ceramics, woodcraft, or mixed media. Training is studio-based and led by master artisans. Duration is six to twelve months depending on the discipline.

04

Business and Market Skills

Alongside craft practice, artisans learn photography, pricing, online selling, and client communication — in multiple languages, at their own pace. We provide devices, connectivity, and materials at no cost.

05

Collective Marketplace and Transition to Independence

Graduates join our collective marketplace and receive continued mentorship. Most transition to fully independent practice within twelve months. Graduates who wish to become trainers are supported into that role.

Program Resources

Free to Artisans

All training, materials, tools, devices, and interpretation support are provided completely free. There are no fees or repayment obligations at any stage.

Multilingual Support

We currently provide interpretation and written materials in Arabic, Somali, Dari, Tigrinya, Kurdish, and Haitian Creole. Other languages on request.

Accredited Certificates

Program completion certificates are recognised by partner employers and the national craft accreditation body, supporting employment and further training pathways.

Refer an Artisan

If you know someone with traditional craft skills who would benefit from our program, we welcome referrals from settlement support workers, community networks, and individuals.
