Our Mission

About Artisan Bridge

Who We Are

Artisan Bridge is a refugee craft skills program that believes traditional handcraft is both cultural heritage and economic future — a living skill that deserves to be taught, preserved, and honored.

We work with displaced artisans who carry deep craft knowledge — weavers, embroiderers, potters, woodcarvers — and provide structured training, business mentorship, and market access that allows them to build sustainable livelihoods in their communities of settlement.

Our work is not charity. It is a recognition that master artisans have expertise that the world urgently needs — and that displacement should not be the end of a craft lineage.

Founding Story

Born From a Church Hall Workshop

In 2016, social worker Amara Diallo noticed that several of the newly arrived families she supported were skilled artisans — a Syrian weaver, a Somali embroiderer, a Kurdish woodcarver — whose expertise was entirely invisible to the settlement support system around them.

With two donated sewing machines, a borrowed loom, and a church hall on Tuesday evenings, Amara brought nine artisans together not for therapy or language class, but to make things. The relief in the room was immediate and unmistakable.

Nine years later, Artisan Bridge has trained 340 artisans from 14 countries across three cities, and Amara serves as the organization's founding director, still attending every first workshop session of every new cohort.

Core Values

What We Stand For

01

Craft as Dignity

We treat artisans as masters of a discipline, not beneficiaries of a service. The relationship is one of peer respect — we provide infrastructure; they provide irreplaceable expertise.

02

Cultural Preservation

Every craft tradition we document and teach is a strand of human heritage that might otherwise be lost. We take that responsibility seriously — archiving techniques with the same care as any museum.

03

Economic Independence

Our goal is not dependency but departure — artisans who leave our program with skills, markets, and confidence to sustain themselves fully and independently.
