Welfare Above All

Horse Care
Philosophy

At Gallop & Grace, the welfare of our horses is not a policy — it is a conviction. Every decision regarding feeding, work schedules, turnout, and veterinary intervention is made with the horse's physical and psychological wellbeing as the primary consideration.

We employ four full-time equine veterinarians, two physiotherapists, and a dedicated equine nutritionist on permanent retainer. Our horses are never overworked, never kept in isolation, and never asked to perform beyond their physical capacity.

Daily Turnout

Minimum 6 hours daily in individual or compatible group paddocks. Natural movement is non-negotiable.

Individual Nutrition

Bespoke dietary programmes designed by our equine nutritionist, adjusted seasonally and with age.

Veterinary Care

Bi-annual health assessments, dental care, and on-site diagnostic imaging including MRI and endoscopy.

Mental Enrichment

Social grouping, environmental enrichment, and low-stress handling protocols developed with an equine behaviourist.

Selective Genetics

Breeding Programme

Our breeding programme is guided by a single ambition: to produce horses of exceptional character, trainability, and longevity — horses that will define the estate's next generation.

I

Stallion Selection

We partner with Europe's foremost stallion stations to select sires combining proven athletic performance with documented temperament genetics. Bloodline diversity is maintained across all four breed programmes.

II

Mare Development

Our broodmares are never merely "retired" horses — each is selected for specific genetic traits. Young mares complete a four-year ridden assessment before entering the breeding programme.

III

Foal Handling

A structured imprinting and socialisation programme begins within 24 hours of birth, guided by our equine behaviourist. Every foal is handled daily and exposed to gradual environmental complexity.

IV

Young Horse Education

The backing process begins at age 3 with a full season of groundwork and lunging. Ridden work follows a slow, progressive timeline — never rushed, always building genuine confidence.
