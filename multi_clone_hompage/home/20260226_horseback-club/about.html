Our Heritage

Seven Decades of Excellence

A History Written in Hoofbeats

From a single paddock in post-war England to 340 acres of equestrian grandeur, the story of Gallop & Grace is one of aristocratic vision and enduring love for the horse.

Foundation
Lord Edward Ashworth establishes the original stables on 40 acres of Surrey farmland with 8 thoroughbreds from his family's Irish stud.
1952
1968
Expansion & First Championships
The estate expands to 150 acres. Gallop & Grace members claim first national dressage titles under the training of Henri Dubois, brought from France.
Grand Stables Construction
The iconic heated stable block — now housing 85 horses — is built by master craftsmen using traditional English timber-frame methods and locally quarried stone.
1981
1997
Andalusian Breeding Program
Partnership with Yeguada de Cartuja establishes the Andalusian breeding line, bringing the pure Iberian tradition to English soil for the first time at this scale.
Conservation Estate Designation
Gallop & Grace receives Heritage Estate status. The woodlands, meadows, and waterways are certified as protected habitat — rewilding 120 acres in perpetuity.
2010
2024
340 Acres, 85 Horses, New Era
The estate reaches its current scale. Three FEI-standard arenas, 40 miles of private trails, and a full equine veterinary suite mark the beginning of a new chapter.

Head of Training

James Ashworth,
Master of the Saddle

Born into the third generation of the founding Ashworth family, James represents both a living legacy and a forward-thinking evolution of classical equestrian philosophy. Trained under the late Henri Dubois and later at the Spanish Riding School in Vienna, James returned to Surrey with a rare understanding of both classical European dressage and modern performance science.

His approach is unhurried, precise, and deeply empathetic — guided by the belief that the finest riding emerges from a partnership of mutual trust between horse and rider, not from dominance or mechanical repetition.

"A horse will show you everything it is capable of — the moment you stop asking and start listening." — James Ashworth

Our Ethos

Horse Breeding Philosophy

◆

Selective Excellence

Every horse at Gallop & Grace is selected or bred for specific virtues: temperament above all, followed by conformation, movement quality, and lineage. We do not purchase volume — we cultivate character.

◆

Welfare First

Our horses live with access to daily turnout, individual nutritional programmes designed by equine dieticians, and psychological enrichment. Work schedules are calibrated to each animal's physical and mental state.

◆

Heritage Bloodlines

Our breeding programme preserves and extends rare equine bloodlines — Lusitano, Andalusian, and old Irish Thoroughbred lines — that represent centuries of careful selection by European nobility.

Stewardship

Conservation
Mission

In 2010, Gallop & Grace committed 120 acres of its estate to active ecological restoration. Ancient hedgerows were replanted, chalk streams cleared of silt, and native wildflower meadows re-seeded across former paddock land no longer needed for riding.

We partner with the Surrey Wildlife Trust and the Rare Breeds Survival Trust to monitor species recovery and maintain our Heritage Estate certification. Barn owls, water voles, and native pollinators have returned in measurable numbers since the rewilding programme began.

We believe that stewardship of land and horse are inseparable. To ride at Gallop & Grace is to participate in a centuries-old covenant with the English countryside.
