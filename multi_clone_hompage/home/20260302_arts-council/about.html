Our Institution

The Voice of the Arts in Public Life

For 79 years, the National Arts Council has stood as the principal advocate and funder for the arts — ensuring that creativity is treated as a public good, not a private luxury.

Born from a Conviction

The National Arts Council was established in 1946, in the belief that a society recovering from war needed art as much as it needed bread. The founder's vision was simple: the arts should be available to everyone, everywhere.

That conviction remains the foundation of everything we do. We distribute public funds to artists and organisations without fear or favour, guided only by artistic quality, public benefit and the imperative of access.

Today we fund 4,800 organisations and 21,000 individual artists across visual arts, theatre, music, dance, literature, film and beyond — from international institutions to community theatre groups in small towns.

Our Principles

What We Stand For

Artistic Excellence

We fund work that is bold, rigorous and distinctive. Excellence is not about tradition — it is about ambition and integrity at every scale.

Radical Inclusion

We measure our success by who is in the audience, who is on the stage, and who is in the boardroom — not just who is receiving grants.

Cultural Ecology

We invest across the whole ecosystem — from experimental small-scale work that seeds tomorrow's culture to the flagship institutions that anchor it.

Transparent Accountability

Every penny of public funding is accounted for. Our annual reports, assessment criteria and decisions are published in full — no exceptions.
