The Glass Path

How We Teach
at Ignis

Our curriculum moves from safety and heat literacy through physical mastery of the gather, formal discipline, colour knowledge and finally the development of personal artistic voice.

"Glass will not be deceived. It knows what temperature it is, what state it is in, what it can still do and what it can no longer do. The glassblower's only task is to listen." — Marco Veltri, Founder
01
Days 1–3

Safety & the Language of Heat

The hot shop is a demanding environment requiring constant situational awareness. All students begin with an intensive safety orientation — learning to move in the space, read glass temperature by colour (from black-heat through yellow-white), understand burn protocols and build the studio habits that prevent incidents.

02
Week 1

First Gather: Contact with Molten Glass

Students make their first gathers from the furnace — experiencing for the first time the weight, the heat, the responsiveness and the urgency of a material that exists at 1,100°C. The blowpipe becomes an extension of the body; learning where your centre of gravity is becomes the first real lesson.

03
Weeks 2–4

Form: Cylinder, Sphere & Open Vessel

The three foundational forms of blown glass. Consistent symmetry is the measure of technical readiness — a cylinder that pulls true, a sphere that holds its centre, a vessel that opens evenly. We practice these until they are reliable before introducing colour or decoration.

04
Weeks 5–7

Colour: Frit, Cane & Murrine

Colour is added through coloured frit (ground glass), drawn cane and prepared murrine — the traditional Venetian millefiori elements that represent centuries of technical refinement. Students learn to apply, control, and design with colour while maintaining the formal discipline of the previous weeks.

05
Weeks 8–9

Signature Work: Personal Studio Project

The final stage is unguided making — two weeks of studio time to produce a series of works that represent the student's emerging artistic voice. These pieces are exhibited in the Ignis annual open studio and form the foundation of the student's professional portfolio.
