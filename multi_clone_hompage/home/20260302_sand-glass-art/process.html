How We Work

The Process

01
The Natural Event
Lightning Strikes
A lightning bolt carries 1 billion volts and strikes at 270,000 km/h. When it hits desert sand, it instantaneously vaporises the material in its path and fuses the surrounding silica into glass. The process takes less than 1/1000th of a second. The result persists for millions of years.
02
Storm Season
We Monitor and Wait
Our meteorological station at the studio tracks electrical storm patterns across the Tassili n'Ajjer plateau. We map potential impact zones and prepare our collection equipment. Then we wait. The desert operates on its own schedule.
03
Post-Storm
Desert Expedition
Within days of a significant storm event, our collection team — led by Mohammed Tineghir — travels to the estimated impact zone. Finding a fulgurite is not simple: they are buried, fragile, and the surface sand quickly obscures the entry point. Mohammed has spent thirty years reading the desert.
04
The Atelier
Study and Classification
Each specimen is cleaned with distilled water and soft brushes. We photograph it at 3× magnification to document the interior glass surface and sand grain inclusions. Karim conducts a full geological assay. Every specimen receives a unique classification code that stays with it for life.
05
Creation
The Work Reveals Itself
Yasmine works with each specimen to determine its destiny. Some become standalone sculptures. Some join others in tray arrangements. Some wait until a commission arrives that matches their character exactly. She has been known to decline commissions because no specimen in the archive was right for the brief.
