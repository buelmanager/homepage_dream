I
Stage One Assessment

We begin with close observation. The broken piece is received, unpacked with care, and placed on a clean surface under directional light. Every fracture is examined — its angle, its depth, the nature of the clay or porcelain at the break. We ask where the piece was made, how old it is, what it has been through. We handle it with the attention of a physician conducting a diagnosis.

The assessment determines the approach — which urushi preparation is appropriate, whether fragments need stabilization before bonding, and which metal will most naturally complement the piece's character. A written assessment is shared with the client before any work begins.

  • All fragments are catalogued and photographed
  • Ceramic body material assessed for urushi compatibility
  • Gold type recommendation provided at this stage
  • Written condition report sent to client before proceeding
Duration 1–2 days
Client Contact Written report provided
II
II
Stage Two Cleaning

Old adhesives are removed with patient chemical treatment. Dust, oxidation, and accumulated debris at the fracture edges are cleared away using cotton swabs, soft brushes, and — where necessary — delicate mechanical means. The cleaning reveals the true surface of the break: the exact topography of the fracture, the grain of the clay, the precise edge that will receive the urushi.

If the piece has been previously repaired with modern adhesives — as many antique and heirloom pieces have been — this stage takes longer. We are patient. Poorly removed adhesive will compromise the final repair. We take whatever time is required.

  • Old adhesives removed without damage to ceramic body
  • Fracture edges cleaned to reveal clean ceramic surface
  • Surface assessed for porosity and urushi absorption rate
  • Any loose particles of ceramic body consolidated
Duration 2–5 days
Tools Used Chemical solvents, bamboo tools
III
III
Stage Three Urushi Bonding

Urushi is the sap of the Japanese lacquer tree, Toxicodendron vernicifluum — harvested by skilled tappers in Japan, Korea, and China with methods unchanged for three thousand years. We mix our urushi compound according to the specific characteristics of each piece: the grain of the ceramic, the width of the fracture, the ambient humidity. The mixture is applied to the edges with a fine brush.

The bonded fragments are then placed in a humidity-controlled curing chamber — a small cabinet in which humidity and temperature are maintained at specific levels to allow the urushi to cure properly. The lacquer does not dry in the conventional sense: it polymerizes through an oxidative process that requires moisture. The cure takes ten to fourteen days.

  • Authentic Japanese urushi lacquer, not synthetic substitute
  • Humidity-controlled curing chamber at 65–75% relative humidity
  • Curing period of 10–14 days minimum
  • Excess urushi removed after initial cure before gold stage
Duration 2–3 weeks
Material Hon-urushi lacquer, Japan
IV
IV
Stage Four Gold Gilding

A thin layer of fresh urushi is brushed onto the cured seam, and then, while still tacky, pure gold powder is applied with a bamboo brush — maedate-fude — a broad, soft instrument that allows the powder to settle without compaction. The gold is worked into every curve and corner of the seam, building up layer by layer until the coverage is complete.

The gold stage is where the transformation becomes visible — the moment when what was a fracture becomes a luminous seam, when damage becomes decoration. For those who commission the full service, we share photographs at this stage. The change is invariably astonishing even to those who expected it.

  • 24k pure gold powder, certified Japanese provenance
  • Traditional maedate-fude bamboo brush application
  • Multiple layers applied for complete, even coverage
  • Photograph documentation sent to client at this stage
Duration 3–7 days
Material 24k gold powder, Japan
V
V
Stage Five Final Polish

Once the gold has fully cured, it is burnished — a gentle, precise process using a small agate stone or specialist polishing tools to bring the surface of the gold to its full depth and brilliance. The burnishing reveals the true luminosity of the gold: what was matte becomes radiant. The seam glows with its own particular light.

The completed piece is then photographed in documentation — full views, detail views of each seam — before being wrapped in acid-free tissue and washi paper for return. A restoration certificate accompanies every piece: the date of receipt, the work performed, the materials used, and the lifetime guarantee of the repair.

  • Agate burnishing to full gold brilliance
  • Final photography documentation of completed repair
  • Acid-free tissue and washi paper wrapping
  • Restoration certificate with lifetime guarantee enclosed
Duration 2–3 days

Our Materials

Only the Authentic

We refuse synthetic substitutes. Every material we use has a provenance, a supply chain we know, and a quality we have verified. The repair is only as good as what you repair with.

漆

Hon-Urushi Lacquer

Kishu & Wajima, Japan

The sap of the Japanese lacquer tree, harvested by hand in the Kishu and Wajima regions. Our urushi is unmodified — traditional composition, traditional preparation. It bonds with the strength of the original firing and cures without brittleness.

金

24k Pure Gold Powder

Kanazawa, Japan

Ground to precise particle sizes in Kanazawa — Japan's historic center of gold leaf production. Our gold powder is certified 99.9% pure, with the soft, warm undertone that distinguishes Japanese gold from Western equivalents.

銀

Fine Silver Powder

Tokyo, Japan

999 fine silver, ground and processed to match the particle specifications of our gold powder. Applied with the same technique, silver brings a cooler, lunar quality to the seam — reflective and restrained.

白

Platinum Powder

Osaka, Japan

The rarest and most precise of our metal powders. Platinum brings a contemporary character to kintsugi — harder, colder, and more architectural than gold or silver. Suited to modern ceramic forms and industrial studio pottery.

赤

Copper Powder

Takaoka, Japan

Warm, autumnal, and deeply organic. Copper seams oxidize gently over time — deepening in color, developing their own patina. A living finish for pieces that age alongside their owners.

紙

Washi Paper

Echizen, Japan

Traditional Japanese paper used in intermediate stages of certain repairs to stabilize fragile fragments before urushi application. Also used as final wrapping for completed pieces — acid-free, archival, protective.

Common Questions

Before You Send

We provide detailed packaging guidance after your commission is confirmed. Generally: wrap each fragment individually in acid-free tissue, pad generously with bubble wrap, and use a rigid outer box. Do not use packing peanuts (they shift). We can arrange insured courier collection for significant pieces.
For significant or valuable pieces, we strongly recommend our insured courier service — we arrange a specialist ceramics courier to collect from your door. All pieces in our care are covered by our studio insurance. A condition report is prepared immediately on receipt.
Most fired ceramics can be repaired with kintsugi — porcelain, stoneware, earthenware, terracotta. Some glazed surfaces require slight adaptation of the urushi preparation. Glass and non-porous materials cannot be bonded with urushi. We assess compatibility at the initial stage and advise honestly.
A typical full restoration takes 6–12 weeks. The urushi curing stage alone requires 10–14 days. We do not rush any stage of the process. Emergency repairs, which use a simplified technique for faster stabilization, can be completed in 2–4 weeks. We will give you a specific timeline estimate when we assess your piece.
Once fully cured, traditional urushi lacquer is food-safe and has been used in Japan for centuries in food vessels. However, we recommend hand-washing only (no dishwasher) and avoiding sustained contact with strongly acidic foods or liquids, which may affect the seam over time. We provide detailed care instructions with every restoration.
Our lifetime guarantee covers the integrity of the urushi bond and the gold application. If a seam fails — if the gold lifts, chips, or the bond releases — we will restore it at no charge. The guarantee does not cover new damage (further breaks) or damage from dishwasher use or impact. We honour the guarantee without time limit.
