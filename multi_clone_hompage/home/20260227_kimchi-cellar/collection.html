Signature Variety

Baechu Kimchi

배추김치 — Napa Cabbage Kimchi

The cardinal kimchi. Whole heads of heritage-variety napa cabbage, halved and layered with a bold yangnyeom paste of gochugaru, saeujeot, garlic, ginger, and green onion. Our signature 18-month aged baechu develops a complex, layered profile — assertive lactic acidity balanced by deep umami, a lingering warmth, and a texture that yields without dissolving. Irreplaceable.

Fermentation18 months, underground onggi
Spice LevelMedium-Hot (Heritage Gochugaru)
Key IngredientsNapa Cabbage, West Sea Salt, Gochugaru, Saeujeot
Best WithSamgyeopsal, Doenjang Jjigae, Steamed Rice

Kkakdugi
깍두기 — Cubed Radish Kimchi

Crisp-cut cubes of Korean mu radish, seasoned with a paste lighter than baechu but equally complex. The radish retains its satisfying snap for months, while the brine develops a bright, juicy acidity. Our kkakdugi is made only with winter-harvested Jeonju mu radish — sweeter and denser than spring varieties.

Aging6 months
SpiceMedium
TextureCrisp, juicy
Seasonal
Oi Sobagi
오이소박이 — Stuffed Cucumber Kimchi

Summer's kimchi. Korean cucumbers scored crosswise and stuffed with a bright, fragrant paste of garlic, ginger, salted Korean chives, and minimal gochugaru. Best consumed within four to six weeks, when the cucumber retains its crunch and the stuffing is vivid and fresh. Seasonal — June to September only.

Aging2–6 weeks
SpiceMild-Medium
SeasonSummer only
Premium — No Chili
Baek Kimchi
백김치 — White Kimchi

Kimchi without gochugaru — an ancient form predating red pepper's arrival in Korea. Napa cabbage fermented in a brine of pear juice, garlic, ginger, chestnuts, jujubes, and pine nuts. The result is ethereal — delicate, lightly floral, cleanly sour, complex without heat. Favoured by the Joseon royal court.

Aging3–6 months
SpiceNone
StyleRoyal court
Water Kimchi
Nabak Kimchi
나박김치 — Water Kimchi

A water kimchi of thinly sliced napa cabbage and radish in a lightly spiced pink brine of gochugaru (added whole, then strained), garlic, and green onion. Effervescent, cooling, and subtly complex. The brine itself is a masterpiece — drunk cold as a digestive tonic for centuries of Korean dining.

Aging2–4 weeks
SpiceVery Mild
StyleWater brine
Seasonal — Spring
Yeolmu Kimchi
열무김치 — Young Radish Kimchi

Young radish greens harvested in early spring — slender, tender, with a peppery verdancy. Dressed in a lightly fermented flour-paste base (pullcha), gochugaru, garlic, and salted shrimp. A bibim guksu essential. Available April and May only, in very limited quantity from our heritage radish plots.

Aging1–3 weeks
SpiceMedium
SeasonSpring only

Gat Kimchi
갓김치 — Mustard Leaf Kimchi

The pride of South Jeolla Province. Korean mustard leaf — gat — has a sharp, horseradish-adjacent pungency that intensifies beautifully through fermentation. Our gat is sourced exclusively from Yeodo Island, where salt air and volcanic soil produce the most complex flavour. One of our most sought-after regional varieties.

Aging3–8 months

Source Ingredients

Every Ingredient
Has a Provenance

◆
Gochugaru
Yeongyang County, N. Gyeongsang

Sun-dried Cheongyang peppers, stone-ground to a coarse flake. The gold standard gochugaru — vivid red, moderately hot, with a subtle sweetness unavailable in imported varieties.

◆
West Sea Salt
Sinan County, South Jeolla

Solar-evaporated in bamboo salt flats. Three-year aged salt carries minerals that accelerate lactic fermentation. Never refined, never iodised. The invisible foundation of every ONGGI kimchi.

◆
Saeujeot
Ongjin, Incheon

Salted fermented shrimp — the umami backbone of traditional baechu kimchi. We use only Ongjin-style saeujeot, made from small yellow shrimp caught in the West Sea and fermented for six months minimum.

◆
Maesil
Gwangyang, South Jeolla

Japanese apricot, harvested green and preserved in sugar to make maesil cheong (plum syrup). Adds brightness, residual sweetness, and acts as a natural preservative. Source-grown in Gwangyang's famous maesil orchards.

Seasonality

Kimchi Follows
the Korean Calendar

Traditional Korean kimchi is governed by the seasons — not only when vegetables are harvested, but when the air temperature enables particular fermentations. Below is our seasonal availability guide.

△
Spring
봄 — Bom

Yeolmu Kimchi (young radish greens), Ppa Kimchi (green onion), Oi Sobagi (cucumber, early season). Light, fresh, short fermentation profiles.

▲
Summer
여름 — Yeoreum

Oi Sobagi (peak season), Nabak Kimchi, Yeolmu Kimchi. Hot weather means faster fermentation — these kimchi are made and consumed rapidly, prizing freshness.

▼
Autumn
가을 — Gaeul

Kimjang season begins. The great baechu kimchi making — November kimjang baechu is packed into onggi for the long winter aging. Kkakdugi from winter radish also made now.

▽
Winter
겨울 — Gyeoul

Opening of aged baechu from previous years. Baek Kimchi and Dongchimi (whole radish water kimchi) peak in winter — the cold slows fermentation to a perfect murmur.

Pairing Guide

How to Serve
Each Variety

Baechu Kimchi
Samgyeopsal & Grilled Meats

Aged baechu cut into bites and wrapped with grilled pork belly, perilla leaves, and garlic is the canonical pairing. The kimchi's lactic acid cuts through rendered fat with precision. Also essential alongside sundubu jjigae.

Kkakdugi
Seolleongtang & Gomtang

The crunch of kkakdugi against the silken, milky depth of ox-bone soup is a study in contrast that Koreans have refined for centuries. The radish brine, drunk alongside, is a digestive tonic of remarkable clarity.

Baek Kimchi
Galbi & Fine Dining

White kimchi's delicacy makes it the ideal companion for premium beef — LA galbi, hanwoo short rib, or tataki-style preparations. The floral, non-spicy profile allows it to accompany wine without conflict.
