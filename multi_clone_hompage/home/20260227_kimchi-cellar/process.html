The Five Stages

How ONGGI Kimchi
Comes to Be

01
Salting
절임 — Jeolim

Whole heads of heritage napa cabbage are halved lengthwise, exposing the densely packed inner leaves. Coarse solar-evaporated West Sea salt — three-year-aged, never refined — is packed generously between every leaf layer and over the entire cut surface. The salted cabbages are stacked in ceramic vats and left undisturbed for 8 to 12 hours, depending on the cabbage's density and the ambient temperature of the day.

Osmosis draws cellular water outward, creating the brine that will eventually carry the fermentation. This is also when the cell walls of the cabbage soften and become receptive — too little salt produces a weak brine; too much overwhelms the final flavour. Master Lee evaluates every batch personally before proceeding.

Stage Details
  • Only West Sea tidal solar salt from Sinan County, minimum 3-year aged
  • Salt concentration: 15–18% by weight of raw cabbage
  • Duration varies seasonally: 8h (summer), 12h (winter)
  • Cabbage turned once at midpoint to ensure even contact
  • Brine collected and returned to cellar for future batches
Duration 8–12 hours
02
Washing
세척 — Sechok

The salted cabbage is washed three times in cold water drawn from our on-site spring well — drilled 32 metres into the Jeonju basin's aquifer. Well water at constant 12°C is preferred over tap water: no chlorine to disrupt nascent microbial populations, no variable mineral content to alter flavour across batches. Three complete immersions with gentle squeezing between each wash remove excess salt to a precise residual level.

After the final wash, each cabbage half is held upright and hand-squeezed firmly, then laid cut-side down on woven bamboo racks elevated 30cm from the ground to allow full drainage. The draining period of 4 hours is as critical as the washing — too much residual moisture dilutes yangnyeom adhesion and alters fermentation chemistry.

Stage Details
  • Water source: on-site well, 32m deep, 12°C constant temperature
  • Three sequential full immersions in separate basins
  • Target residual salinity: 2–3% by weight
  • Drainage on traditional bamboo racks (대나무 채반)
  • 4-hour minimum drainage before yangnyeom application
Duration 4–6 hours total
03
Yangnyeom
양념 — The Seasoning Paste

The yangnyeom paste is the heart of kimchi — and the element that most distinctly carries son-mat, the maker's signature. ONGGI's base paste: coarsely ground Yeongyang gochugaru (sun-dried, stone-ground, never processed), Ongjin saeujeot (six-month fermented salted shrimp), maesil syrup from Gwangyang orchards, fresh-pressed garlic and ginger, green onion, and a glutinous rice flour porridge (pullcha) that binds the paste and feeds Lactobacillus during early fermentation.

Every batch of yangnyeom is mixed by hand in a large ceramic bowl. Master Lee uses only his dominant right hand, gloved in rubber to protect the skin but maintaining the direct contact required for son-mat transfer. The paste is tasted and adjusted before application — no two batches are identical. Each cabbage half is opened leaf by leaf and the paste is massaged thoroughly between layers, from outermost to innermost.

Yangnyeom Components
  • Gochugaru: Yeongyang Cheongyang variety, sun-dried, stone-ground
  • Saeujeot: Ongjin-style, 6-month minimum fermentation
  • Maesil cheong: Gwangyang orchards, hand-extracted green plum syrup
  • Garlic: Uiseong black garlic cultivar, pressed fresh daily
  • Pullcha: sticky rice flour porridge, Lactobacillus substrate
  • Hand-mixed by Master Lee — never machine-processed
Duration 2–3 hours per batch
04
Packing
담기 — Damgi

Each fully seasoned cabbage half is folded onto itself — the outer leaf wrapped around the inner leaves — and packed tightly into a waiting onggi. Packing technique is critical: the kimchi must be pressed firmly enough to expel all air pockets and ensure the yangnyeom is in direct contact with the brine, but not so aggressively that the leaf structure is compromised. Air is the enemy of anaerobic lactic fermentation.

When the onggi is filled to 80% capacity, a large flat river stone is placed on top to keep the kimchi submerged in its released brine. The pot's neck is then sealed with waxed rice paper and a slip of Jeonju white clay — a traditional seal that breathes CO₂ out but prevents oxygen ingress. The onggi label — batch date, variety, lot number, target aging — is inscribed in ceramic ink on the shoulder of the pot.

Stage Details
  • Each onggi filled to 80% to allow CO₂ expansion headroom
  • Flat river stones used as traditional pressing weights
  • Waxed Korean rice paper (한지) seal allows CO₂ out, blocks O₂
  • Clay slip secondary seal for long-aging batches (12+ months)
  • Each onggi individually labelled with ceramic ink
Duration 1 day room temp, then cellar
05
Underground Aging
숙성 — Sukyeong

The sealed onggi descends into the cellar, lowered by rope into one of three temperature zones: 4°C (reserved for 24+ month batches), 6°C (the primary aging zone, 6–18 months), or 8°C (for short-fermentation varieties, 1–6 months). The earth itself provides the insulation — four metres of compacted Jeonju clay maintains seasonal stability within 1°C variance regardless of surface conditions.

Master Lee evaluates every onggi monthly. He opens each pot briefly — listening for the hiss of CO₂ pressure, smelling the headspace gases, and tasting a small sample. His decisions — to rotate the pot's position, to open it for tasting, or to declare it ready — are guided by 40 years of sensory experience and the meticulous batch records kept since 1962. Some pots are opened on schedule. Others tell him when they are ready.

Aging Zones
  • Zone A (4°C): Ultra-long aging, 24 months to 3 years
  • Zone B (6°C): Primary zone, 6–18 months — our signature baechu
  • Zone C (8°C): Short aging, 1–6 months — oi sobagi, nabak
  • Monthly evaluation by Master Lee: smell, sound, taste
  • Batch records maintained continuously since 1962 — 60+ years of data
Duration 1 month to 3 years
The Vessel

Why Onggi
Cannot Be Replaced

No plastic container, stainless steel vat, or glass jar can replicate the precise microenvironment created by an onggi pot. The vessel is as much an ingredient as salt or gochugaru. Potter Choi Min-ki's pots — made with the same clay body his grandfather developed in 1979 — are the only vessels used in the ONGGI cellar.

  • Material Gyeonggi-do stoneware clay, proprietary formula
  • Firing Temp 1,200°C, traditional wood-fired anagama kiln
  • Porosity Enhanced porous body: 8–12% — regulates humidity and oxygen micro-exchange
  • Capacity 40L standard; 80L long-aging; 120L special reserve
  • Production 6 days per pot; 4 pots per kiln firing (3 firings/year)
  • Lifespan 50–100 years if uncracked; oldest in use: 1958
Fermentation Science

The Biology Behind
the Transformation

◆
Lactic Acid Bacteria

Lactobacillus sakei, L. plantarum, and Leuconostoc mesenteroides are the primary bacteria responsible for kimchi fermentation. They convert the sugars in cabbage and the pullcha binder into lactic acid — the source of kimchi's signature sourness and its preservation power. Dr. Kim So-yeon tracks bacterial populations monthly across all three cellar zones.

◆
Anaerobic Fermentation

Lactic acid bacteria thrive without oxygen. The combination of salt brine (which suppresses aerobic spoilage organisms), the waxed paper seal, and the weight of the pressing stone creates near-perfect anaerobic conditions inside each onggi. The CO₂ produced during fermentation further displaces remaining oxygen over the first 48 hours.

◆
Temperature and Time

Fermentation rate is exponentially temperature-dependent. At 8°C, Lactobacillus activity is slow and meticulous — flavour compounds develop with depth. At 4°C, the process nearly pauses, allowing years of very gradual transformation. The result is umami complexity, structural integrity in the vegetables, and a flavour profile impossible to replicate at higher temperatures or in less time.
