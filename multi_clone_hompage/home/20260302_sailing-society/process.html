Training Programme

The Path
to Blue Water

Our five-level seamanship programme has produced offshore sailors for 60 years. It demands commitment and rewards it with the confidence to sail anywhere in the world.

Each level builds on the last, taught by experienced offshore sailors who have completed ocean passages under every condition imaginable.

1
Day Sailing
Inshore — Protected Waters

The foundation of all sailing. We cover sail trim, basic navigation, boat handling under sail and engine, and the rules of the road. Conducted entirely in the Solent under instructor supervision.

Minimum 10 days on the water. Assessment includes a practical boat-handling test and written chart navigation examination.

2
Coastal Passage
Up to 50nm Offshore

Night passages, passage planning, tidal calculations, and weather routing. Members complete at least two overnight passages as watch-keeper before assessment.

Crew management and emergency procedures are introduced at this level. A VHF radio licence is required before progression.

3
Offshore
Up to 200nm from Safe Haven

Short offshore passages in open water, including the English Channel and Biscay approaches. Members face real offshore conditions and are expected to take responsibility for watch-keeping and navigation.

Heavy weather sailing, storm management, and medical emergency procedures are covered in dedicated shore-based sessions.

4
Ocean
Atlantic or Pacific Crossing

The step from offshore to ocean sailing is profound. Members join an experienced society skipper and crew for a transatlantic or transpacific passage — typically 14–35 days at sea.

Celestial navigation, heavy weather management at sea, and offshore medical care are required competencies before ocean assessment.

5
Blue Water
Independent Command Qualification

Blue Water qualification is the mark of a complete offshore sailor. Holders have demonstrated the ability to plan and execute ocean passages independently, managing vessel, crew, and all contingencies.

Assessment is by a society panel and involves the presentation of passage plans, a practical boat-handling assessment, and a formal interview.
