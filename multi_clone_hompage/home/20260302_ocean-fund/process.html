Methodology

How We Restore

Restoration Approach

Science Before Action

Ocean restoration is not simple, linear, or fast. Every ecosystem is different, every intervention has side effects, and every marine environment demands bespoke solutions. Pelagic's methodology is built on this complexity — not a desire to oversimplify it.

Five-Phase Cycle

From Assessment
to Living Sea

01
Phase One
Marine Health Baseline
Autonomous underwater vehicles, ship-based sampling, satellite oceanography, and biodiversity surveys establish a comprehensive picture of current ocean health. Includes water chemistry, sediment analysis, species composition, and plastic load mapping.
02
Phase Two
Pollution Source Intervention
Targeting primary degradation pathways — coastal agricultural runoff, vessel waste, fishing gear abandonment, and deep-water accumulation zones. Intervention design addresses causes, not only symptoms, and includes community engagement to ensure durable behaviour change.
03
Phase Three
Active Ecosystem Restoration
Coral fragment nurseries, seagrass replanting, kelp forest seeding, and mangrove restoration. Each habitat type requires specialist techniques, seasonal timing, and continuous monitoring to assess survival rates and adaptation outcomes.
04
Phase Four
Legal Protection & Governance
Working with coastal governments and international marine bodies to secure legally binding marine protected area designations. Governance frameworks include local community representation, enforcement protocols, and regular scientific review.
05
Phase Five
Permanent Monitoring & Open Science
Permanent sensor arrays and annual biodiversity surveys track multi-decade recovery trajectories. All data published open-access. Pelagic shares methodology with partner organisations globally, amplifying impact beyond our direct operations.
Field Infrastructure

What We Deploy

Research Fleet
Three ocean-going research vessels operating year-round across Atlantic, Pacific, and Indian Ocean zones.
Autonomous Monitoring
120+ autonomous underwater vehicles and 340 fixed sensor buoys providing real-time ocean chemistry and species data.
Coral Nurseries
18 active coral propagation nurseries maintaining 42 coral species for reef seeding operations.
