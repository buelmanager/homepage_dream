Our Story

Four Generations,
One Obsession

Since Don Aurelio Agavero planted his first blue agave in the highlands of Jalisco in 1928, this family has made one promise: to never compromise the spirit for the sake of efficiency.

1928
The Beginning

Don Aurelio's Vision

In the spring of 1928, a young agave farmer named Don Aurelio Agavero made a decision that would shape a family legacy for the next century. He had worked the red volcanic soil of Los Altos his entire life, selling raw piñas to larger commercial producers. He had watched as industrial methods slowly eroded the character from the region's tequila. He decided to stop watching.

With a single copper pot still purchased from a retiring mezcalero in Oaxaca, Don Aurelio converted the old barn of his father's rancho into the first Agavero distillery. His first run produced 47 liters of blanco — tasted by the family, debated over dinner, and declared imperfect. He tried again.

"Perfection is not a destination. It is a direction. As long as we keep walking toward it, we have done our work."

Don Aurelio's philosophy — rooted in the belief that tequila is not manufactured but rather revealed from the agave — has guided every decision the family has made since that first, imperfect run nearly a century ago.

Jalisco Highlands

The Land That
Makes the Spirit

The Hacienda San Marcos sits at 1,800 meters above sea level in the Los Altos region of Jalisco — the highlands district that produces tequila of a distinct character: more floral, more fruity, more complex than the lowland valleys. The altitude and latitude conspire to create agave plants of extraordinary density and sugar content.

The soil of our estate is a deep red volcanic clay, iron-rich and mineral-dense, formed by centuries of volcanic activity. This same mineral profile — the terroir — passes into the agave juice and ultimately into the finished spirit. You can taste the red earth of Jalisco in every glass of AGAVERO.

The seasonal extremes of the highlands also shape the agave profoundly. Warm, dry winters build structure; cool, wet summers encourage growth. The temperature swings between day and night can exceed 20°C during ripening season — a stress the agave converts into aromatic complexity.

The Jimador Tradition

The Hands That
Shape the Spirit

In a world of increasing mechanization, the art of the jimador remains irreducibly human. A jimador is the master craftsman who harvests the ripe agave piña — the heart of the plant — using a tool called the coa de jima: a long-handled, round-bladed implement that strips the long agave leaves (pencas) with practiced efficiency.

The skill of the jimador lies not in harvesting speed, but in judgment. Each agave plant ripens on its own schedule. The jimador reads the plant — observing the color of the leaves, the shape of the central shoot, the density of the pencas — and determines by eye whether that individual plant, at that particular moment, is ready. A plant harvested too early lacks sugar; too late, and the piña becomes woody and astringent.

The Agavero hacienda employs eleven full-time jimadores, several of whom represent the second and third generation of their families to work our estate. This continuity of knowledge — of reading the same fields across decades — is something no algorithm can replicate.

Coa de jima — the iconic round-bladed harvesting blade, custom-forged for each jimador's preferred weight and balance.
Piña selection — only 100% mature agaves are harvested; any plant showing immaturity is left to complete its cycle.
Replanting tradition — for every harvested agave, our jimadores plant two hijuelos (agave pups) — ensuring the estate regenerates faster than it is consumed.
Our Commitments

What We Stand For

01

No Diffuser

We have never and will never use a diffuser — the industrial shortcut that eliminates cooking and crushes agave with water, destroying complexity at the root.

02

No Additives

AGAVERO is certified additive-free. No glycerin, no caramel color, no artificial sweeteners. What is in the bottle is exclusively agave, water, time, and craft.

03

Estate Only

Every piña distilled under the AGAVERO name grows on our land. We source nothing externally — this is a complete hacienda-to-bottle estate tequila program.

04

Community First

Our jimadores earn above-regional-average wages with full benefits. The Agavero Foundation supports the schools and healthcare infrastructure of Arandas.
