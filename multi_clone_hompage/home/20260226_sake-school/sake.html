The Method

How Sake is Made

Every bottle of Kurabito sake passes through the same eight-hundred-year-old process, refined by five generations of toji wisdom.

01
Rice Polishing
精米 · Seimai

The outer bran of each grain is removed. The deeper we polish, the purer the fermentation and the more delicate the final sake. Our Daiginjo is polished to 48% — less than half of each grain remains.

02
Koji Cultivation
製麹 · Seikiku

Aspergillus oryzae mold is cultivated on steamed rice in the koji room for 48 hours. The toji monitors temperature by hand, by smell, by instinct. This is the heart of sake.

03
Fermentation
発酵 · Hakko

Rice, koji, water, and yeast are combined in cedar tanks. The mash ferments for 25 to 60 days at temperatures as low as 5°C for ginjo styles. The toji sleeps beside the tanks in winter.

04
Pressing & Aging
搾り · Shibori

The mash is pressed through traditional fune (wooden pressing box), separating the sake from the lees. Premium sake is then aged in enameled steel tanks or ancient cedar barrels for three to twelve months.

Limited Releases

Seasonal Sake

Sake is a seasonal art. The best rice is harvested in autumn, the brewing season runs from October to March, and certain expressions can only be made once a year.

Winter · Limited Release
Shinshu Arabashiri

The first-run sake of the season, pressed in early December from the newborn mash. Raw, vibrant, and alive — available only in our tasting room for six weeks.

Available: December – January · Kura only
Spring · Seasonal Release
Haru Kasumi

Spring Haze. A light, effervescent nama (unpasteurized) sake that captures the awakening of Kyoto in cherry blossom season. Chilled and poured in ceramic cups beneath the kura's plum tree.

Available: March – April · 480 bottles
Summer · Cold Service
Natsu Ginjo

A lighter, refreshing Ginjo formulated for summer service at 8°C. Lower alcohol and higher acidity balance the heat of Kyoto summer. Recommended with river fish and cold tofu.

Available: June – August · 600 bottles
Autumn · Reserve
Hiyaoroshi

The seasonal classic — sake pasteurized once in spring and then aged through summer, released in September when the autumn air cools the kura. Mellow, deep, and perfectly mature.

Available: September – October · 320 bottles
Table Harmony

Sake & Food Pairing

The Japanese principle of ma — space and harmony — applies to the table as much as the brewery. Our sommeliers have created the following pairing guide for home use.

Grade Style Best Temperature Classic Pairings Avoid
Junmai Full-bodied, savory, warm 45–50°C (atsukan) Grilled fish, aged tofu, miso soup, yakitori Delicate raw seafood (overpowers)
Ginjo Light, floral, clean 10–15°C (hana-hie) Sashimi, steamed clams, edamame, light salads Rich sauces (compete with aromatics)
Daiginjo Ethereal, aromatic, dry 8–12°C (yuki-hie) Uni (sea urchin), lobster, fine white fish, nothing Anything with strong flavor — drink alone
Nigori Rich, creamy, sweet 5–8°C (well chilled) Spicy dishes, rich cream desserts, fresh fruit, cheese Delicate dashi (sweetness overpowers)
