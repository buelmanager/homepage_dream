Our Story

The Kura & the
Toji

Since 1872

A Brewery Built on
Five Generations of Devotion

The Nakamura sake brewery was founded in Meiji 5 — 1872 — in the underground-water-rich district of Fushimi, Kyoto. What began as a small family kura serving local sake to the temples and teahouses of the old capital has grown into one of Japan's most respected sake schools and tasting houses, without ever abandoning the artisan principles of its founders.


Fushimi is famous for its soft, mineral-rich groundwater — fed by ancient granite mountains — which gives Kurabito sake its distinctive silken texture. Our water has never been filtered or altered. It falls as rain on Mount Hiei, travels through limestone and granite for decades, and arrives at our kura pure and ancient.

Toji Lineage

The Nakamura Family

Each toji inherits not only the brewery but the accumulated wisdom of every generation before — passed through years of observation, through seasons spent sleeping by the fermentation tanks.

1872
Kaijiro Nakamura
Founder · First Toji

Opened the original kura in Fushimi with a single cedar tank and a dream of sake worthy of the emperor's table. His first brew won regional distinction in 1875.

1906
Seiichi Nakamura
Second Generation · Master Toji

Introduced the low-temperature ginjo fermentation method that would become a Kurabito hallmark. Survived the Great Kanto Earthquake and rebuilt the kura in the Taisho style.

1948
Yoshio Nakamura
Third Generation · Toji

Rebuilt after the war years using reclaimed timber and ancestral recipes. Won the National New Sake Competition three years in succession, 1961–1963.

1978
Hiroshi Nakamura
Fourth Generation · Toji & Educator

First of the Nakamura toji to open the kura's doors to students, launching the informal apprenticeship that would become today's Kurabito school.

2008 — Present
Takeshi Nakamura
Fifth Generation · Head Toji

Formalized the school curriculum, earned JSSA endorsement, and launched the award-winning Seasonal Immersion program. Recipient of the 2022 Kyoto Cultural Merit Prize.

Our Philosophy

The Kurabito Way

Sake is not manufactured — it is cultivated. The toji listens to the rice, reads the temperature of the air, watches the movement of the koji mold under lamplight. Every decision is a conversation between the brewer and the living organisms in the tank. This patience cannot be automated. It can only be taught.

"The koji does not work for the toji. The toji works for the koji."
— Hiroshi Nakamura, 4th Toji
水
Water
Mizu

The soul of sake begins with water. Fushimi's groundwater is among the softest and purest in Japan — low in iron, rich in the minerals that allow koji to thrive. We use it unmodified.

米
Rice
Kome

We source Yamada Nishiki rice exclusively from a single farm in Hyogo Prefecture. Each autumn, Toji Nakamura visits to assess the harvest before a grain is purchased.

時
Time
Toki

No shortcuts. Our Daiginjo mash ferments for sixty days at near-freezing temperatures. The toji sleeps beside the tank during the critical winter weeks.

Head Toji

Meet the Toji

Takeshi Nakamura
Fifth Generation Toji · Head of Kurabito

Born in the kura in 1973, Takeshi Nakamura grew up with the smell of fermenting rice and the sound of cedar tanks in his earliest memories. He studied fermentation science at Kyoto University before returning to apprentice under his father, learning that the science explains but never replaces the instinct of the toji.

Under his stewardship, Kurabito has won 23 national and international awards, including Gold at the International Wine Challenge Sake Division four years running. He teaches every course himself, believing that knowledge without direct transmission loses something essential.

B.Sc. Fermentation Science, Kyoto University (1997)
JSSA Certified Sake Expert and Educator
Gold Medal, IWC Sake Division 2019–2022
2022 Kyoto Cultural Merit Prize recipient
Guest lecturer, Tokyo University of Agriculture
