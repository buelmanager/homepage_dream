The Founding

A Biologist's Obsession

Elena Vasari and Lago di Ledro

In 1978, freshwater biologist Elena Vasari became convinced that the neglected freshwater mussel could produce pearls that equaled — and in some properties surpassed — anything grown in saltwater. The scientific consensus was dismissive. She acquired cultivation rights to Lago di Ledro anyway.

Eleven years later, the first LACUSTRE collection debuted at Salone del Mobile in Milan. The critics who expected cheap alternatives were confronted with something they had not anticipated: freshwater pearls of extraordinary luster, grown in conditions so pristine that their nacre was measurably thicker than saltwater equivalents.

Today Elena is 79 and still personally approves every pearl that enters the collection. The Lago di Ledro estate remains the heart of LACUSTRE, even as five additional lakes have been added over the decades.

The Estates

Six Lake Estates

Lago di Ledro

Italian Pre-Alps · Est. 1978

The founding estate. Glacially fed at 652m altitude, producing pearls with warm rose overtones and exceptional luster.

Lake Saimaa

Finnish Lake District · Est. 2001

The world's fourth-largest freshwater lake system. Cold, dark water produces dense nacre with a distinctive silver-white orient.

Lago Cochrane

Chilean Patagonia · Est. 2015

The world's southernmost pearl estate. Glacier meltwater of unparalleled purity creates the densest, most lustrous pearls in the collection.
