Rooms & Suites — Where Bamboo Becomes Home

Our Philosophy of Space

Twelve Rooms, Each Utterly Different

No two rooms at Kuretake share the same view, the same cedar grain, or the same quality of morning light. Each has been designed in conversation with its particular corner of the bamboo grove — accommodating the tree that grew through the wall, the spring that surfaces near the north foundation, the ancient pine that casts its shadow across the veranda at three in the afternoon.

Premier Suite

Bamboo Grand Suite

From ¥85,000 per night · Two guests

Our most expansive accommodation occupies the uppermost position in the bamboo grove, where the canopy closes overhead and birdsong arrives before dawn. The suite comprises two tatami rooms, a private hinoki bath, and a rotenburo — an outdoor soaking tub positioned on a cedar deck so that you bathe beneath an open sky latticed with bamboo.

The tokonoma alcove holds a rotating selection from the Yamamoto family's scroll and ceramic collection. Meals are served in-room by your personal nakai attendant.

Garden View Room

From ¥45,000 per night · Two guests

Facing the east moss garden — Hideo Yamamoto's masterwork, planted in 1922 — this room exemplifies the traditional ryokan aesthetic at its most refined. The sliding shoji screens open onto a viewing veranda that places you within arm's reach of the ancient stone lantern.

The in-room hinoki cedar bath fills with mineral water piped directly from the mountain spring, carrying a faint sulfurous warmth that has been drawing visitors to this mountainside for over a century.

Signature Room

Forest Bath Chamber

From ¥62,000 per night · Two guests

The most architecturally distinctive accommodation at Kuretake, the Forest Bath Chamber dissolves the boundary between interior and exterior. Three walls are traditional plaster; the fourth is the bamboo grove itself — living culms that grow through a custom glass panel, making the forest a literal element of the room's architecture.

The soaking tub is positioned to face this living wall. You bathe watching bamboo sway in the mountain breeze, the grove's shadow-play becoming your evening entertainment.

Classic Room

Matsu Wing

From ¥32,000 per night · Two guests

The original Meiji-era wing of Kuretake has been lovingly maintained rather than renovated. The floorboards still carry the slight unevenness of hand-planing. The tansu chest in the corner belonged to Toshiro Yamamoto's wife, Setsuko. The ceiling shows the same smoke-darkening from a century of charcoal winters.

Matsu Wing guests share access to the cedar communal onsen — a larger, sociable bath where, if the timing is right, you may find yourself soaking beside the same silence that has settled on this mountain since 1890. Meals are served in the common dining room, where the kaiseki kitchen's full seasonal menu is presented at a communal table.
