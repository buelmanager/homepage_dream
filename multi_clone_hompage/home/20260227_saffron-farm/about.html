Our Story

A Family Estate, a Generation's Dedication

ZAFARAN began in 2002, when the Shirazi family consolidated their ancestral plots in the Gonabad district of South Khorasan into a single, unified estate. What had been fragmented small holdings across three generations became 120 hectares of purpose-cultivated saffron land.

The land itself is the inheritance. The altitude — 1,350 meters above sea level — the alkaline clay soil, and the pattern of hot dry summers followed by cold winters creates conditions that Crocus sativus has adapted to over thousands of years. No agricultural science can replace this geography.

Today, the Shirazi family operates with a team of 240 seasonal harvesters, all from Gonabad and surrounding villages, whose families have picked saffron for generations. The estate employs them year-round in corm preparation, irrigation maintenance, and grading work.

Our Principles

What We Stand For

⬡

Provenance Transparency

Every batch carries a lot number traceable to the specific field section, harvest week, and grading run. We share our lab certificates publicly and welcome independent testing.

⬡

Harvest Integrity

We never blend current-year stock with previous harvests. No artificial dyes, no moisture addition, no chemical treatment. Our product is pure stigmas, nothing else.

⬡

Community Stewardship

Our harvesters earn above the regional median wage and receive full healthcare. The estate funds the Gonabad Saffron School, training the next generation of master graders.

⬡

Environmental Care

Crocus sativus improves soil structure and requires no pesticides. Our irrigation uses drip systems to minimize water use in an arid region where water is precious.

⬡

Scientific Rigor

Coloring strength, safranal content, and moisture levels are measured for every batch at our on-site laboratory and independently verified by ISO-accredited third parties.

⬡

Generational Thinking

We replant corms on a 7-year rotation. Short-term yield is sacrificed to maintain soil health and corm vitality. The quality of our saffron depends on decisions made years in advance.

Life on the Estate

A Year in Khorasan

The Shirazi Family

Three Generations of Saffron

Ahmad Shirazi, the estate's founder, inherited eight hectares from his father — a Gonabad farmer who had grown saffron as one of many crops. Ahmad saw the potential to specialize completely, dedicating all land to Crocus sativus and pursuing export markets directly rather than selling through middlemen.

His son, Dariush, holds a degree in food science from the University of Tehran. He introduced the on-site laboratory, ISO certification process, and the estate's direct export model. Today Dariush's daughter, Shireen, manages international sales and the brand strategy that has made ZAFARAN known in Michelin-starred kitchens from Paris to Tokyo.

The family's conviction is simple: saffron is too important, too complex, and too often adulterated to be sold anonymously through commodity chains. Every gram of ZAFARAN saffron carries the name of the family that grew it.
