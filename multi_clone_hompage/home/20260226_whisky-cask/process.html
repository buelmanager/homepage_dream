Common Questions

Frequently Asked

Is whisky cask investment regulated in the UK? +

Whisky cask investment is not currently regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), though the FCA has issued guidance on the sector. CASK SOCIÉTÉ operates as a cask brokerage and custodian, not as an authorised investment firm. We strongly recommend members seek independent financial advice before investing.

What are the tax implications of owning a cask? +

In the UK, a whisky cask is generally classified as a "wasting asset" under TCGA 1992 — with a predictable life of 50 years or less — and is therefore exempt from Capital Gains Tax on disposal. Excise duty is payable only on bottling or removal from bond. We recommend independent tax advice, as individual circumstances vary.

What happens if the distillery closes or is sold? +

Your cask is held in your legal name in a third-party bonded warehouse, entirely independent of the distillery. If a distillery closes, its whisky stocks — including your cask — are typically sold as part of the asset realisation, often at significant premium. Distillery closures have historically increased the value of remaining stocks.

Can I visit my cask in the warehouse? +

Yes. Investor and Connoisseur members may arrange warehouse visits by prior appointment. These are arranged in partnership with our distillery network and include guided access to your specific cask, a tasting from your own stock, and a tour of the production facility.

How long must I hold my cask? +

There is no minimum holding period imposed by CASK SOCIÉTÉ. However, Scotch whisky legally requires a minimum of 3 years' maturation before it may be legally sold as "Scotch Whisky." We recommend a 10–25 year horizon for optimal flavour development and investment return.

What are the annual costs of ownership? +

Annual costs include: warehouse storage and insurance (typically £300–£500 per annum per cask), annual valuation report and sample dispatch (included in membership fee), and HMRC bonded warehouse administration. Full cost schedules are provided in the membership information pack.

Risk Disclosure

Investing in whisky casks involves risk. The value of casks can fall as well as rise, and past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results. Whisky cask investment is not regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority in the United Kingdom. Capital may be at risk. You may not receive back the amount you invest. The illiquid nature of cask investment means exit may take longer than anticipated and at values below expectations. CASK SOCIÉTÉ is not an authorised investment firm and does not provide regulated financial advice. All prospective members are strongly encouraged to seek independent financial advice before committing capital. This website does not constitute a financial promotion.
