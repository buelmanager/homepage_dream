Our Story

About Verdant National Parks

Over a century of protecting wild places in the national interest — the story of how wilderness became heritage.

Founded 1916

A Century of Wild Stewardship

Verdant National Parks was founded in 1916 under the National Parks Mandate Act — the world's first legislation to permanently protect wild landscapes as a public trust. The founding principle was radical for its time: that certain places were too magnificent, too ecologically vital, and too important to the national spirit to be surrendered to private interests.

Beginning with six wilderness areas covering 2 million acres, the agency has grown to manage 24 national parks across seven ecological zones, protecting 48 million acres for the benefit of every citizen. From boreal forests to coastal wetlands, from mountain glaciers to high desert canyons, our parks represent the full diversity of the nation's natural heritage.

Today, 3,200 rangers, scientists, and educators carry forward the founding mission — not simply to preserve static landscapes, but to actively restore, monitor, and interpret wild nature for a world in which climate change demands ever-greater ecological ambition.

Our Principles

What We Stand For

01

Wilderness First

When in doubt, the interests of wild nature take precedence over human convenience. We manage for ecological health, not visitor comfort, wherever these come into conflict.

02

Universal Access

All parks are free to enter. We actively work to remove barriers of cost, distance, and culture that prevent citizens from accessing their national inheritance.

03

Science-Led Management

Every management decision is grounded in the best available ecological science. We employ over 400 scientists and work with 60 partner universities to expand our knowledge base.
