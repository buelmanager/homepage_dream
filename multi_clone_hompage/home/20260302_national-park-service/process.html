Science & Stewardship

Conservation

How we protect, restore, and monitor 48 million acres of wild nature using the best available ecological science.

Scientific Approach

Evidence-Led Conservation

Every management decision across our 24 parks is grounded in rigorous ecological science. We maintain one of the world's largest continuous ecological monitoring networks — tracking biodiversity, climate indicators, water quality, and wildlife populations in real time across 48 million acres.

I

Biodiversity Monitoring

Annual surveys track over 4,000 species of plants, birds, mammals, insects, and fungi, providing the baseline data that guides all habitat management decisions.

II

Climate Adaptation

Working with 60 partner universities, we model how climate change will affect each park's ecosystem and develop management strategies to build ecological resilience.

III

Rewilding

Active rewilding programmes — including carnivore reintroduction and riparian restoration — are rebuilding natural ecological processes in landscapes long altered by human activity.

Restoration

Rebuilding What Was Lost

Our restoration teams work across 120,000 acres annually — removing invasive species, replanting native vegetation, and restoring natural water flows. In the last decade we have removed 18,000 miles of deer fencing, allowing native woodland to regenerate naturally across the northern parks.

Our most ambitious project is the Great Forest Recovery Programme — a 20-year effort to restore 2 million acres of native woodland to the deforested uplands of the central region. Planting began in 2019 and is already showing remarkable results for biodiversity and carbon sequestration.

Education & Engagement

Building Tomorrow's Conservationists

Ranger Education

Our ranger-led programmes reach 2 million schoolchildren annually, delivering hands-on wildlife and ecology education directly in the parks — learning that cannot happen in a classroom.

Volunteer Corps

12,000 trained volunteers contribute 400,000 hours annually — maintaining trails, monitoring species, removing invasives, and welcoming visitors across all 24 parks.

Research Partnerships

We host over 300 university researchers each year, providing access to our ecological data, field stations, and monitoring infrastructure to advance conservation science globally.

Community Rangers

Our Community Ranger apprenticeship programme recruits young people from urban communities to train as professional rangers — building diversity into the conservation workforce of the future.
