From Vision to Print

The Method

Creative Process

Every image in the LUMIÈRE portfolio passes through four distinct phases — each an act of deliberate refinement, from first concept to finished museum-grade print.

01
Phase One
Observation & Research

Before a single frame is exposed, weeks or months are spent observing the subject — visiting locations at different hours, seasons, and weather conditions. A project is never begun until the photographer has a specific, clearly defined visual question to answer.

02
Phase Two
The Exposure

Shooting exclusively on large-format film (4x5 and 8x10 inch) or 120 medium format. Each sheet of film represents a commitment — there is no motor drive, no burst shooting. The frame is studied on the ground glass before the shutter is released. Typically fewer than ten sheets are exposed per day of work.

03
Phase Three
Darkroom Processing

All film is developed by hand in the Paris studio darkroom using pyrocat developer — a formula that produces extended tonal range and exceptional shadow detail. Contact prints are made first to evaluate the negative before enlargement. The entire process takes approximately three weeks from exposure to finished print.

04
Phase Four
Edition & Release

Each final print is dry-mounted on museum board, selenium-toned for permanence, and stored in archival clamshell boxes. Edition sizes are determined by the project — typically 5 to 25 prints. Once an edition is closed, no further prints are made. Ever.

The Tools

Equipment & Approach

I.
Large Format
Linhof Technika 4×5

The primary camera for landscape and architectural work. Movements allow precise perspective control and extended depth of field impossible with other formats.

Format: 4×5 inch sheet film
Primary lenses: Schneider 90mm, 150mm, 210mm
Film: Ilford FP4 Plus, Kodak T-Max 100
II.
Medium Format
Hasselblad 500C/M

The workhorse for documentary and editorial assignments. Faster to deploy than large format while retaining sufficient resolution for exhibition-scale prints.

Format: 120 film, 6×6cm
Lenses: Carl Zeiss Planar 80mm, Distagon 50mm
Film: Kodak Tri-X 400, Ilford HP5
III.
Darkroom
Durst L1200 Enlarger

A professional colour/black-and-white enlarger capable of handling up to 4×5 inch negatives. Used with cold light head for even, contrasty illumination across the print surface.

Papers: Ilford Multigrade FB, Adox MCP 310
Chemistry: Pyrocat developer, Selenium toner
Processing: Archival fibre base, 60-min wash
Fieldwork

Location Scouting

The majority of landscape work requires multiple reconnaissance trips before a single sheet of film is exposed. Specific light conditions — the particular quality of early morning fog in the Pyrenees, the flat grey of an overcast Nordic winter — may only occur a handful of times per year.

Preferred working locations include the Pyrenean highlands, the Breton coast, the Scottish islands, and the Norwegian fjords. Documentary projects have taken the studio to Morocco, Japan, Peru, and across the American Southwest.

Each location is thoroughly researched before arrival — historical images studied, light mapping conducted via satellite imagery, and local contacts established to understand seasonal conditions.

Finishing

Post-Production

01
Contact Proofing

Each developed negative is contact-printed at 1:1 scale for evaluation. This reveals the tonal quality and composition exactly as exposed, before any enlargement decisions are made.

02
Test Strips

Up to twelve test strips at varying exposures and contrast grades are made before the final print exposure is committed. Time-consuming, but it ensures the print is exactly what the negative contains.

03
Archival Washing

Each fibre-based print undergoes a 60-minute wash in running water followed by Hypo Clear treatment, ensuring complete removal of residual chemistry for maximum archival permanence.

04
Selenium Toning

A brief selenium toning step converts silver to silver selenide — increasing print density, separating shadow tones, and extending the archival lifespan to an estimated 200+ years.
