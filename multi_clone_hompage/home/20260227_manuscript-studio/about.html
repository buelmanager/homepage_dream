A Studio Founded
on Conviction

Scriptorium was founded in 1997 by master illuminator Eleanor Vane-Hartley, who trained for seven years under Belgian calligrapher Brother Pieter De Coene at the Abbey of Sint-Andries before establishing her studio in a converted stone granary on the Oxfordshire estate of a sympathetic patron.

Her founding conviction — that the medieval scriptorium was not a museum exhibit but a viable and urgently necessary artistic practice — has guided every decision since. The studio's first commission, a Psalter for a collector in Edinburgh, took eleven months and established the studio's reputation immediately.

In 2003, two further master calligraphers joined: Raphael Castrignano, trained in the Florentine tradition of humanistic scripts, and Saoirse Ní Dhálaigh, a specialist in Insular and Celtic illumination from the Royal Irish Academy. Together, the three principals extended the studio's range across the full spectrum of Western manuscript traditions.

By 2010, Scriptorium had completed more than a hundred commissions for private patrons, museums, libraries, and ecclesiastical institutions across fifteen countries. Its work has been exhibited at the Victoria and Albert Museum, the Bodleian Library, and the Bibliothèque nationale de France.

Today the studio accepts no more than twelve commissions per year. This limit is not artificial scarcity — it is the honest arithmetic of excellence. The finest manuscripts have always taken time.

Our Illuminators

Master Hands of the Scriptorium

Eleanor Vane-Hartley
Founding Master — Illumination & Gold

Trained at the Abbey of Sint-Andries and the Hochschule für Grafik und Buchkunst Leipzig. Eleanor specialises in raised gold illumination, historiated initials, and border systems drawn from the Flemish and English manuscript traditions of the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries.

Gothic Textura Carolingian Insular Majuscule
Raphael Castrignano
Master Calligrapher — Humanistic & Italic Scripts

A graduate of the Scuola del Libro di Urbino and former associate of the Istituto Centrale per il Restauro, Rome. Raphael brings the Renaissance humanist tradition to the studio: Italic and Roman scripts rendered with a precision that has drawn comparison to the manuscripts of Bartolomeo Sanvito.

Humanistic Minuscule Italic Roman Capitals
Saoirse Ní Dhálaigh
Insular & Celtic Illumination Specialist

A fellow of the Royal Irish Academy and graduate of the National College of Art and Design, Dublin. Saoirse is the studio's authority on Insular art — the interlace, zoomorphic, and carpet-page traditions of the Hiberno-Saxon world, from the Gospels of Durrow to the Book of Kells.

Studio Ethos

The Commitments We
Cannot Compromise

01

Authentic Materials Only

No synthetic vellum, no inkjet gilding, no digital under-drawing. Every material is sourced from traditional suppliers: calf and kidskin from specialist English tanneries, lapis lazuli ground from Badakhshan stone, iron-gall ink made from Aleppo oak galls. A Scriptorium commission is made of the same materials as the manuscripts of Charlemagne's court.

02

Full Historical Fidelity

We consult primary sources — manuscript facsimiles, treatises such as the Theophilus De diversis artibus and the Mappae Clavicula — before each commission. Period-accurate pigment recipes, ruling methods, and binding structures are employed as a matter of principle, not as an affectation.

03

The Patron Is a Collaborator

Every commission involves the patron at each significant stage. We share preliminary drawings, colour tests on spare vellum, and gilding samples before proceeding. Nothing is finalised without consent. The commission is yours; we are its executors.

04

Silence as a Working Condition

The medieval scriptorium was a silent place. We honour this. No music, no telephone in the studio. The concentration required by fine illumination is not compatible with distraction. Our patrons tell us they can sense this in the work — a quality of presence, of sustained attention, that distinguishes it from even very accomplished imitation.
