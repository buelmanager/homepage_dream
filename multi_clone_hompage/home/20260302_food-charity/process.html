How It Works

Our Approach

From Surplus to Table

The path from farm surplus to community table is shorter than most people imagine. The Harvest Commons has spent 19 years refining the logistics, building the relationships, and training the people that make this journey fast, dignified, and reliable.

The Food Journey

Five Steps to Every Table

01
Step One
Surplus Mapping & Commitment
We work year-round with 86 farm partners to forecast and pre-commit surplus harvests before they reach the farm gate. Real-time digital mapping shows where food is available, what volume is expected, and which community distribution points are nearest.
02
Step Two
Collection & Quality Sorting
Volunteer teams collect surplus produce daily from farm partners using our refrigerated collection network. All food is hand-sorted and quality-graded on arrival — ensuring that every recipient receives fresh, excellent produce, not discards.
03
Step Three
Kitchen Processing & Transformation
Surplus is transformed in our 32 community kitchens: preserved, prepared as ready meals, packed into Harvest Boxes, or used in Kitchen Academy cooking sessions. Nothing is wasted; every edible item has a planned destination before it arrives.
04
Step Four
Dignified Distribution
Food reaches families through neighbourhood collection points, community table events, and partner food network hubs. Distribution is designed to feel like a community market, not a charity hand-out — because dignity is non-negotiable.
05
Step Five
Education & Food Sovereignty Building
Every distribution touchpoint is an education opportunity. Kitchen Academy classes, seasonal recipe guides, growing workshops, and preservation courses ensure that food knowledge travels alongside food itself — building genuine long-term food sovereignty.
Our Infrastructure

What Makes It Work

Farm Network
86 partner farms from small holdings to regional cooperatives, all pre-committing surplus via our real-time surplus mapping platform.
Community Kitchens
32 fully equipped community kitchen spaces staffed by trained volunteers and local kitchen leads, processing and distributing food daily.
Volunteer Network
1,200 active volunteers trained in food handling, quality sorting, community engagement, and Kitchen Academy teaching.
