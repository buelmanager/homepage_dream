Our Belief
"In a world that grows more than enough food for every person alive, hunger is a logistics problem — not a scarcity problem. We solve the logistics."
Our Leadership

Grounded in
Community

The Harvest Commons was founded in 2007 by food system advocates who had worked in the gap between agricultural surplus and urban food insecurity for years. They saw the systemic failure clearly: food was being wasted at farm level while people went hungry two miles away. The solution was not charity — it was connection.

Our leadership team draws from food science, community development, urban agriculture, and social enterprise. Every strategic decision is reviewed by a community advisory panel drawn from the neighbourhoods we serve.

Rosa Fielding
Founder & Executive Director
Core Values

What Drives Us

Dignity Always
Every person who receives food through the Commons does so with the same dignity as a paying customer at a fine restaurant. We design every programme around this principle.
Zero Waste
Every edible item that goes to landfill is a failure of distribution. Our mission is to close the gap between agricultural surplus and food insecurity — completely.
Community Ownership
We build programmes that communities own and run. Our role is to provide resources and systems; the community provides the care, knowledge, and culture.
Our History

Nineteen Years of
Shared Tables

2007
Founded in Bristol
The Harvest Commons began with a single community allotment, one kitchen space, and a handful of volunteers committed to turning food waste into nourishment.
2012
First Farm Network
Establishment of our first farm partnership network with 20 regional growers, and expansion to 10 community kitchens across four cities.
2018
100-Tonne Year
Annual food redistribution exceeded 100 tonnes. Launch of the Urban Growing Initiative in six cities and the Kitchen Academy nutrition education programme.
2024
National Network
32 kitchens, 86 farm partners, 1,200 active volunteers. 420 tonnes redistributed annually across 24 cities, reaching 2,400 families every week.
