The Meridian Light — Cape Meridian, Maine — Est. 1887
Our Story

138 Years of Coastal Legend

On a windswept promontory overlooking the Atlantic, a lighthouse has stood since 1887. Built to warn merchantmen of treacherous granite shoals, the Meridian Light guided four generations of keeper families through Atlantic storms, fog-bound winters, and the quiet miracle of every dawn.

Today, that same light still burns. And the lighthouse that once housed solitary keepers now welcomes eight guests at a time to share in the singular experience of life at the edge of the sea.

History

The Light Through Time

From its construction in the post-Civil War era to its transformation as a boutique inn, the Meridian Light has witnessed the full sweep of American coastal history.

1887

The Light is Built

Commissioned by the United States Lighthouse Board, the Meridian Light rises 52 meters from the Cape Meridian headland. Its original third-order Fresnel lens projects a beam 24 nautical miles into the Atlantic — visible from Halifax to Portland.

1891

The Aldrich Family Arrives

Keeper Samuel Aldrich and his wife Martha become the lighthouse's first resident family. Martha's journals — preserved in our Maritime Archive — describe "mornings of impossible beauty, the sea silver as a mirror, and the beam still cutting through the last of the mist."

1924

The Great Atlantic Storm

Keeper William Aldrich Jr. maintains the light through the catastrophic November gale of 1924, guiding three vessels to safety as the storm claimed a hundred lives along the New England coast. The Aldrich lantern room desk still stands in our Captain's Quarters.

1958

Electrification & Automation

The lighthouse is electrified and partially automated by the Coast Guard. The last resident keeper, Eleanor Aldrich, passes her family's maritime logbooks to the Cape Meridian Historical Society before departing — a collection now displayed throughout the inn.

1996

Historic Landmark Designation

The Meridian Lighthouse is designated a National Historic Landmark. The original Fresnel lens, brass mechanisms, and keeper quarters are preserved under federal protection — ensuring their survival for future generations.

2018

The Inn Opens

After five years of careful restoration by the Meridian Trust, the lighthouse opens as an intimate eight-room inn. Every original element is preserved; every comfort is carefully added. The light still burns. The sea still speaks. Now, guests are invited to listen.

The Keepers

Guardians of the Light

Four generations of keeper families shaped the character of the Meridian Light. Their stories live on in every room.

First Era · 1887–1921

Samuel Aldrich

Head Keeper, 1887–1921

A former merchant navy officer from Portsmouth, Samuel Aldrich brought naval discipline and deep ocean knowledge to the lighthouse. He hand-ground the Fresnel lens monthly, recorded weather observations daily for 34 years, and is credited with the design of the captain's observation platform that still crowns the lighthouse tower.

"The sea does not distinguish between the careful and the careless. Only the light does."
Second Era · 1921–1948

William Aldrich Jr.

Head Keeper, 1921–1948

Samuel's son William was born in the lighthouse itself, delivered in the room now known as the Lantern Room suite. He survived two World Wars and the Great Depression at his post, guiding Allied convoys through the U-boat-infested North Atlantic and carving the names of every ship he guided safely into the keeper's log beam.

"Every ship that passes, I think — someone's son. Someone's father. The light is for them."
Third Era · 1948–1958

Eleanor Aldrich

Head Keeper, 1948–1958

The first female head keeper in the Maine lighthouse service, Eleanor Aldrich documented lighthouse life with extraordinary literary grace. Her published memoir, "The Light and the Sea," became a New England classic. The inn's library houses a first edition, and each room contains a framed excerpt from her writings.

"I learned everything I needed to know from this light: that darkness is not permanent, and that even the loneliest shore is still part of the sea."
What We Stand For

Our Guiding Principles

MERIDIAN was restored and opened by a team of historians, conservationists, and hospitality artisans who share a conviction: that the most extraordinary experiences come from places with genuine souls.

Historic Stewardship

Every original element — the brass Fresnel lens mechanism, the iron spiral stair, the keeper's logbook shelves — is preserved and explained. We are curators first, innkeepers second.

Ecological Responsibility

Our coastal location demands coastal conscience. Geothermal heating, rainwater harvesting, and a zero-plastic policy throughout the inn reflect our commitment to the ocean that makes MERIDIAN possible.

Radical Intimacy

Eight rooms. No conference rooms. No events. Just eight guests sharing one of the most storied promontories on the Atlantic coast — and the staff to staff ratio to give each guest undivided attention.
