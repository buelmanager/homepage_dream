The Distillery

Built to Last,
Weathered to Taste

"The sea doesn't care about your marketing. But it will get into your spirit if you let it."
— Cressida Tregothnan, Master Distiller

The building that houses SALTERN was constructed in 1919 from granite quarried three miles away. It was built as a salt-house and fish-curing facility for the Porthcurno fishing fleet. When Arthur Tregothnan converted it in 1973, he kept the structure unchanged — the sea-facing windows, the uninsulated walls, the stone floors that pull cold up from the earth even in August.

The decision was partly economic and partly instinctive. The walls breathe. The casks in our warehouse are subject to the same weather as the coastline 40 metres away. In winter, the humidity rises to 95% and the salt content of the ambient air is measurable. It gets into the wood. The wood gets into the spirit.

The Stills

Mabel, Ethel,
Joan & Edgar

Mabel
Hand-riveted Speyside, 1972 · Wash Still · 8,000L

The tallest of the original trio, Mabel's long neck creates a light, floral spirit by allowing heavy compounds to condense and fall back before reaching the lyne arm. She's been repaired twice — the same copper both times.

Ethel
Hand-riveted Speyside, 1972 · Spirit Still · 5,200L

Ethel runs the second distillation — taking Mabel's low wines and concentrating them to spirit. Her shorter, more rotund shape retains more heavy congeners, contributing SALTERN's characteristic richness at the mid-palate.

Edgar
Speyside, 2024 · Experimental Still · 3,000L

The newest arrival. Edgar is a smaller still for experimental batches — unusual grain sources, longer fermentations, high-proof expressions. Cressida named him for her late father. He hasn't run yet, but he will.
