The Craft

From Barley
to Bottle

01

Malting & Milling

West Cornish barley steeped in spring water, then spread on our traditional floor for 5 days. Hand-turned every 8 hours. Germination converts starch to fermentable sugar. The malt is then kiln-dried — lightly peated, or unpeated, depending on the expression — and milled to grist.

02

Mashing

The grist meets granite spring water in three temperature stages. The first water at 63°C extracts fermentable sugars. Subsequent waters recover residual sugars and are held for the next batch. The wort — sweet, faintly peaty, smelling of new bread — flows to the washbacks.

03

Fermentation

Wort ferments in Oregon pine washbacks for 72 hours with our house yeast strain — unchanged since 1973. The wash reaches 8% ABV. Long fermentation creates the fruity esters that give SALTERN its characteristic coastal freshness.

04

Distillation

Mabel takes the wash through first distillation. Ethel runs the low wines to spirit. The cut is made by Cressida by nose alone — no instrument measures this. The new-make spirit at 68% ABV is colourless, raw, and already unmistakably SALTERN.
