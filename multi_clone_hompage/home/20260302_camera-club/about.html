Our Origin

Born in a Darkroom Queue,
1996

The Aperture Society was founded in 1996 by six photographers who met repeatedly in the queue for the same darkroom at the local art college. They were not students — most had day jobs in architecture, medicine, and graphic design — but they shared the conviction that photography was a serious art form that demanded serious study.

Their early meetings were informal: they gathered each Thursday to show prints and argue about them. The critique was rigorous and occasionally uncomfortable. Out of those discomforts came better work. Within a year they had thirty members. Within five years they had produced their first public exhibition and established a permanent darkroom of their own.

Today the Society serves over 320 members across analogue, digital, and alternative process photography. Our annual exhibition, attended by over 2,000 people in 2024, is now recognised as one of the most significant amateur photography exhibitions in the region.

The Six Founders

Those Who Started It

Anna Fletcher
35mm Documentary

Architect and street photographer. Her 2003 series on urban demolition was the Society's first nationally reviewed exhibition work.

Richard Osei
Large Format Portrait

NHS radiologist who brought a scientist's attention to exposure and chemistry. First chairman of the Society, 1997–2004.

Mariana Kovacs
Darkroom & Alternative Process

Czech-born printmaker who introduced cyanotype and platinum-palladium printing to the Society's programme in 1998.
