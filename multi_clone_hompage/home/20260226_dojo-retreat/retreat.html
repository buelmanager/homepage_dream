Rooms

Twelve Rooms.
One Standard:
Simplicity.

Each of the twelve guest rooms was designed with the same principle that governs our programs: remove what is unnecessary, keep what serves. Rooms feature a hand-laid tatami floor, a cedar-framed single futon with organic buckwheat pillow, a tansu chest for belongings, and a small writing alcove with shoji screen opening to the garden.

There are no televisions, no desks, no work surfaces. The rooms are for sleeping, sitting, and looking at trees. The shared bathhouse uses water heated by a wood-fired system maintained by the resident caretaker. The private cedar onsen is available by hourly reservation.

  • Traditional tatami floors, restored annually
  • Organic cotton and silk bedding
  • Shoji screen sliding door to private veranda
  • Tokonoma alcove with seasonal arrangement
  • Shared bathhouse with hinoki wood soaking tub
  • Private cedar onsen (by reservation)
  • No Wi-Fi in guest rooms (common area only)
  • Wake bell delivered by resident monk at 05:15
Food & Nourishment

Shojin Ryori

We serve shojin ryori — traditional Buddhist temple cuisine. Plant-based, entirely seasonal, and prepared by a cook trained at a Zen monastery. Meals are not a break from practice. They are practice. We eat mindfully, in the correct posture, in silence during sesshin weeks.

朝 Morning Meal Asa Gohan — 07:30

Rice porridge (okayu) with pickled plum, miso soup with seasonal vegetables, roasted sesame, and barley tea. Eaten after morning zazen. The body is quiet. The food meets it where it is.

昼 Midday Meal Hiru Gohan — 12:30

The largest meal of the day. Seasonal tofu preparations, simmered root vegetables, mountain herbs, rice, and a composed small plate using whatever the garden has provided that morning.

夕 Evening Oryoki Yū Shoku — 19:00

Served in the oryoki (nested bowl) ceremonial format. Light: miso, pickles, grain. Each guest prepares and cleans their own bowls. The ritual slows the close of day intentionally.

When to Come

Four Seasons,
Four Teachings

MUSHIN holds programs across all four seasons. Each period has a distinct character that shapes the retreat experience in ways that cannot be manufactured.

春 Spring Haru March — May

Cherry blossoms fall through the cedar canopy. The light changes hourly. Spring is the season of beginners — everything appears possible, everything is fragile.

"Begin again, every morning."
夏 Summer Natsu June — August

Heat, cicada, and the smell of wet cedar after afternoon rain. Summer practice is demanding: the heat sits in the dojo and does not leave. There is no relief. There is only continuation.

"Endure. Then endure differently."
秋 Autumn Aki September — November

The most requested season. Maples turn red and gold among the cedars. Autumn is for refinement — the beginner's excitement is past, the practitioner begins to understand what they do not yet know.

"The most beautiful form is the one after ten thousand."
冬 Winter Fuyu December — February

Few request winter. Those who come rarely regret it. The cold strips the forest bare and does the same to the practitioner. The tatami is cold at 05:30. The body is awake in a way summer never creates.

"Cold is not an obstacle. It is the teacher."
Getting Here

How to Arrive

01
Fly to Osaka Kansai or Tokyo Haneda

Most international guests arrive at Kansai International (KIX), 75 minutes from Kyoto by express train. From Tokyo, the Shinkansen Nozomi reaches Kyoto in 2 hours 15 minutes.

02
Transfer to Kyoto Station

From KIX: take the Haruka Limited Express directly to Kyoto Station (75 min, reserved seat). From Tokyo: Shinkansen to Kyoto. We recommend arriving at Kyoto Station by 14:00 on your arrival day.

03
Our Driver Collects You

A MUSHIN car will meet you at the Karasuma Central Exit of Kyoto Station. Your driver holds a wooden sign with the MUSHIN enso mark. The journey to the temple takes approximately 40 minutes through the Ohara valley road.

04
Arrival at the Temple

You will be received at the main gate by a resident. Your shoes are left at the genkan. You are shown to your room. The first bell rings at 05:30 the following morning. The program has begun.
