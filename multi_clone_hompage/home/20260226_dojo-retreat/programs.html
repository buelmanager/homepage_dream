The Disciplines

Four Arts,
One Path

Each discipline at MUSHIN is a complete path. You may arrive drawn to the sword, the mat, or the cushion — but the destination is always the same. We offer focused single-discipline programs and the Full Immersion, which integrates all four arts into a structured fourteen-day residential journey.

合
The Way of Harmony

Aikido
合氣道

7-Day Intensive Program

Aikido at MUSHIN is taught in the Iwama-ryu tradition, emphasizing the inseparable relationship between taijutsu (empty-hand) and bukiwaza (weapons work). Sensei Nakamura's curriculum begins not with technique but with the principle: how does the body meet force without resistance and without collapse?

Each morning session covers basic kihon and paired practice. Afternoon sessions introduce jo and bokken work, completing the circle that Morihei Ueshiba intended between open-hand and weapons training. Evening lectures explore Aikido philosophy and its relationship to bushido ethics.

Duration 7 Days
Level All Welcome
Daily Hours 4–5 hrs mat
Group Size Max 8
  • Ukemi (breakfalling) and body positioning foundations
  • Ikkyo through Gokkyo — the fundamental pins
  • Koshinage and projection techniques
  • Jo and bokken paired kata (31-count, 13-count)
  • Morning Zazen integrated into daily structure

居
The Drawing Blade

Iaido
居合道

7-Day Study Program

Iaido is the art of the sword's transition — from stillness to movement, from sheathed to drawn, from thought to action. It is, at its core, a meditation practice. The sword is the focus object. Hayashi Sensei teaches that no one is ready to hold a blade until they understand what it means to be empty.

The program begins with three days of unarmed study — posture, breathing, and the mental state required before the practice even begins. Students then work through the Muso Shinden-ryu Omori-ryu curriculum, with special focus on chiburi and noto (the cleaning and resheathing sequences) as meditative acts in themselves.

Duration 7 Days
Level Beginner Welcome
Daily Hours 3–4 hrs
Blades Provided (Iaito)
  • Days 1–3: Unarmed posture, breathing, seiza study
  • Grip, draw, and the mechanics of the cut (kirioroshi)
  • Omori-ryu Shoden — the foundational 12 kata
  • Chiburi and noto as contemplative completion
  • Optional: practice with a shinken (live blade) for experienced students

坐
Seated Meditation

Zazen
坐禅

7-Day Silent Retreat

The Zazen retreat follows the Rinzai Zen format: four to six periods of seated meditation daily, walking kinhin between each period, and three individual dokusan sessions with Roshi Abe across the week. The schedule is deliberately demanding — not as an endurance test, but because sustained sitting is the only way to encounter the mind's actual character.

Noble silence is observed from the evening of Day 1 through the morning of Day 7. Meals are taken in the oryoki style — a structured ceremonial form that transforms eating into practice. The dokusan sessions are the program's most private element; what happens in that room stays in that room.

Duration 7 Days
Silence Days 1–6
Daily Sitting 5–6 hrs
Dokusan 3 Sessions
  • Posture and breathing instruction (Days 1–2)
  • Rinzai-style zazen: facing the wall, eyes half-open
  • Kinhin (walking meditation) between sitting periods
  • Oryoki ceremonial meals in the meditation hall
  • Three private dokusan interviews with Roshi Abe

道
Living Heritage

Traditional
Arts 伝統

5-Day Rotating Program

Not every path to mushin runs through combat. The traditional arts program rotates through four disciplines — Chado (tea ceremony), Shodo (calligraphy), Kyudo (archery), and Ikebana (flower arrangement) — spending roughly one day on each before a closing integration day.

Each art is presented not as a cultural experience but as a genuine practice: a discipline with its own vocabulary of attention, its own demands on the body and the mind. The instructor for each session is a licensed teacher of that specific art. The goal is not competence, but contact — the experience of what it is like to be fully present in a structured form.

Duration 5 Days
Arts Covered 4 Disciplines
Instructors Licensed Teachers
Group Size Max 8
  • Chado — Urasenke school. Full temae sequences, tea room etiquette
  • Shodo — Brush technique, ink preparation, classical kanji forms
  • Kyudo — The eight stages of shooting. The arrow as metaphor
  • Ikebana — Sogetsu school. Negative space and the living arrangement
  • Integration day: journaling, group discussion, closing ceremony
