The Atelier
of Echizen

Where water, fiber, and 1,200 years of knowing hands converge into a single sheet of paper.

Paper is not merely a surface. It is a landscape of fiber, water, and time — each sheet holding a moment of human patience.
Our Philosophy

FOLIO was founded by master papermaker Kenji Murakami and stationery designer Aiko Tanaka with a single conviction: that the most meaningful objects are those which carry the memory of their making.

We believe in the intelligence of slow craft — that a sheet of washi pulled by hand from cold mountain-spring water carries something no machine can replicate. That intelligence is what we seek to share, with every commission, every workshop, and every sheet we press.

Our atelier sits beside the Ota River in Echizen, where the same spring water that fed paper vats in the Nara Period still runs clear through our sukibune halls.

Our Town

Echizen — The Paper Capital
of Japan

In the mountains of Fukui Prefecture, a town has lived and breathed paper for thirteen centuries. This is where we were born, and where we make.

Echizen, Fukui Prefecture

Echizen paper is celebrated for its exceptional durability and beauty. The unique combination of pure mountain water, kozo fiber grown in the local hills, and generations of accumulated technique has produced a paper tradition unlike any other in the world.

Today, Echizen is home to approximately 70 paper-making studios, producing everything from delicate translucent sheets for shoji screens to robust archival paper used in museum conservation worldwide.

How We Work

From River Water
to Finished Sheet

Every FOLIO sheet follows the same ancient sequence — a process unchanged for over a millennium.

I
Fiber Preparation

Kozo bark is steamed, peeled, soaked in mountain-spring water, and beaten by hand until individual fibers separate and cloud the vat.

II
Sheet Pulling

The papermaker dips the suketa frame into the fiber vat, drawing it forward and back in a precise rolling motion that determines texture and weight.

III
Pressing & Couching

Sheets are stacked, pressed under stone weight overnight, then carefully separated and laid on cedar drying boards.

IV
Drying & Inspection

Boards dry in open air. Each sheet is hand-inspected for even fiber distribution, tears, and the unique surface quality that defines FOLIO washi.
