St. Bartholomew's Cathedral — East Window

Commissioned by the Diocese of London, the new East Window for St. Bartholomew's presented one of the most complex narrative programs in our history. Spanning 340 square metres across sixteen lancet lights, the glazing depicts the life of the patron saint in a contemporary idiom informed by medieval typological traditions.

Glass was sourced exclusively from Lamberts of Bavaria, with hand-painted grisaille detailing executed by Yuki Tanaka over eighteen months. The architectural integration required close collaboration with conservation architect Julian Marks of Feilden Mawson.

National Arts Centre — Light Atrium

Appointed through international competition, LUMINIS created a suspended abstract composition for the new National Arts Centre's central atrium. The work — entitled "Caledonian Spectrum" — references the colour palette of Scotland's ancient landscape through 180 square metres of hand-blown art glass.

The installation required the development of a bespoke structural support system in collaboration with Arup Engineers, capable of supporting 2.8 tonnes of glass while accommodating seismic movement allowances.
