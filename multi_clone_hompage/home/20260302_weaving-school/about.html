Our Heritage

About The
Loom School

Sixty years of unbroken textile tradition, rooted in the living weaving culture of Oaxaca and enriched by exchanges with master weavers on every continent.

A School Born from the Loom

The Loom School was not planned — it grew. When Elena Morales began gathering weavers in her courtyard in 1961, there was no curriculum, no faculty, no prospectus. There was only the loom, the thread, and the urgent sense that what these women knew must not be allowed to disappear.

Six decades later, the school has trained over 2,400 weavers from sixty countries. The courtyard has become a campus. The informal circle has become a faculty. But the urgency has never left: knowledge lives in hands, and hands must teach hands.

We remain, at our heart, a gathering place — where tradition meets curiosity, where the patience of the loom restores the impatient mind, and where every thread woven is a small act of cultural preservation.

Our Beliefs

What We Stand For

Preservation Over Innovation

Before we can innovate, we must understand what we have. Our curriculum insists on full mastery of traditional methods before any departure from them. Innovation built on ignorance is merely noise.

The Ethics of Learning

We teach weaving traditions that belong to living communities. Every programme includes study of the cultural contexts, intellectual property considerations and ethical obligations of working with inherited craft knowledge.

Slowness as Discipline

The loom cannot be hurried. The rhythm of shuttle and treadle imposes its own time — and in that imposed slowness, students typically find both the skill and the stillness they came looking for.

Material Knowledge

To weave well, you must know your materials — where the fibre grew, how it was prepared, what it will do under tension and heat. Our approach to textiles begins with raw materials and respects the entire chain of production.
