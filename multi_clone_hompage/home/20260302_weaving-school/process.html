The Loom School
Method

Our curriculum moves from material knowledge through structural mastery to colour literacy and original design. Each stage builds irreversibly on the last.

"The loom is not a machine — it is a logic. Once you understand its logic, every cloth you will ever weave is already inside you, waiting for your hands to find it." — Catalina Morales, Founding Director
01
Week 1–2

Thread: Fibre & Yarn Knowledge

Before touching the loom, students must understand the materials. Wool, cotton, silk, linen, bast fibres — each has its own behaviour under tension, its own response to moisture, its own relationship with dye. We spend two weeks handling, measuring, breaking and understanding yarn before we weave a single row.

02
Week 3–5

Architecture: Dressing the Loom

The warp is the invisible architecture of every cloth. Students learn to calculate sett, measure and wind warp, thread heddles and sley the reed. Both back-to-front and front-to-back warping methods are taught and compared. Tie-up and treadling notation are introduced as a universal language of weave structure.

03
Week 6–10

Structure: Weave Families

Plain weave, twill, satin, overshot, Summer and Winter, double weave — each belongs to a family with its own logic, visual character and practical use. Students work through each family in focused sampling sessions, building both technical understanding and an intuitive sense of what each structure wants to become.

04
Week 11–16

Colour: The Dyehouse

Students move from the weaving studio to the dyehouse, where they learn mordanting, plant extraction, overdyeing and resist techniques using cochineal, indigo, weld, madder, black walnut and logwood. Each student builds a dye notebook that will serve as a personal reference for the rest of their practice.

05
Week 17–20

Voice: Final Project

Everything converges in the final project: one cloth, fully conceived and fully woven by the student. Structure chosen for intention, yarn selected for purpose, colour developed in the dyehouse. The finished piece is exhibited alongside a written design statement. This is where technique becomes expression.
