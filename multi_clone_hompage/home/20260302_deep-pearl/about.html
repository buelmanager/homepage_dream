The Atelier

People of
the Deep

"A pearl is not made in a factory. It grows in silence, in the dark, under pressure. Our work is to earn the right to hold it."

ABYSSÉ was founded in 1987 by Mira Okafor on a singular conviction: that the finest pearls in the world exist at depths no commercial diver had ever reached, and that only someone willing to go there could find them.

Today, ABYSSÉ employs sixteen people across our Broome diving operations and atelier. Each member of the team shares a relationship with the sea — some born to it, others drawn by curiosity. All bound by respect for its gifts.

The People

Artisans & Divers

Mira Okafor
Founder & Head Diver

Mira holds three world records for breath-hold diving depth. After 38 years, she still dives every pearl harvest season, trusting only her own hands to select the finest specimens from the beds she discovered.

Thomas Adeyemi
Master Jeweller

Trained in Valenza, Italy, Thomas joined ABYSSÉ in 2003. His approach to setting pearls is sculptural — treating each mounting as a conversation between metal and nacre rather than mere containment.

Yuki Tanaka
Pearl Grader

Yuki's father was a Mikimoto grader in Mie Prefecture. She brought that tradition to ABYSSÉ — a 47-point grading protocol that ensures every pearl entering our atelier meets the highest nacre standards.

Our Values

What We Believe

01

Patience

A pearl takes 7 years. We take as long as needed. Every commission, every harvest, every setting follows its own necessary pace. We never rush what the ocean teaches you to wait for.

02

Transparency

We publish full supply chain documentation for every batch of pearls. We believe luxury and accountability are not in tension — they are inseparable. Our RJC certification is renewed annually.

03

Singularity

No two ABYSSÉ pieces are identical. The pearl dictates the design. We do not manufacture; we interpret. Every jewel is signed by the person who made it — permanently, inside the setting.

04

Ocean Stewardship

We plant three native oysters for every one we harvest. Our diving practices are zero-net-impact. The Kimberly coast is our heritage — we are its custodians, not its exploiters.
