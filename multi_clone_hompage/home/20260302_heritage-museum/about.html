About the Museum

160 years of collecting, conserving, and interpreting the material evidence of human civilisation.

Founded 1864

A Century and a Half of Heritage

Founded by Royal Charter in 1864 at the height of the great age of archaeological discovery, The National Heritage Museum was established to collect, preserve, and interpret the artefacts emerging from excavations across the ancient world. Our founding director, Sir Edmund Blackwell, articulated a vision that remains our guiding principle today: that the national heritage belongs to the people, and that the Museum's role is to act as their custodian.

Over 160 years, our collection has grown from 4,200 founding objects to more than two million. Our building, originally a modest Victorian hall, has expanded across an entire city block. Our reach, once confined to visiting scholars, now extends to millions worldwide through our digital collection and online learning resources.

Through two world wars, economic upheavals, and the profound changes of the post-colonial era, the Museum has adapted — grappling honestly with the complex histories of its own collection and working with source communities to reframe the stories we tell.

Principles

What We Stand For

I

Universal Access

Free admission to the permanent collection is a non-negotiable commitment. The national heritage must be freely accessible to every citizen, regardless of means.

II

Scholarly Integrity

Our interpretation is grounded in rigorous scholarship. We commit to representing the complexity and contested nature of history honestly and without simplification.

III

Provenance & Ethics

We are committed to researching the full provenance of every object in our collection and engaging honestly with the difficult histories of acquisition and repatriation.

IV

Long-term Stewardship

Our obligation is not to the present but to all future generations. We hold the collection in trust — preserving, researching, and interpreting it for centuries to come.
