About Open Canvas Studio

Who We Are

Open Canvas Studio is a disability arts collective that provides adaptive creative expression, art therapy, and community exhibition programs for disabled artists of all disciplines and diagnoses.

We exist because disabled artists deserve more than access ramps and sign language interpreters at other people's events. We are a studio built from the ground up with access as its primary design brief — a place where disabled artists come to make serious work, not to be included in someone else's vision.

Founded in 2012 by a group of disabled artists and arts facilitators who were frustrated with the marginal inclusion of disability in mainstream arts programming, we have grown from a weekly community centre session to a nationally-recognised studio with 220 members and twelve annual exhibitions.

Founding Story

Built by Artists, for Artists

The studio was conceived over coffee by Priya Mehta — a visual artist with chronic fatigue syndrome — and Leo Andrade — a sculptor who uses a power wheelchair. Both had spent years navigating arts spaces that were theoretically accessible but practically exhausting.

They wanted a studio where the question was not "can we accommodate you?" but "what do you need to make your best work?" They recruited ten other disabled artists, negotiated a rolling hire arrangement with a community centre, and launched in January 2012.

Priya remains the studio's Artistic Director. Leo serves as Head of Access Design — a role that ensures every new program, tool, or policy is evaluated first for its accessibility implications.

Our Values

What We Stand For

01

Artist-Led

Disabled artists lead our programming, curation, and governance. Access to leadership is as important as access to studio space.

02

Access as Creativity

Adaptive practice is not a workaround — it is a creative act. The innovations that emerge from designing for access often produce the most interesting art.

03

Serious Work

We hold our artists to the same standard as any professional studio. The work is not therapeutic in intent — it is art, full stop.

Our History

A Timeline

2012

Founded — The Wednesday Studio

Twelve artists, a community centre, and a shared determination to make something serious.

2015

First Public Exhibition

Twenty-two artists show work publicly for the first time. Three pieces acquired by local gallery collection.

2018

Dedicated Studio Opens

Purpose-built, fully accessible studio designed by disabled artists. National press coverage.

2021

Digital Arts Program Launches

Switch-access and voice-controlled digital arts extends participation for artists unable to attend in person.

2025

National Arts Bursary Award

220 members. 12 annual exhibitions. Recognised as the leading disability arts collective in the country.
