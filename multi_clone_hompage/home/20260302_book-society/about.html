Our Story

Seventy-Seven
Years of Letters

Founded in a Bloomsbury flat by six booksellers and professors, the Folio Society of Letters has grown into one of London's most distinguished private literary communities.

We began with a conviction that serious reading deserves a serious community — and 77 years later, that conviction has never been stronger.

How We Began

A Flat in Bloomsbury,
1947

It was the winter of 1947, and six friends who happened to share a passion for serious reading decided that London needed a literary society worthy of its tradition. They met in a Bloomsbury flat, argued about Proust, and by the end of the evening had founded the Folio Society of Letters.

The founding principle was simple: the best books deserve the best readers, and the best readers deserve each other's company. The reading room opened fifteen years later, and what began as six friends meeting in a flat became a community of hundreds.

A book is a dream that you hold in your hand. This society was founded to help more people dream more deeply.

Today we are 340 members strong, with a library of 4,200 carefully curated titles, a programme of over 40 events each year, and a Rare Books Archive that would be the envy of any institution.

Our Council

The Readers
Who Lead Us

Professor Margaret Ellis
Society President

Emeritus Professor of English Literature at University College London, author of eight books on the Victorian novel, and a member of the Folio Society since 1988.

Oliver Fairfax
Librarian & Archive Curator

Former senior cataloguer at the British Library, Oliver has overseen the growth of the Folio collection from 2,100 to 4,200 titles over the past eighteen years.

Clara Ashworth
Director of Programmes

Clara curates the society's annual programme, coordinates author talks, and has brought over 60 celebrated writers to the reading room since joining in 2015.
