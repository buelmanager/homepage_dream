About the Atelier

An Institution
Built on Resistance

Atelier des Beaux-Arts was founded on the conviction that formal art education had become an obstacle. Nearly a century later, we remain defined by the question: what does it actually take?

Our Story

From a Printshop
to a Global Standard

Émile Vasseur opened the Atelier in a rented printshop in 1926 with no curriculum, no examinations, and no degrees. He had one rule: make work every day and defend it honestly. That rule still governs us.

The school grew not through marketing but through reputation. Artists who had trained here became known for something specific — a willingness to be uncomfortable, a refusal of easy resolution, a seriousness about the material. Gallerists in Paris, Berlin, and New York began to look for the Atelier in a CV.

Today we accept twenty to thirty students per year, drawn from fifty countries. The building on Rue des Abbesses has been expanded four times, but the north-facing skylights that Vasseur first rented for their light remain unchanged. The light was always the point.

What We Believe

The Principles
That Guide Us

01

Smallness Is a Virtue

We deliberately cap enrolment. Every student must be known to every tutor. When a school becomes too large to remember its students, it has become something other than a school.

02

Making Precedes Theory

We teach theory through practice, not before it. Students arrive at ideas through the friction of the work itself. Abstraction without material experience is merely language.

03

Critique Is Care

We are honest. We do not protect students from the full force of rigorous criticism. We believe the only respectful response to a serious work is a serious engagement with its failure as well as its success.
