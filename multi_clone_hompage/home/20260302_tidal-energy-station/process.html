From Ocean Survey to Power Grid

A tidal energy station represents years of hydrographic research, precision engineering, and environmental stewardship. Our five-phase process ensures every installation meets the highest standards of performance and ecological responsibility.

01

Tidal Resource Assessment

We begin with comprehensive oceanographic surveys of candidate sites, deploying acoustic Doppler current profilers and tidal gauges for 12–18 months to characterize flow velocities, directionality, and seasonal variation.

Sites must demonstrate sustained velocities exceeding 2.5 m/s during peak flow periods to qualify for development.

02

Marine Environment Study

Baseline ecological surveys document the existing marine habitats, fish populations, marine mammal routes, and benthic communities at each candidate site. This informs turbine placement, rotation speed limits, and operational schedules to minimize wildlife impact.

03

Turbine Engineering & Fabrication

Each TIDALUX turbine is custom-engineered for its specific site, optimizing rotor diameter, blade pitch, and generator specification for peak efficiency at the measured flow velocities. Fabrication takes place at our facility in Aberdeen, Scotland.

04

Seabed Installation

Our specialist marine construction vessels deploy gravity-base foundations or pile-mounted towers depending on seabed conditions. Turbines are typically installed and commissioned within a four-week weather window.

05

Grid Connection & Operation

Subsea cables carry power to onshore substations, where it enters the national grid. Remote monitoring systems in our Aberdeen control center track every turbine in real time, with predictive maintenance algorithms flagging potential issues before they impact generation.
