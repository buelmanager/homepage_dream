The Process

Five stages from mountain to collector

Every Crestille piece follows an unbroken chain from mineral extraction to final documentation. No shortcuts. No substitutions. The piece is only complete when its geological story is as fully expressed as its setting.

The process takes 4–12 months depending on the commission. We photograph every stage and maintain an archive that outlasts the piece itself.

Stage by Stage

The Five Stages

01

Mineral Prospecting

Élise prospects specific alpine veins each season with a geologist and photographer. Over 80% of what we extract is discarded for inadequate optical clarity or structural weakness. Only specimens that exceed our threshold enter the design process.

02

Spectroscopic Analysis

Selected crystals receive full spectroscopic, refractive index, and inclusion mapping analysis. This data is the foundation of the provenance certificate — and directly informs the setting design by revealing the stone's optical axes.

03

Setting Design

Each setting is drawn specifically for the individual mineral — no templates, no standardized mounts. Precious metal choice follows from the stone's color frequency. Every prong position is calculated against the crystal's cleavage planes.

04

Hand Setting

All setting work is performed by Élise under 10x magnification. 20–60 hours per piece. Prong geometry distributes stress evenly across the crystal's cleavage planes, ensuring structural security without constraining the stone's optical freedom.

05

Documentation & Delivery

Every piece ships with a full geological and craft certificate. Origin coordinates, formation depth, spectroscopic analysis, setting specifications, and maker's signature — in a sealed archive document that registers the piece permanently.

Materials

Three Mineral Families

Beryl Family

Aquamarine and emerald varieties from high-altitude veins in the Mont Blanc and Dolomite massifs. The cold-growth conditions produce unusually clear specimens with deep color saturation.

Source: 1,800–2,800m Alpine Zone

Tourmaline

Parti-colored and elbaite tourmalines from the French and Italian Alps. The parti-color gradient — green to pink — follows the crystal's growth axis and cannot be replicated by heating or irradiation.

Source: Chamonix & Aosta Valley

Quartz Group

Smoky quartz, clear alpha quartz, and citrine from specific high-altitude pockets. Natural irradiation at altitude produces the smoky coloration without any treatment — the color is geological, not induced.

Source: 1,600–2,600m Veins
