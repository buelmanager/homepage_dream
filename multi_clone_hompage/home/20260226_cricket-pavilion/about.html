The History.

Est. 1891

A Brotherhood
Forged in Play

The Crease was founded in the summer of 1891 by twelve English gentlemen who believed that cricket was not merely a game — it was a philosophy, a code of conduct, and a way of understanding the world.

They purchased eleven acres of Berkshire farmland, engaged the architect Reginald Hartley-Pierce to design a pavilion in the Arts and Crafts manner, and laid two cricket pitches by hand before the season's end.

"To bat is to know patience. To bowl is to know strategy. To field is to know brotherhood."

— Sir Edmund Cartwright-Hall, Founding Member, 1891

The Twelve Founders

In the summer of 1891, twelve men of the same conviction gathered in Berkshire and established what would become England's most exclusive private cricket institution.

EC
Sir Edmund Cartwright-Hall
First President
Yorkshire-born barrister. Captained the club 1891–1908. Author of The Crease Charter.
WM
William Montague-Firth
First Secretary
Keeper of the original scorebooks. His calligraphy still decorates the Long Room wall.
AR
Archibald Rowlandson
Grounds Patron
A gentleman farmer who donated the original eleven acres. The Meadow Ground bears his initial in the pavilion stone.
JB
James Beauchamp-Lyons
Head Coach (Original)
A former county professional from Middlesex who coached the first XI and established coaching doctrine still used today.

Grade II Listed

The Pavilion Building

Designed in 1891 in the Arts and Crafts manner by Reginald Hartley-Pierce, the pavilion building is a masterpiece of understated English craftsmanship. Red Berkshire brick, hand-carved stone detailing, and the famous covered veranda overlooking the Pavilion Ground.

The interior was redesigned in 1953 when the Long Room was extended, and restored faithfully to the original vision between 2006 and 2008. Today it is one of the finest examples of sporting architecture in England.

The Crease Academy

Established in 1968, The Crease Academy has produced some of England's most distinguished cricketers. Our coaching faculty comprises former Test players who are committed to the classical traditions of the game — technique, character, and respect for the pitch.
