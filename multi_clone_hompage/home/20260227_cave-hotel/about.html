Stone, Time
& Civilization

Five millennia of unbroken human habitation beneath Cappadocia

Our Foundation

Born from
Ancient Stone

GROTTO exists because of a singular geological miracle: the volcanic eruption of Mount Erciyes and Mount Hasan approximately 60 million years ago deposited thick layers of ash and lava across the Cappadocia plateau. Over millennia, this soft rock — called volcanic tuff — was sculpted by wind and water into the extraordinary fairy chimney formations that define the landscape today.

Early inhabitants discovered that tuff was remarkably easy to carve by hand, yet hardened when exposed to air. They began cutting dwelling chambers into the rock perhaps as early as 3000 BC — and never stopped. Every civilization that passed through Cappadocia — Hittite, Persian, Greek, Roman, Byzantine, Seljuk, Ottoman — added its layer to the stone.

When our founders first descended into the chambers that would become GROTTO, they encountered not empty rock but a living archive: 3,000 years of carved niches, painted walls, worn thresholds, and smoke-blackened ceilings. The restoration took twelve years. The result is what you find today.

Geology

The Science
of Our Stone

The volcanic tuff that forms GROTTO's walls is among the most extraordinary building materials on Earth — not carved, not poured, but born. Its properties make it uniquely suited to human habitation across centuries.

01

Thermal Stability

Tuff maintains a constant 18°C interior temperature year-round regardless of external conditions, providing natural air conditioning in summer and warmth in winter.

02

Acoustic Dampening

The porous mineral structure of volcanic tuff absorbs sound with exceptional efficiency, creating the profound silence that makes GROTTO's underground experience unique.

03

Mineral Richness

Veins of obsidian, pumice, and rare volcanic minerals run through our cave walls, each one a record of distinct eruption events stretching back 60 million years.

04

Air Quality

Negative ion concentration underground is measurably higher than at surface level, contributing to the deeply restful sleep that guests consistently report at GROTTO.

Chronology

Five Thousand Years
in the Stone

Neolithic First Carvers

Nomadic peoples of the central Anatolian plateau discover that tuff can be carved with obsidian tools and hardened in open air. The first deliberate cave dwellings are cut into the valley walls of what is now Göreme.

3000 BC
1200 BC

Hittite Fortifications

The Hittite Empire carves underground cities across Cappadocia as military refuges. Deep passages, ventilation shafts, and communal storage chambers are cut to depths of sixty meters below the surface.

Persian Caravans

Under Achaemenid Persian rule, Cappadocian cave complexes serve as way stations on the Royal Road from Sardis to Susa. Merchants rest their horses in carved stable caves still visible in our eastern passage.

550 BC
100 AD

Roman Provincial Life

Roman Cappadocia becomes a wealthy province. The caves are expanded into comfortable dwellings with carved columns, arched doorways, and barrel-vaulted ceilings reflecting Greco-Roman architectural ideals.

Byzantine Sanctuary

Early Christians fleeing Roman persecution carve sanctuaries and hermitages into the Göreme valley. The iconic rock-cut churches with their extraordinary fresco programs — some of which remain visible in our suites — date from this era.

400 AD
1180 AD

Seljuk Silk Road

The Seljuk Sultanate of Rum constructs a network of caravanserai across Anatolia. The Cappadocian caves are adapted as comfortable resting quarters for Silk Road merchants traveling between China and Constantinople.

Ottoman Village Life

Local Greek and Turkish communities inhabit the cave dwellings as permanent homes for centuries. The caves are whitewashed, elaborately decorated, and connected by carved passages into the extended family networks still visible in the rock.

1500 AD
2007

GROTTO Restoration Begins

Our founders, working with UNESCO conservation specialists and traditional tuff craftsmen from Göreme village, begin twelve years of meticulous restoration of the cave complex that would become GROTTO.

GROTTO Opens

After twelve years of restoration — the longest and most expensive cave hotel restoration in Turkish history — GROTTO opens its fifteen suites to the world. The cave complex earns five-star designation from the Turkish Ministry of Culture and Tourism.

The Founders

Guardians of the Stone

AK

Ahmet Karadağ

Co-Founder & Cave Architect

A native of Göreme village whose family has inhabited cave dwellings for seven generations. Ahmet trained as an architect in Istanbul before returning to Cappadocia to restore the family caves. His intimate knowledge of tuff geology forms the foundation of every structural decision at GROTTO.

SR

Sofia Renard

Co-Founder & Creative Director

A French archaeologist and interior designer who first visited Cappadocia on a UNESCO fresco documentation project in 2004. Sofia spent three years living underground in the caves before co-founding GROTTO, developing the design philosophy that honors the cave's geological narrative without compromise.

MÇ

Murat Çelik

Master Stone Craftsman

The last practicing master of the traditional Cappadocian tuff carving technique, handed down through his family since the Seljuk period. Murat trained a team of twelve artisans in the ancient methods of cave carving, shaping every surface at GROTTO with tools unchanged for centuries.
