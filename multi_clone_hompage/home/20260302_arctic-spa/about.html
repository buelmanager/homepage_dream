About BOREAS

Born at the Edge of the Arctic Circle

BOREAS was founded in 1987 by Halvard Eriksson, a Norwegian marine biologist who had spent three winters studying the physiological adaptations of Arctic marine mammals. His central observation: animals that thrived in glacial waters possessed extraordinary circulatory resilience — and that resilience could be cultivated in humans through deliberate cold exposure.

The location Halvard chose was not accidental. The Lofoten Islands sit above the Arctic Circle, where the Gulf Stream meets the Norwegian Sea — creating an extraordinary microclimate of geothermal activity beneath freezing surface temperatures. Deep bore wells reveal mineral springs at 40°C rising just meters from glacial fjord water at 3°C.

The Discovery of the Springs

In the winter of 1982, Halvard discovered the geothermal springs while surveying a collapsed sea cave. The water that emerged was unlike anything tested in the region — carrying dissolved silica, magnesium, and sulfur compounds at concentrations three times higher than Iceland's famous Blue Lagoon.

Five years of hydrological study, guest trials, and collaboration with the University of Tromsø medical faculty confirmed what local fishermen had known for generations: the springs had remarkable healing properties, particularly for inflammatory conditions of the skin and joints.

Four Decades of Practice

Today BOREAS welcomes 6,000 guests annually from more than 40 countries — each one guided through our arctic wellness protocol by therapists trained in cold physiology, hydrotherapy, and Nordic body-work traditions. The waiting list for our signature seven-day retreat extends to 18 months.

We have never expanded beyond our original four pools. Intimacy is not a constraint — it is the product itself.
