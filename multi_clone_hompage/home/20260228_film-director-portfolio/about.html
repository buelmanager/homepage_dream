Biography

A Life in Frames

Marcus V. Reyes began his relationship with cinema not in a classroom, but in the projection booth of a crumbling repertory theater in East Los Angeles, where his uncle threaded 35mm prints of Kubrick and Tarkovsky for audiences who had no idea they were witnessing the formation of a director.

After formal training at the AFI Conservatory, Reyes spent three years shooting short documentaries in West Africa and Eastern Europe — work that would permanently calibrate his eye toward the grammar of available light and the poetry of silence.

His debut feature, Hollow Mountain (2018), premiered at AFI Fest and announced a director willing to take uncompromising formal risks in service of emotional truth. The film's seventeen-minute single-take sequence remains one of the most discussed sustained shots in American independent cinema of the decade.

Since then, Reyes has directed six features, eleven shorts, and a growing body of commercial work for brands including Maison Celeste and Noir Collective — each project an extension of the same restless visual inquiry that began in that East LA projection booth.

Cinematic Vision

What Cinema Is

"I am not interested in showing the world as it is. I am interested in showing the world as it feels at 3am when the truth becomes unavoidable."

On Darkness

Darkness is not an absence of light. It is the presence of everything we cannot name. I build visual spaces where the shadows carry as much emotional information as the lit surfaces — often more. The ratio of dark to light in any given frame is a moral statement about what the director believes deserves to be seen.

On Genre

Genre is a set of audience expectations. I use those expectations the way a musician uses a key signature — as a framework within which to operate, and occasionally, violate. The most interesting cinematic moments occur when a film does exactly the opposite of what its genre promises, and the audience forgives you for it.

On Performance

The camera sees everything the actor is thinking. Every flicker of uncertainty, every suppressed impulse, every moment where the character and the actor briefly separate — the lens records all of it. My job as a director is to create the conditions in which truth becomes the only option available to the performer.

On Film Stock

I shoot on 35mm whenever the project and budget allow. Not from nostalgia, but because film grain is temporal — it moves differently at 24 frames per second. Digital is clean; analog breathes. When I watch a film print projected, I am watching time itself rendered visible, one silver crystal at a time.

Influences

Mentors & Influences

Andrei Tarkovsky
Director — Formative Influence
The poet-director who proved that cinema could operate at the speed of consciousness rather than the speed of plot. "Sculpting in Time" remains the only book about filmmaking I have read more than three times.
Harlan Geller
Director of Photography — Mentor
AFI professor who taught me that the meter reading is the last thing you check — not the first. Showed me how available light, respected rather than fought, always tells the truer story.
Kelly Reichardt
Director — Sundance Lab Mentor
The director who convinced me that patience and restraint are not weaknesses but the most sophisticated tools in a filmmaker's kit. "Slow cinema" is a poor label for what is actually the most demanding cinema.
Agnès Varda
Director — Posthumous Influence
The most freely curious filmmaker who ever lived. Varda taught me — through her films — that documentary and fiction are not opposites but siblings, and that the camera is always a love letter to what it points at.
Roger Deakins
Cinematographer — Craft Influence
The DP who proved that technical mastery in service of story is the highest form of cinematic intelligence. Every Deakins frame is an argument for why cinematography is a narrative art, not just a technical one.
Pablo Larraín
Director — Sundance Lab Mentor
Taught me that historical material is always about the present — that the past is a lens through which we examine the anxieties we cannot yet name. His formal audacity gave me permission to trust my own instincts.
