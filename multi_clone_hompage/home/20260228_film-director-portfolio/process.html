METHOD
How We Work

The Process

Four phases. Months of preparation. Infinite attention to the invisible details that separate a sequence from a scene, a scene from a film.

01
Phase One

Pre-Production

A film is won or lost in development. The choices made before a camera is ever unpacked — script analysis, location scouting, visual research, casting — determine ninety percent of what will appear on screen. We spend more time here than anywhere else.

→
Visual ResearchWeeks of reference gathering — paintings, photography, architecture, precedent films. We build a look book before we write a single shot list.
→
Location ScoutingEvery interior is lit by its existing architecture. We choose locations that already tell the story — then we augment rather than replace what's there.
→
Camera & Lens TestsAt least three days of camera and lens testing before principal photography. Stock selection, lens character, filtration — all decided with full tests, never assumptions.
Pre-production · Location Scout
02
Phase Two

Production

On set, the director's job is to create the conditions in which something true can happen. We maintain a calm, focused environment. We do not rush. We allow time for the unexpected, because the unexpected is frequently where the film lives.

→
Single Camera ApproachWe shoot with a single camera, always. Multiple cameras fragment the actor's concentration. The performance that happens once, in a single direction, is always the truest.
→
Available Light PriorityWe begin every scene by asking what the existing light already provides. We add only what the story demands. Practical sources are our preference.
→
Extended Rehearsal TimeActors are given substantial rehearsal time before the camera is ever on. Blocking evolves with the performer. We do not impose movement; we discover it.
Production · Lighting Setup
03
Phase Three

Post-Production

Editing is the second writing of the film. Color grading is its final painting. We work through a minimum of eight assembly cuts with our editor before touching color — because the structure must be right before the texture is set.

→
Collaborative EditorialDirector remains present throughout the entire editorial process. We cut together with the editor rather than reviewing cuts in isolation.
→
Sound Design IntegrationSound design begins during picture edit. We work with our sound designer in parallel, not sequentially — because the sound shapes the cut.
→
Film-Out GradeFor 35mm projects, we scan and grade directly from negative. For digital, we work in ACES with a custom LUT developed during pre-production camera tests.
Post-Production · Editorial
04
Phase Four

Distribution

A film without an audience is a private dream. We work closely with distributors and festival programmers to ensure each project finds its rightful audience — in cinemas, at festivals, and eventually at home.

→
Festival StrategyWe develop a targeted festival strategy for each project. Feature films are held from all streaming platforms until after theatrical and festival runs.
→
Theatrical PresentationAll films are delivered in DCP format, calibrated for theatrical projection. We attend screenings whenever possible and supervise first-run projection.
→
Archival MastersEvery project produces an archival master in its highest quality format. Films are preserved as if they will be screened one hundred years from now.
Distribution · Theatrical Release

Typical Feature Timeline

From Greenlight to Screen

Months 1–3

Development & Prep

Script work, visual research, initial department head meetings, location surveys, casting process begins. First look book assembled.

Months 4–5

Camera & Lens Tests

Full camera, lens, and stock tests. Grade palette established. Lighting diagrams for key scenes completed. Final cast confirmed.

Months 6–8

Principal Photography

Typically 30–45 shooting days for a feature. Daily rushes reviewed. Editorial begins during production.

Months 9–12

Post-Production

Minimum 8 editorial passes. Sound design overlapping with picture edit. Color grade (minimum 10 days). DCP master.

Month 12+

Festival & Release

Festival submission strategy. World premiere at target festival. Theatrical release. Streaming window (minimum 12 months post-theatrical).
