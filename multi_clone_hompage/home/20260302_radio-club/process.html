Getting Licensed

Your Path
to the Air

Five-Step Journey

From Zero
to On the Air

Our structured training program is designed to take complete beginners from their first visit to their first licensed QSO. Every step is supported by experienced elmers who love sharing the hobby.

01

Visit the Club

No experience needed
Come to any Monday evening open house at the club station. You'll meet active operators, get a tour of the shack, listen to live contacts, and receive a welcome packet with licensing study resources. No obligation — just show up and listen.
02

Study the Material

4–8 weeks typical
Join our dedicated study group which meets Thursday evenings. Members use the ARRL Technician manual and online practice tools. Our elmers cover FCC regulations, radio wave theory, antenna basics, and operating procedures in a relaxed group setting — at your own pace.
03

Pass the Exam

Monthly sessions
Frequency ARC hosts FCC examination sessions on the first Saturday of each month. Our team of Volunteer Examiners will guide you through FCC registration, the exam administration, and submission. Technician and General class exams available. Most members pass on their first attempt after our study group.
04

Get On the Air

Immediate access
Once your callsign appears in the FCC database — typically 48 hours after passing — you're licensed to transmit. Club members have full access to the W0FRQ station during supervised sessions. Our elmers will help you make your first contact on the evening net, guide you through proper operating procedure, and get your logbook started.
05

Upgrade & Excel

Ongoing
The hobby grows with you. Technician class opens VHF/UHF. General opens most HF privileges. Extra class opens the entire frequency allocation. We offer upgrade study groups for all levels, plus specialist tracks in digital modes, emergency communications, antenna theory, and SDR experimentation.
License Classes

Three Levels
of Privilege

Technician

Entry-level license. Full VHF/UHF privileges and limited HF access on 10 metres. Perfect for local repeater operation, emergency communications, APRS, and satellite contacts. 35-question exam from a public pool.

Entry Level

General

Significant HF privileges added — 80m, 40m, 20m, 15m, 10m and more. Opens up long-distance DX contacts and contest participation. 35-question exam. The most common target for our members after Technician.

Intermediate

Amateur Extra

Full frequency privileges across all allocated amateur bands. Access to the exclusive 60-question Extra class exam segments. Required for certain advanced operations and for serving as a Volunteer Examiner.
