Gold Leaf Frames

The picture frame is not a border — it is a threshold between the world and the image. A properly gilded frame, burnished by hand, creates a resonance between the gold and the painting that no reproduction can suggest.

We work with all periods and profiles: Baroque swept, Neoclassical, Regency, Biedermeier, and contemporary clean-line. New frames are made to commission; existing frames are restored to their original specification where documentation allows.

  • Water or oil gilding in 22k, 23k, or 24k leaf to client specification
  • Custom bole colours: terra rossa, yellow, black, or grey to period specification
  • Burnished, matt, or mixed finish; distressed or pristine
  • UV-stable protective wax coating as standard
  • Period-appropriate profile replication from archive drawings
Pricing: from £480 per linear metre — detailed quotation provided after object assessment
02 — Furniture Gilding
02

Furniture Gilding

Gilded furniture requires the gilder to think across time — the object was made once, has lived for decades or centuries, and must be returned to a state that honours both its origins and its use. Surface preparation is as important as the leaf itself.

We gild console tables, chairs, mirrors, cabinets, and beds in styles from Louis XIV through Art Deco and contemporary design commissions. Repairs and full regild are equally within our remit.

  • Full regild or partial restoration matched to surviving original finish
  • Oil gilding for furniture that must tolerate handling and movement
  • Water gilding for showcase and salon pieces requiring maximum brilliance
  • Carved detail gilding with gilding tips and specialist cushion work
  • White gold, silver, and palladium leaf available for modern commissions
Pricing: by commission — from £2,200 for a single chair; suite pricing available
03 — Art Restoration
03

Art Restoration

Conservation gilding is the discipline of minimal intervention — understanding what a gilded surface has lost, what it retains, and what must be preserved unchanged. We work to international conservation ethics: reversibility, documentation, and consolidation before restoration.

Our conservation laboratory is equipped with UV illumination for detecting prior restoration campaigns, binocular microscopy for cross-section analysis, and a full archive of traditional materials matched to historical sources.

  • Condition survey and photographic documentation before any intervention
  • Consolidation of flaking leaf with reversible Paraloid B-72 or animal glue
  • Loss compensation in new leaf matched to original metal content and karat
  • Icon panel restoration including gesso, bole, and raised halos
  • Full treatment report provided as standard — essential for insurance and provenance
Pricing: by assessment — condition survey at no charge for institutional clients
04 — Architectural Gilding
04

Architectural Gilding

Gilding at architectural scale transforms a space. A gilded dome changes the quality of light within a room for every hour of the day. A gilded cornice makes a ceiling expand. We have completed commissions ranging from a single ornamental panel to 1,200 square metres of gilded plaster ceiling.

All architectural gilding projects begin with a site survey. We assess surface chemistry, humidity, temperature variation, and light. The gilding system — oil, mordant, burnished water — is selected only after this analysis.

  • Site survey and technical specification document included
  • External architectural gilding in 23k or 24k leaf with weather-sealed finish
  • Interior burnished water gilding for domes, ceilings, and cornices
  • Scripted staging and sequencing for occupied buildings
  • Full photographic survey at completion for insurance and maintenance records
Pricing: from £380 per m² — site survey provided before quotation at no cost
Materials

Six Gold Leaf Types

The character of gold changes with karat and alloy composition. We maintain stock of all six leaf types and advise each client on the most appropriate choice for their object and intended environment.

24 Karat — Deep Yellow
24k / 99.9% pure

The purest gold leaf, warm and richly coloured. Standard for icon panels, sacred objects, and any work requiring the most traditional appearance. Does not tarnish.

Best for: icons, sacred art, burnished frames
23 Karat — Lemon Gold
23.5k / silver alloy

A slight silver content produces a cooler, more luminous tone. The preferred leaf for French period frames and much eighteenth-century European furniture.

Best for: period frames, French furniture, architectural interiors
22 Karat — Warm Gold
22k / copper alloy

Copper in the alloy produces a warmer, slightly redder tone. Often used for contrast effects alongside 23k leaf, or for contemporary work requiring a richer, aged appearance.

Best for: mixed finishes, contemporary commissions, warm interiors
White Gold — Pale Silver
12k / palladium alloy

A cool, almost grey-white leaf that does not tarnish. Increasingly used in contemporary architectural projects where yellow gold would overpower the palette of the space.

Best for: contemporary architecture, modern furniture
Palladium Leaf
Pure palladium

A precious metal leaf with a cool, neutral silver-white colour. Harder and more durable than silver leaf; does not tarnish. Used in high-specification architectural and conservation work.

Best for: exterior elements, museum conservation, long-term durability
Copper Leaf
Pure copper

Used as a ground beneath translucent gold varnishes to achieve the deep, rich glow of vernis Martin or japanned finishes. Also used for contemporary decorative effects.

Best for: lacquer finishes, decorative grounds, contemporary work

Aftercare

Caring for Gilded Surfaces

Properly applied gold leaf requires almost no maintenance. It does not oxidise, corrode, or fade under normal indoor conditions. What it requires is protection from abrasion and handling. Every AURUM commission is accompanied by a full written care guide specific to the object, the gilding method used, and the environment in which it will live.

Gold surfaces should never be cleaned with abrasive cloths. A soft, dry sable brush to remove dust is all that regular maintenance requires. If the surface accumulates atmospheric grime over decades, it should be returned to the atelier for professional cleaning rather than treated at home.

We are available for maintenance consultations on any object we have gilded, regardless of how much time has passed since the original commission. Properly applied water gilding over traditional gesso has a service life measured in centuries, not decades.

Care Guidelines
  • —Dust with a soft, dry sable or badger brush only — never use cloths or dusters
  • —Never use water, solvents, or commercial cleaning products on or near gilded surfaces
  • —Avoid handling gilded surfaces with bare hands — oils from skin damage leaf over time
  • —Maintain 45–60% relative humidity — extremes cause gesso movement and leaf loss
  • —Avoid prolonged direct sunlight — not because gold fades, but because heat stress affects the gesso beneath
  • —Schedule professional inspection every 25–40 years for re-waxing and condition assessment
Questions

Frequently Asked

How long does a commission typically take?

A single picture frame takes four to six weeks from receipt to completion — most of this is curing time, not active work. A suite of furniture may take three to four months. Architectural commissions are individually scheduled. We are transparent about timelines from the outset and do not rush work.

What is the difference between real and imitation gold leaf?

Imitation gold leaf is copper-zinc alloy. It looks like gold initially but oxidises to a brassy, dull colour within months without heavy protective coating — and even with coating, its colour shifts noticeably within years. AURUM uses only genuine precious-metal leaf. The additional cost is modest relative to the labour involved in any quality commission, and the result lasts centuries.

Can damaged gilded surfaces be repaired?

Yes. This is one of the great strengths of traditional gilding over gesso — it is entirely repairable and, in the case of water gilding over traditional bole, fully reversible. Areas of loss can be consolidated and re-gilded to match the surrounding surface almost invisibly when the work is done by an experienced gilder with the right patination technique.

Do you work with objects of any size?

Yes — from objects smaller than ten centimetres to architectural commissions exceeding a thousand square metres. Scale changes logistics and scaffolding requirements, but not the craft itself. All work, at any scale, is executed by hand.

Do you provide condition reports for insurance purposes?

Yes. All conservation commissions are accompanied by a full photographic condition report documenting the object before, during, and after treatment. For institutional clients requiring reports in specific formats for insurance or collection management systems, we accommodate these requirements as standard.
