Our Story

The Art
Behind the Box

Three generations of craftsmanship in the heart of Kyoto.

1988 Founded in Kyoto
Origin

Where Beauty
Meets Nourishment

"Every box I make is a letter. The ingredients are the words. The arrangement is the handwriting."
— Kenji Murakami, founder

Bento Bijou was founded in 1988 by Kenji Murakami, a former kaiseki chef who believed that great food deserved great containers — and that the ritual of opening a bento should be as meaningful as the meal itself.

Working from a small studio in Nishiki, Kyoto's ancient food market, Kenji began crafting bento boxes for a handful of local families who commissioned seasonal celebrations. Word spread quietly, as it does in Kyoto. By 1995, Bento Bijou was catering to three of Japan's largest corporations for their year-end ceremonies.

Today, the studio is led by Kenji's daughter, Hana Murakami, who trained under Osaka's finest kaiseki masters before returning home. The studio now employs six full-time artisans, produces 200 bespoke boxes each month, and hosts intimate bento-making workshops for guests from around the world.

Our Values

The Philosophy
of the Box

Japanese bento is governed by ancient principles — balance of color, flavor, texture, and meaning. At Bento Bijou, these principles are our design language.

間

Ma — Negative Space

What is left empty in a box is as intentional as what is placed. We never fill a box completely — the pause between flavors is part of the meal.

旬

Shun — Seasonality

Each ingredient is chosen only when it is at its precise peak. We refuse to source out of season. A bento tells the time of year it was made.

色

Iro — Color Harmony

A proper bento contains five colors. This is not decoration — it is nutritional balance codified in aesthetic form, a principle unchanged for 500 years.

形

Katachi — Form

Ingredients are cut to express their natural geometry. A carrot becomes a maple leaf. A radish becomes a chrysanthemum. Form honors nature's design.

Our Journey

Thirty-Six Years
of Craftsmanship

1988
Studio Founded

Kenji Murakami opens a small bento studio on Nishiki Koji. First season serves twelve local families.

1995
Corporate Gifting Begins

Three of Japan's largest corporations commission year-end bento collections. Capacity doubles to 50 boxes per month.

2002
Wajima Partnership

Bento Bijou partners with Wajima lacquerware artisans in Ishikawa to produce exclusive hand-lacquered boxes.

2010
Workshop Program Launched

Hana Murakami returns from Osaka training and launches the studio's first bento-making workshops for guests.

2024
200 Boxes Per Month

Studio expands to six artisans. Monthly capacity reaches 200 custom bento boxes. Workshop program serves guests from 30 countries.
