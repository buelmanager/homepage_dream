About Mouvement

Sixty Years of
Forming Dancers

Mouvement was built on the belief that the most complete dancer is one who can both interpret and create — who inhabits the body of tradition and the mind of the avant-garde simultaneously.

Our Story

From a Single Studio
to a National Reference

Marguerite Colombe opened Mouvement in 1962 in a converted warehouse in the Croix-Rousse district. She had spent fifteen years as a soloist with the Paris Opera Ballet. She left because she believed the academies were producing beautiful technicians without souls.

Her approach was radical for the time: ballet training and contemporary work in the same curriculum, the same students, the same week. The idea that one enriched the other was not widely accepted. The results made the argument for her.

Today Mouvement is recognised as one of the foremost dance conservatories in France. Alumni perform with the world's leading companies. The Mouvement Platform — our annual graduate showcase — draws company directors from across Europe.

What We Believe

Three Principles
of Formation

01

The Complete Dancer

Technique is necessary but insufficient. We train musicians, athletes, and artists simultaneously. The body must be disciplined; the mind must be curious; the imagination must be wild.

02

Tradition and Rupture

You cannot break from tradition you have not mastered. We insist on deep classical training not to preserve the past but to give students something solid to push against.

03

Stage Readiness

Education without professional preparation is incomplete. Everything at Mouvement — the Platform, the resident company, the visiting directors — is designed to prepare students for the real conditions of professional dance.
