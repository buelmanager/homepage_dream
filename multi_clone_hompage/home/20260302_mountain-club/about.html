Our Foundation

Born from
Post-War Ambition

The Alpine Meridian Club was established in the autumn of 1948 by a group of twelve climbers who had spent the war years dreaming of mountains. Returning to peacetime Europe, they were determined to explore the great alpine ranges not with the desperation of escape, but with the patience and preparation that serious mountaineering demands.

Their founding charter established principles that remain in force today: every member must be trained before they climb at altitude; no one goes into the mountains alone; and the club exists to make the summit accessible to those willing to learn. These principles have sustained an outstanding safety record across seven decades.

We have led expeditions to 24 countries, completed 340 documented summits, and trained thousands of climbers across all levels. Three of our members have been awarded national mountaineering distinctions.

Notable Expeditions

Peaks We Have Climbed

Himalaya, Nepal
Mera Peak
6,476m

First ascended by a club team in 1985. Now a regular expedition destination for members with alpine experience above 4,000m.

Andes, Argentina
Aconcagua
6,961m

The highest peak in the Western hemisphere. Club teams have completed the Normal Route nine times since the first attempt in 1972.

Alps, Switzerland/Italy
Monte Rosa Traverse
4,634m

Our annual flagship alpine expedition. A demanding multi-day traverse of the Monte Rosa massif run every summer since 1958.
