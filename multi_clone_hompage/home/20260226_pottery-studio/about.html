The Beginning

Born from
Obsession

Terra Forma was not founded. It was grown — slowly, deliberately, from a single kick-wheel in a rented warehouse on the edge of Shoreditch in 1985. Eleanor and James Whitmore had just returned from eighteen months studying under the great Shoji Hamada tradition in Mashiko, Japan, carrying with them a philosophy that would define everything to come.

"Clay does not lie. It holds the memory of every hand that has touched it, every hour of patience or impatience, every decision made at the wheel. A pot knows its maker."

What began as a private studio and a modest gallery of twenty pieces became, over four decades, one of the world's most respected names in luxury handmade ceramics. Our client list now spans forty countries. Our waiting list for bespoke commissions extends to eighteen months. But the clay is still sourced from the same three quarries, and the anagama kiln still burns for seventy-two hours.

Nothing essential has changed. Only our understanding has deepened.

Master Potter

Eleanor Whitmore

Co-founder & Creative Director

Eleanor Whitmore studied fine arts at the Slade School before spending three formative years in Mashiko, Japan, absorbing the mingei philosophy of everyday beauty and the wabi-sabi tradition of imperfect perfection. Returning to London, she brought both the technical rigour of Japanese ceramic discipline and a distinctly English understanding of restraint and quiet luxury.

Her work has been exhibited at the Victoria & Albert Museum, the Musée des Arts Décoratifs in Paris, and the Museum of Arts and Design in New York. In 2018, she was awarded the prestigious Craft Council Medal — the highest recognition for sustained excellence in British craft.

Today Eleanor divides her time between her personal commission work, guiding the studio's creative direction, and teaching the Private Masterclass programme — the only workshop she leads personally.

What We Believe

Four Pillars of
Practice

01 — Clay

Clay

Clay is not simply a material. It is a living archive of geological time, mineral pressure, and glacial patience. We approach it with the reverence it deserves — selecting each batch by hand, testing it through firing cycles before a single piece is thrown.

02 — Fire

Fire

Fire is the final maker. Our anagama kiln creates conditions no electric oven can replicate — the natural ash deposits, the flame paths, the unpredictable magic of 1300°C over 72 hours. Every piece emerges as a collaboration between hand, clay, and flame.

03 — Form

Form

Form must earn its existence. We do not add decoration that the clay has not earned. Every curve, every lip, every foot-ring is considered as a consequence of function and necessity. The most beautiful form is the one that could be no other way.

04 — Time

Time

We refuse urgency. A commission may take six months from conception to delivery. A single vessel may be thrown and rejected fifty times before the clay speaks the right word. Time is not a cost — it is the primary ingredient in everything we make.

Recognition

A Legacy of
Excellence

1992

British Crafts Council — Emerging Maker Award

Recognising exceptional promise in the field of British ceramics, awarded to Eleanor Whitmore for her Wabi Series.

1998

Victoria & Albert Museum — Solo Exhibition

"Earth Memory" — a forty-piece retrospective spanning thirteen years of studio production. Attended by over 24,000 visitors.

2004

International Ceramics Festival — Gold Award, Aberystwyth

The studio's Anagama Series won the top prize at the world's most prestigious ceramics festival, judged by a panel of nine master potters.

2011

Royal Warrant — Supplier to the Royal Household

Terra Forma was granted a Royal Warrant for the supply of luxury ceramics to the Royal Household — an honour held by fewer than 800 companies worldwide.

2018

Craft Council Medal — Sustained Excellence in British Craft

The highest individual recognition in British craft, awarded to Eleanor Whitmore for four decades of transformative contribution to the ceramics tradition.

2023

UNESCO — Intangible Cultural Heritage Recognition

Terra Forma's traditional anagama firing methodology was recognised by UNESCO as a significant example of living intangible cultural heritage.
